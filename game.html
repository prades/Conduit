ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARDS
Hazards appear on floor tiles in zones beyond home. Watch your step.
Acid Pool — Glowing green puddles. Deals 3 dmg every 0.75s to any actor standing on it. Player takes 4 dmg. Covers 2–3 tiles.
Live Cable — Frayed wires stretching from wall cracks to the floor. Arc cycles: ON for ~0.7s then OFF for 1.5–3s. While live, deals 5 dmg every 0.25s to nearby actors, 6 dmg to player. Watch the sparks.
Element Triangles — two rock-paper-scissors loops. Strong = 1.3× dmg, Weak = 0.7× dmg. Ice vs Core = 0× (completely blocked).
⬡ VOLATILE Fire > Flux > Toxic > Fire
⬡ ARCANE Electric > Core > Ice > Electric

Each follower rolls one perk, one combat trait, and one natural trait at recruitment. These are permanent and cannot be changed.
PERKS (passive stat modifiers)
Iron Will
All incoming damage is reduced by 25% (multiplied by 0.75 before applying). Best on frontline Brawlers and Campers who take constant hits.
Siphon
Heals for 10% of every point of damage dealt. Self-sustaining on high-damage followers. Pairs extremely well with Berserker combat trait — dealing more damage heals more.
COMBAT TRAITS (active behavior)
Berserker
When the follower's HP drops below 30% of max, all damage output multiplied by 1.5×. Dangerous but volatile — low-HP followers die fast without support. Best with Siphon perk or Aggressive personality.
Opportunistic
Automatically re-targets the lowest-health enemy rather than the nearest. Excellent for finishing off wounded enemies and snowballing kills. Pairs well with Snipers who have the range to pick targets.
NATURAL TRAITS (idle behavior)
Empathetic
Prefers staying near other allied followers when idle. Creates natural clustering — good for coordinated pushes and mutual defense. Empathetic followers are easier to keep together.
Lone Wolf
When idle (no combat orders), wanders up to 4 tiles away from the player on its own. Can patrol or scout but may stray into danger. Harder to keep in formation.

SPAWN ESCALATION
Rank upgrades cascade from the front zone backward, one zone per night threshold.
Night 6+: front zones become Beetle. Night 11+: Scorpion. Night 16+: Spider.
Each night beyond the threshold converts one more zone to the new rank.
Enemies to kill: 3 + (Night−1) × 2 per night (Night 1: 3 kills, Night 5: 11 kills, Night 10: 21 kills).

PYLON MODES
Attack Mode: A pylon with an assigned element acts as a defensive tower. Automatically fires at enemies within its detection range (~2.5 tiles). Each element fires its own attack type.
Support & Disruption (wave mode): A SUPPORT pylon (electric, core) works on your side; a DISRUPTION pylon (fire, ice, flux, toxic) on the enemy. Each rests on STANDBY — dim, no effect, no power drawn — until a unit it works on comes within 3 tiles, then blinks awake and runs its network’s tier effect, going back to sleep 1.5s after the last one leaves. Place 2 adjacent pylons of the same element — they pair up automatically and project a zone-effect corridor between them. The zone type matches the element.
Element combos: link a support or disruption pylon to an awake one of a different element within 3 tiles and the strip between them runs a combo — a braided cable with its name on it. Fifteen pairs, fifteen combos: STEAM (fire + ice) blinds predators so they cannot attack, BLACK ICE (ice + flux) drags and freezes, OVERDRIVE (electric + core) hastes, shields and charges ultimates, ANTIDOTE (core + toxic) heals… Each pylon joins at most 2 combos, they grow with both networks’ tiers, and the first time you find one it is kept in the codex under COMBOS.
Tip: CORE pylons forming an enclosed triangle create a protected zone. Flux solo pylons emit an inward-pulling glow ring around them.

⚡ POWER GRID — NESTS ARE BATTERIES
A nest you have taken is a battery with a life level, and a pylon’s abilities run off it. The chain is nest → generator → pylon: a pylon spends out of the nest linked to the generator nearest it, and the level is drawn on the nest itself so you can watch it go down. Power runs down the chain: a pylon with no generator of its own in reach takes power from a lit pylon within link range, so a row of pylons fed at one end is fed all along.
Capacity200 × (1 + its zone), so zone 1 holds 400 and zone 3 holds 800 (each zone of depth adds 1× the base). Deeper nests are bigger batteries — pushing forward is still what grows the grid.
HomeThe home portal — the nest labelled CRYSTAL — has its own reserve of 300. It is what a new game runs on before you have taken anything. It feeds only the generators standing near it, within 6 tiles: it is the base’s own supply, not a cable across the map. A generator out in a zone gets nothing from it and has to be wired to a nest of its own. A generator or connector built near it links to it by itself — there is nothing to connect by hand.
Every nestEvery nest you have neutralised is a battery you can draw from. Every generator and connector links itself automatically to the closest one within 6 tiles (the home reserve within its own reach) — the nest turns blue and a cable runs to it. If that nest stops being in reach or yours, it relinks to the next one. A nest still spawning is not a source until its zone is cleared.
GaugeOver every nest that holds power — the CRYSTAL and each zone you have taken — a percentage sits above a bar that empties as it is drawn down, with quarter ticks. It turns red and blinks under 20% and reads EMPTY at zero.
OverchargeHold a nest you control → top of the ring: ⚡ OVERCHARGE. Every nest in its grid gives up 40% of what it holds, and for 8s every pylon on that grid surges: turrets fire 3× as often on rounds that cost nothing, support and disruption pylons work one network tier higher, wake up and draw nothing. Needs the grid at least 50% full; it recharges for 60s (a thin line under the gauge fills back up). Not in build mode — there the top of the ring is BUILD.
RegenEvery live battery creeps back up by 0.9 a second. Finite means a reserve you can empty and have to nurse — not one you can flatten for good.
The two abilities spend it in completely different shapes:
AttackPays 1 per shot. A turret with nothing in range costs you nothing at all — that is what makes it the cheap one. The energy charges the round. A turret with no generator in reach, or on an empty nest, still fires — plain rounds at 0.7× damage — so it never stands silent.
WavePays 1.8 a second while it is awake — and nothing at all on standby, with nobody near it. Awake, it is the expensive one. The only two ways to stop paying are to switch it back to attack mode or to cut it off from the generator — or let the battery run dry: a wave pylon then shuts off (WAVE OFFLINE) and stays off until the nest has refilled to 0.25 of its capacity.
NetworksWave pylons on the same nest are one network: the first costs the full 1.8 a second and each extra only 0.1 of that, so a six-pylon tier III zone costs about 1.5× a pair. When the nest cannot pay, pylons shut off one at a time, 180 frames apart, so the zone steps down through its tiers instead of dying at once.
When a battery runs flat everything drawing on it goes dark: the pylon keeps its element and its setting, loses its range ring and turns grey, and comes back by itself as the nest creeps up again. A pylon with no generator in reach is dark whatever the batteries hold — nothing is carrying power to it. The ⚡ line on the NETWORK panel totals what is left across the batteries you are drawing on.
Read the wiring. Every cable runs along the floor like a pipe on the tile grid — straight runs joined by right-angle elbows, under the things standing on it — and the charges run along it in the direction the power goes — nest → generator → pylon. How bright the line is and how many charges are on it follow the actual draw, so you can look at a base and see what is costing you:
No wireNothing is reaching that pylon — no generator in range. It is dark and it will stay dark.
Dim dashesConnected, but drawing nothing. An idle turret with nothing in range looks like this, and it is costing you nothing.
Lit, pulsingPower is moving. A turret surges on each round and fades between them; an awake wave pylon stays full, because its draw never stops while it works, and goes quiet on standby.
Free either way: a plain pylon and a generator both spend nothing. The generator is the thing carrying the power, not a thing using it.
PREDATORS & YOUR PYLONS
Predators leave your pylons alone — an alarm or nightfall does not send them after one, and they no longer wander off to convert them. A predator only turns on a pylon that is switched on and hurting it: a turret that is shooting it, or a wave zone that has held it long enough. An idle or unpowered pylon is safe.
Electric pylons make your side faster. Followers and clones within 2.5 tiles of any lit electric pylon — turret or wave — or on the link between two, move 1.35× / 1.6× / 1.9× as fast at network tier I / II / III (2 / 4 / 6 linked electric pylons), with yellow speed streaks behind them while it lasts — the lane to send them back to the fight or out for shards. An enemy’s slow still wins over it.
A few predators hunt. About 1 in 4 is a hunter, chosen when it spawns: left alone and with no alarm up, it walks to your nearest pylon and works it over to its side, which is how nests start. The rest ignore pylons. The crystal heals slowly while no alarm is up (0.6 a second), so one bad night is not permanent.
Scouts leap to escape, not to attack. A scout’s LEAP only fires when it is below 40% health with a foe close by, it jumps away from that foe, and landing hurts nobody. A healthy scout never leaps.
Every new play starts with 100 shards to set things up with — a pylon, a generator, a connector — before you have earned any. A restart grants them again; the tutorial tops you up to that much if you are short.
NEST GRIDS & THE NEST SWITCH
Nests share power. When a generator or connector linked to one nest stands within reach of a relay linked to another nest (a connector’s 14 tiles, or a plain link between two generators), the two nests become one grid, shown by a gold cable on the floor between the relays. A pylon still draws on its own nest first; when that nest is empty or switched off, the fullest nest in the grid pays instead. So you can carry the power of a nest you took earlier into the network of the next one.
Long-press a nest you hold → NEST OFF. Nothing draws on its battery while it is off — it keeps charging, and its gauge reads OFF — HELD — so you can save it for when the predators come. NEST ON puts it back.
GENERATOR SWITCH & THE PYLON TURRET
Long-press a generator → left button: TURN OFF / TURN ON. Off, it feeds nothing and mends nothing, so every pylon it was powering goes dark until you turn it back on (a connector in reach still carries them). The state is written over it, and a dark pylon’s info panel says GENERATOR IS OFF.
A pylon in turret mode wears a self-aiming gun. It fires every 0.75s, the moment an enemy steps into range, and each round is a glowing bolt that homes onto its target and does its damage when it lands — it cannot miss. Charged rounds (a generator feeding it) are bigger and hit in full; plain rounds hit at 0.7×. Each hit is 2.5× its base attack power, +35% per network tier of its element, plus 5% of the target’s max HP so it still counts against the big ones. It turns to the nearest enemy within 1.6× its firing range and flashes when a shot goes out. With no target it sweeps slowly.
🔌 CONNECTOR PYLON — POWER FOR THE FAR ZONES
A neutral pylon you pick from the same menu as the generator, and built under the same rule: within 6 tiles of a nest, and it links to that nest by itself. Where a generator only feeds the pylons beside it, a connector feeds every pylon within 14 tiles — run one out to the later zones and the whole cluster there draws off the nest it is wired to. A generator beside a pylon still comes first; a connector is what reaches a pylon no generator does.
CircuitLong-press the connector → left button reads OPEN CIRCUIT or CLOSE CIRCUIT. Open, it feeds nothing, and every pylon hanging off it goes dark together; closed, they all light at once. The state is written over it; its 14-tile reach is drawn as a ring on the floor only while you have it selected (its ring or INFO open), so it does not cover the map. Use it to bring a whole group online for the push on a new nest, and to cut them off while you rest.
Like a generator it spends nothing itself — the pylons it feeds pay out of the nest, shot by shot or frame by frame, exactly as before.
GENERATOR — HEALING AURA
Every pylon linked to a generator mends whoever is standing near it — your followers, your clones and you. Enemies get nothing.
Both the rate and the reach are multiplied by that pylon's NETWORK TIER, so a lone linked pylon is a trickle and a tier III network is a field hospital. Tier 0 means "not networked at all" and still mends at the tier I floor — a linked pylon is never worth nothing.
The faint ring on the floor around a linked pylon is its actual reach, drawn from the same numbers it heals by, and it visibly widens as the tier climbs. Lose the generator or the pylon and the aura goes with it.

What they areEnemy spawn points scattered through zones. Continuous threat.
Take itYou cannot order followers to destroy a nest, and there is no button for it. A zone's nest goes out when you clear that zone's wave — which you start by hacking it.

ZONE OVERVIEW
The world is divided into zones (15 tiles wide each). Zone 0 is your Home base. Zones 1+ are hostile forward zones with escalating enemies. Control more zones to unlock powerful Armament purchases.
ZONE OBJECTS
◈ HOME PORTAL — zone 0
Zone 0's back wall holds a green portal, not a hive. Tap it to open the Crystal — it is the second way in, alongside tapping the Crystal itself.
It was always inert: the spawn loop starts at zone 1 so it produced nothing, and the hack skips zone 0 so it raised no alarm. It simply looked exactly like the six hives trying to kill you, on the one tile you are safe on. It cannot be attacked, linked or destroyed, and it is no longer counted as a spawn mouth — it used to answer that it was one, which meant anything counting zones from zero would have poured predators out of your own doorway.
◉ WALL NEST
Hack it — stand in the green patch of floor in front of a nest for 3 seconds and you raise that zone's alarm deliberately, on your terms. There is no button and no hold: it is proximity, so the floor is lit to say where. The wall reads HACKING while the bar fills.
A tile is only lit if it genuinely works. A tile beside an un-hacked wall panel is left dark, because the panel takes the siphon first and the nest would never count — hack the panel, and the tile lights up. The home portal, a dead nest and any nest during an alarm show no zone at all.
A vortex in the back wall of each forward zone, standing in the wall plane rather than lying flat. It is the zone's only mouth — there used to be a second one in the floor, and there is no hole in the ground any more. 200 HP; it dims and shrinks as you hurt it, and collapses to a dark still swirl when destroyed.
Hack, don’t destroy — the only thing you do to a live nest is hack it, by standing in the lit patch of floor in front of it. Clearing the wave that raises puts the nest out and stops spawns from that zone. Fire meteor still deals 2× damage to nests.
◈ THE THREE STATES OF A NEST
A nest's colour is the only thing that says whose the zone is.
HostileOrange, turning, lit. Alive and spawning. This is every forward zone when you first reach it.
NeutralGrey, still and silent — the zone is yours. It reads ◇ NEUTRAL. It produces nothing, because its nest was the zone's only mouth.
ControlledBlue, turning again, with a blue cable running along the floor to one of your generators. It reads ◈ CONTROLLED. Blue and not green on purpose: green is home, and a zone you hold is not home.
Clearing a zone's wave neutralises it. The moment the ZONE n CLEARED message appears, that zone's nest goes out and stays out — through every wave that follows, and through a refresh. You do not have to go back and break it by hand.
Take control — build a generator or connector within 6 tiles of a neutral nest. It links to the nest automatically: the nest turns blue and a cable runs along the floor to it. There is no CONNECT button any more.
▦ WALL PANEL
Appear on back walls in forward zones (~18% chance per wall tile). Interact to activate.
Reward Panel (92%) — Awards 10–30 shards on activation, plus ammo. Each panel pays half as much the next time round, so a zone is worth farming once, not forever.
Decoy Panel (8%) — Triggers an alarm that makes nearby enemies immediately hostile, and raises the wave number. It used to be 40%, which meant five or six of them waiting in the first three zones and a new player escalating themselves several waves just by farming shards. After any alarm the panels reshuffle to exactly one decoy in the whole map, so the opening now matches the rest of the game. Three alarm types:
• Proximity — Enemies within 6 tiles become hostile
• Zone — All enemies in the same zone become hostile
• Facility — All enemies on the map become hostile
In a zone you have taken the panels are neutralised with the nest, and they turn blue to say so. There is nobody left in there to raise, so no panel can trip the alarm — not even the decoy. They still pay, at 40% of the usual shards, so a cleared zone is still worth walking back through without being as good as one you have not fought for. The one decoy is only ever placed in a zone that can still raise it.
◈ WHO CAN BE ATTACKED
Predators never attack you. You are a saboteur moving through the tunnel, not a body on the line — the squad does the fighting. Hazards still hurt: acid pools, vent blasts and ground zones are not predators, and dodging them is on you.
Recruits are never attacked while they walk to the Crystal — not by predators, not by your clones, not by your followers' area attacks, and you cannot order an attack on one. A recruit is on the enemy's team until it arrives, which is why your own side used to cut them down on the way in.
Nor hurt by anything else. A recruit on its way in now takes no damage at all — hazards included. The predators' specials were never the problem: all seven land in full on a follower standing beside a recruit and do not scratch the recruit. What was killing them was something that never picks a target — the slow health decay that bleeds anything on the enemy's team. A recruit could arrive dead without a single predator having chosen to hurt it.
◈ PREDATOR PROGRESSION — EACH ZONE TEACHES ONE THING
Zone 1 — ANTS: just attackers. They walk up and bite. No special, no leap, no hauling, no pylon hunting — this is where you learn the bugs are hostile.
Zone 2 — BEETLES: the haulers. Three times an ant’s health and less bite. A beetle carries charged mass from the floor to its wall nest, and every 20 paid in hatches a predator of the lesser tier (an ant). Beetles are the only predators that haul.
Zone 3 — NYMPH swarms. Most of what comes out is nymphs, fast and weak, and they arrive in packs of 3.
Zone 4 — the GRUB. A pale larva with a huge pool of health and no bite. It eats — charged mass on the floor, pylon wrecks and pylons the enemy took from you, and the bodies of predators that die in its zone — and its bar shows how close it is to evolving. At 120 it becomes the BROOD TYRANT, a giant boss that guards its zone: it never leaves it, predators near it hit 1.3× harder, and it hatches nymphs every 15 seconds. Kill the grub before it evolves and there is no tyrant; while the zone’s nest stands, a new grub hatches a minute after the last one is gone.
◈ HUNTERS AND THE WALL NESTS
About 1 in 4 predators is a hunter. Left alone, it walks to a pylon of yours and chews it over to its side in about 25 seconds. Interrupt it and the pylon recovers about six times faster than it was taken. A taken pylon is lost until you reclaim it — nothing grows on the floor around it: there are no floor nests or cocoons any more.
Beetles feed their wall nest. An idle beetle picks up a lump of charged mass lying on the floor (charged or already neutralised), carries it to its zone’s nest on the wall as a glowing number over its head, and pays it in. Every 20 mass paid in hatches another predator there — an ant, the tier below. The wall nest shows how much it holds toward the next hatch.
You are racing them for the same lumps: mass left lying after a fight is shards for you or a new predator for them. Kill a carrier and the lump drops back on the floor.
Predators settle back into work at their own pace after a fight, so they do not all resume on the same frame when an alarm ends.
◈ TAKING A PYLON BACK
RECLAIM is the only way. Long-press a red pylon and pick it: a crew of up to 4 followers walks over and rebuilds it. The crew is drawn from your selected element first and then from whoever else is free — a squad whose elements no longer match the element tab is the normal state once the Crystal's modulation starts handing out something else, and it used to mean RECLAIM quietly did nothing.
A follower holding a POSITION order can be sent; one part-way through a real task is left to finish it. If the crew is killed the reclaim stops where it stood and you can simply order it again — an abandoned attempt does not lock the pylon.
You cannot UPGRADE an enemy pylon. It is not yours to upgrade — the radial says NOT YOURS and the option does nothing. Upgrading used to set the element and the turret without ever changing whose the pylon was, which handed it back for free and skipped the reclaim that is meant to be the price.
◈ SIGNAL TOWER
Spawns in zones 4 and beyond (zone center, y=1). While uncaptured, grants the enemy enemy territory — increasing enemy push strength in that zone.
Capturing a Signal Tower converts it to player territory and removes the enemy spawn advantage. Counts toward zone control.
◧ MEMORY BANK
A hacked nest node — found in some zones. Capturing it marks the nest zone as player-controlled, enabling you to use it as a forward spawn point for the next wave.
TERRITORY CONTROL

RECRUITMENT
When an enemy reaches the Crystal and doesn't immediately attack, it converts into a follower. The conversion assigns:
Random personality (Aggressive / Cautious / Cunning / Stoic / Wild)
Random element from the unlocked element pool
Auto-assigned role (Brawler / Sniper / Camper / Hybrid) based on stats
Random combat trait (Berserker or Opportunistic)
Random natural trait (Empathetic or Lone Wolf)
Random perk (Iron Will or Siphon)
A Crystal Modulator (dropped by bosses) lets you set an active element pair — all newly recruited followers draw from that element pool instead of random.
PROGRESSION — A CLEARED WAVE EARNS, THE CRYSTAL ACTIVATES
You start with five of the six: ELECTRIC, CORE, TOXIC, FLUX and FIRE. Every worker job except the ice block is therefore open from the first round.
There is no shop. Clear a wave, earn an element — one every wave, in this order. An earned element does nothing until you carry it back to the Crystal and activate it on the MODULATION tab.
ICE
wave 1
1 element to earn, so the ladder is spent after 1 wave — ICE is the last one left, and after that clearing a wave earns nothing. Clearing another wave while one is still pending stacks a second — the reward is for the wave, so putting off the trip back does not forfeit it. The Crystal button flashes ELEMENT READY when something is waiting.
ULTIMATEThe bar under your health is your own, and it is siphoned from followers near you — anything within 6 tiles sends a wisp of static across every couple of seconds, and the charge lands when the wisp arrives. So the rate depends on how many are with you: ten fill it in about 30 seconds, three take a few minutes, and none in reach means it does not fill at all — the bar reads NO SQUAD IN RANGE when that happens.
Brawlers close to 2 tiles and snipers hold at 4, so they are drawn from as a matter of course. A camper anchored to a far-off pylon and a worker away on a chore are not — walk over to them, or keep fighters at your side, if you want the bar moving.
Tap the bar when it is full to spend the whole thing on an ARMY SURGE: every follower and clone healed, WILL refilled, its own ultimate charged, and 1.6× damage for 10 seconds. Nothing is siphoned while a surge is running, so a surge never pays for the next one.
The ⌁ switch at the right end of the bar turns the siphon off. Wisps already in the air still land. There is no cost to leaving it on — the switch is there so you can quiet the effect down.
Kills are still counted — the Crystal shows your lifetime total — but they no longer earn anything.
Activating a new element makes your modulation stale, and both the Crystal button and the modulation chip flash RE-MODULATE.
MODULATIONWhat your followers are made of. It lives in one place: the Crystal's MODULATION tab. The crystal on that screen pulses through the colours actually in the mix, and the row of swatches under it is the control — tap one to add that element or drop it; at least one has to stay on. Followers take their element at the Crystal — when a recruit reaches it, and again every time one respawns — so re-modulating gradually re-colours the whole squad as they cycle.
An untouched mix means every element you have, so an element you bring online is in the mix the moment it is activated and nothing nags you about it. The Crystal only asks you to RE-MODULATE when a new element lands outside a mix you have narrowed by hand, and opening the tab puts that prompt down.
CLONE SUMMONING
A clone is a better version of what you killed. It fights with 3× the health and 3× the power of its species — the power multiplier was always meant to be there, but only the summon applied it, so a clone that died once (or merely survived a wave) came back an ordinary predator for the rest of the game. All three now build the same thing.
They come back. A killed clone respawns at the Crystal after 30 seconds — ten times a follower's wait, because it is worth ten times as much. The Crystal shows a green countdown for every clone on its way, soonest first, so you know whether to hold the line or fall back.
Telling them apart: an allied predator carries a green health bar with a green outline. On the ordinary bar the colour means health, so a wounded clone looked exactly like the enemy beside it; on the ally bar the colour means whose it is and the length still means health.
Summon them from the CLONE BAY, opened by the turning DNA button at the top right of the HUD — it is the only way in. A badge on it counts the clones you could summon right now. Once out, clones appear in the CLONES tab of the bottom-left panel; command them like followers via the gesture system.
