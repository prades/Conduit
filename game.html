▣ GAME INDEX
Conduit in one line: take the zones of a living circuit board one by one, build and power a network of pylons to hold them, and keep the Crystal alive through every night.
HOW A RUN GOES
1
Gather a squad
Walk up to a wandering entity and it joins you. Followers fight for you and respawn at the Crystal.
2
Earn shards
Stand by a wall panel to hack it for shards and ammo. Kills drop shards and DNA too.
3
Build pylons
Tap BUILD to open the build window, pick a turret, wave pylon or battery, then tap a tile (tap it again to build). A pylon is 10 shards.
4
Power them
Hold the zone’s nest, then BOOT the power unit in the back wall (tap it). Its cells store the power; pylons spend it.
5
Take the next zone
Stand in the lit patch in front of its nest for 3 s. That raises its alarm: clear the wave and the zone is yours.
6
Survive the night
At night the alarm brings everything. The Crystal has 300 HP — if it falls, the run is over.
☀ DAYBuild, hack panels, recruit, take nests. Thieves come for your shards on quiet days.
☾ NIGHTThe alarm raises the zones: kill the quota to clear the wave. From night 3 every night has a siege twist.

In detail — autoplay
🤖 AUTOPLAY
The robot button on the top bar hands the game to your squad. Every second or so it follows a short list: at night everyone fights the enemies around the alarm; by day a team of 3 walks to the next nest and hacks it (followers can hack when ordered to), and when shards run low one follower drains a wall panel; always it spends shards — a generator at every nest you hold, a connector at the frontier reaching toward the next zone, and a base plan at each nest in the squad’s commonest element: two turrets first, a bank of 6 batteries (tier III), the other two turrets so the four fuse into an ULTRA TURRET, a square of four wave pylons that makes a formation (black hole, spinning firewall, ice generator or toxic tower) with a bank of 4 batteries of its element beside it when that is not the network element (both need tier II), and — only with shards to spare — 2 support pylons, then MK II on its pylons one at a time. Then that nest is finished; fires any full ultimate with an enemy close (the duo if a partner can); keeps about a quarter of the squad on the work crew for shards; and presses NEXT WAVE itself. It never moves your character — go wherever you like while the squad plays. Tap it again to take back control — its orders are cleared.
Every order is a gesture or lives in the long-press ring, and the ring changes with what you press.

BUTTONS
SQUAD Toggle SEL (selected followers) / ALL (every follower)
BUILD Opens the build window (and build mode). Tabs: TURRETS, WAVE, POWER, MODULES — each item shows its picture and price. A LEVEL row sets what one placement makes: TURRETS 1 a turret, 2 a turret already at MK II (needs a tier I network of its element there), 3 a 2×2 of four that fuses into an ULTRA turret (needs tier II); WAVE 1 a pylon, 2 MK II, 3 a 2×2 formation; POWER batteries 1 one, 2 a pod of 4, 3 a pod of 6. Anything that could not be powered is greyed out: every zone needs its own GENERATOR (built by a nest you hold) before any tech in it works — a tile in a zone without one shows NO GENERATOR IN ZONE, and a tile nothing would feed shows NO POWER HERE. Pick one and it is in hand: a ghost shows where it goes, green if it can, red with the reason if not. Touch: tap a tile, tap it again to build. Mouse: click to build. It stays in hand for the next one — tap the item again (or Esc) to put it down and walk. DEMOLISH in the title bar takes your pylons down for a refund; ▾ shrinks the window, ✕ closes it. The long-press ring still works too (BUILD / UPGRADE on the top button). While build mode is on, units cannot be selected
LEVELS Every unit has a level and stats, shown over its health bar as e.g. Lv 12 FIRE BRAWLER, Lv 8 ANT SCOUT, Lv 6 ANT CLONE. A unit's stats are its level-5 base scaled by its level (×2 at 20, ×4 at 50, ×7.3 at 100). A follower's base is the standard stats × its personality; HP, attack, defense, special attack, accuracy and will grow with level (walking speed does not). A predator's base is its species and class, plus armour by class (beetles have extra); its level comes from the wave (one every two waves) and the zone (three per zone from zone 3, six per deep zone), ±1. A clone is its species ×4, born at the frontier's level. A recruit joins at the level of the zone it was found in, three below that zone's predators (never under 5). Defense now protects everyone, weighed against the attacker's level. XP comes from kills: a predator is worth its class's yield × its level / 5 (elites 1.5×); the killer gets 60% and your units within 4 tiles share 40% (a turret's kill: half to the units near it). Workers earn it by working, at the same scale (the enemies' level in your zone / 5): neutralising a lump 30, delivering one 50, putting a pylon back online 80, mending a clone 0.12 per point of health, setting an ice block 40 (and 10 every 2 s an enemy is held against it). Total XP for a level is its cube (Lv 10 = 1,000). A unit that dies keeps its level but loses a quarter of its progress to the next. Cap: level 100 (the deep tunnel's predators go past it). Hold a unit and press INFO for its full stat sheet.
REPAIR CREW A pylon brought to nothing is a wreck, not gone. Only an ANT clone on worker duty rebuilds it — and what it puts back up is an offline frame (OFFLINE · NEEDS CORE): standing, but no rounds, no wave, no charge, no relay. A CORE follower on the work crew re-arms the frame into exactly the machine it was (RE-ARMING n%, about 3 s). Ants also patch up a damaged pylon's health, so a crew of ants and core keeps a turret alive through a fight. The ring cannot upgrade or convert an offline frame.
COLLATERAL Your equipment gets caught in the fighting. A predator's area specials — a beetle's CARAPACE SLAM charge, an ant's MANDIBLE FRENZY, a mantis's BLADE FLURRY, a moth's BLINDING DUST — damage any turret, wave pylon, battery or relay in their radius (60% of what they do to a unit, like a bash). An enemy round (an abdomen or web shot, a goo machine's spit) that passes over your equipment on its way to a follower hits the equipment and stops there. Worn to nothing, it is destroyed. Your own rounds and your clones' specials never hurt it.
MODULES Structures you build anywhere the grid reaches (the build window's MODULES tab). Each works only while powered and covers the ground around it; build as many as you like. MED BAY (◆40): followers and clones within 3 tiles heal — 6 HP a second plus 1% of their max, so a big clone fills too. FORCE WALL (◆50): a wall of energy across the whole tunnel at its column. Predators cannot cross; they stop and beat on it. Your units walk through. It mends while powered; broken, it is down for 10 s, then rises again. OVERCLOCK HUB (◆45): pylons within 3 tiles fire 50% faster and their wave links hit 50% harder — but each round costs double power. DEMOLISH gives half a module's price back. The old camp buildings are retired; any you bought were refunded.

In detail — the wheel on every hit
☯ THE ELEMENT WHEEL
Two triangles. Fire > Flux > Toxic > Fire and Electric > Core > Ice > Electric. A hit of the element that beats its target’s does 1.3×; the one it beats does 0.7×; ice does nothing to core. It runs on every hit — your followers’, your turrets’ and the predators’ — against anything that has an element: your followers always, bosses, and every predator on an Elemental Breakout night. (The goo machines have their own rule: fire ¼, electric ×2.)
In detail — follower attacks by element
FIRE Proc: 30% · Unlocked by default
Physical — Ring of Fire: 0.4× ATK dmg to all enemies within 1.5 tiles. Also hits nearby nests for 2× bonus damage.
Special — Meteor: 0.9× SP.ATK dmg in 2.0-tile radius at target. Deals 2× damage to nest pods within 3.0 tiles.
Ultimate — Meteor Burst: 2s cast. Map darkens, reticle locks the highest-HP enemy. On impact: 3.0× SP.ATK dmg in 5.0-tile radius (falls off with distance). Leaves a flame crater that deals lingering fire DoT for ~10s.
Burn proc (30%): DoT for ~2s on physical hits, ~3s on special hits. Extended burn applied by ultimates.
ELECTRIC Proc: 40% · Unlocked by default

Special — Static Field: 0.7× SP.ATK to all enemies in 3.0-tile radius. High stun proc rate.

PYLON MODES
Attack Mode: A pylon with an assigned element acts as a defensive tower. Automatically fires at enemies within 3 tiles. Each element fires its own attack type.
Support & Disruption (wave mode): A SUPPORT pylon (electric, core) works on your side; a DISRUPTION pylon (fire, ice, flux, toxic) on the enemy. A TOXIC link also mends your clones standing on it while it poisons the predators, and a hurt clone wakes it. Each rests on STANDBY — dim, no effect, no power drawn — until a unit it works on comes within 3 tiles, then blinks awake and runs its network’s tier effect, going back to sleep 1.5s after the last one leaves. Place 2 adjacent pylons of the same element — they pair up automatically and project a zone-effect corridor between them. The zone type matches the element.
Element combos: link a support or disruption pylon to an awake one of a different element within 3 tiles and the strip between them runs a combo — a braided cable with its name on it. Fifteen pairs, fifteen combos: STEAM (fire + ice) blinds predators so they cannot attack, BLACK ICE (ice + flux) drags and freezes, OVERDRIVE (electric + core) hastes, shields and charges ultimates, ANTIDOTE (core + toxic) heals… Each pylon joins at most 2 combos, they grow with both networks’ tiers, and the first time you find one it is kept in the codex under COMBOS.
Tip: CORE pylons forming an enclosed triangle create a protected zone. Flux solo pylons emit an inward-pulling glow ring around them.

In detail — MK II and status
◆ MK II — PYLON STATUS
Every pylon has a status, and its damage is multiplied by it — turret rounds, wave links, formations and Ultra turrets: plain ×1, upgraded (reclaimed and rebuilt, with its healing pulse) ×1.15, MK II ×1.3.
Out of build mode, long-press your turret or wave pylon → top: MK II 15◆. It needs its element’s tier I network where it stands (2 linked batteries close by) — otherwise the button says what it needs. MK II keeps everything an upgraded pylon has, takes 25% more health, and wears a gold ◆ with its status written over it.
In detail — turret, generator, shield cells and connector
GENERATOR SWITCH & THE PYLON TURRET
Long-press a generator → left button: TURN OFF / TURN ON. Off, it feeds nothing and mends nothing, so every pylon it was powering goes dark until you turn it back on (a connector in reach still carries them). The state is written over it, and a dark pylon’s info panel says GENERATOR IS OFF.
A pylon in turret mode wears a self-aiming gun. It fires every 0.75s, the moment an enemy steps into range, and each round is a glowing bolt that homes onto its target and does its damage when it lands — it cannot miss. Charged rounds (a generator feeding it) are bigger and hit in full; plain rounds hit at 0.7×. Each hit is 2.5× its base attack power, +35% per network tier of its element, plus 5% of the target’s max HP so it still counts against the big ones. It turns to the nearest enemy just before it comes into range (1.2× its firing range) and flashes when a shot goes out. With no target it sweeps slowly.
◎ THE GENERATOR — one in every zone
A thicker pylon with a big pale orb. It carries power from its nest to the pylons around it and mends them — and it is required: every pylon, turret, battery and module in a zone is offline (dark, silent, greyed out in the build window) until that zone has a working generator, switched on and fed by a nest. It is tough (240 health) — and the predators know it: at night about a third of them go straight for the nearest one, and pylon hunters pick it first. Lose it and the zone goes dark. Its mending reach shows as a dotted ring while you hold it or build.
⬡ SHIELD CELLS — the CORE tech
A powered CORE wave pylon (support, not a turret) drops a shield cell on the floor beside it every 15s (a MK II twice as fast; at most 2 lying there). Four of them in a square fuse into a SHIELD FORGE, which makes one every 2.5s. Walk over it — or let a follower — and it goes into your store, shown beside your shards (up to 40). Each follower or clone with no shield spends one cell for a full shield: three quarters of its max health (at least 30), a thin pale bubble and a blue bar. A shield takes 100% of every hit until it breaks; after a hit it waits 2s before a new one goes on. The FORCE WALL (the shield barrier) runs on cells too: 2 to rise again after it breaks, and one per 100 health it mends.
🔌 CONNECTOR PYLON — POWER FOR THE FAR ZONES
A neutral pylon you pick from the same menu as the generator, and built under the same rule: within 6 tiles of a nest, and it links to that nest by itself. Where a generator only feeds the pylons beside it, a connector feeds every pylon within 14 tiles — run one out to the later zones and the whole cluster there draws off the nest it is wired to. A generator beside a pylon still comes first; a connector is what reaches a pylon no generator does.
CircuitLong-press the connector → left button reads OPEN CIRCUIT or CLOSE CIRCUIT. Open, it feeds nothing, and every pylon hanging off it goes dark together; closed, they all light at once. The state is written over it; its 14-tile reach is drawn as a ring on the floor only while you have it selected (its ring or INFO open), so it does not cover the map. Use it to bring a whole group online for the push on a new nest, and to cut them off while you rest.
Like a generator it spends nothing itself — the pylons it feeds pay out of the nest, shot by shot or frame by frame, exactly as before.
In detail — Ultra turret and formations
◆ ULTRA TURRET
Build four attack turrets in a 2×2 square on a tier II network of their element (a bank of 4 batteries within 6 tiles; the tier falls a step every 6 tiles further from the bank) and they fuse on their own into an ULTRA TURRET — below tier II they stay four turrets and the square is outlined with what it needs: a Tesla array over all four tiles — a coil tower crowned with a ring, a rod at each corner, and live arcs jumping between them, more as it charges. It fires one round for all four — their damage added together and half again — from the middle of the square, reaches 2 tiles further than a turret, and each round bursts for half its damage on every enemy within a tile of the one it hits. Its element is the one most of the four carry. It is solid: your squad, the predators and you all walk round it. The four turrets go into it as it rises; lose any of the four and the rest go back to being ordinary turrets.
◉ BLACK HOLE, SPINNING FIREWALL, ICE GENERATOR, TOXIC TOWER & SHIELD FORGE
Build four flux wave pylons in a 2×2 square and a black hole opens in the floor where the four meet — a vortex spiralling down into a black pit — and the four pylons go see-through. Every enemy within 2.5 tiles is entangled: dragged in toward it, slowed and crushed every half second, tied to the pit by a tether. The drag is gentle enough that a predator walking hard can creep out of the rim. Beetles and boss predators are too heavy to drag: a black hole, a flux link or a flux follower's singularity cannot pull them, though they are still slowed and crushed if they walk through. The vortex lights up, widens and spins faster the more it holds. All four must be powered and the element’s network tier II at the square — a bank of 4 batteries close by (otherwise it says NEEDS POWER or NEEDS … TIER II); break the square and it closes. The four pylons go into it: they vanish as it rises from their tiles and stop working as pylons of their own; it carries their health as one bar, and if they are broken it falls apart.
Four fire wave pylons in a square make a SPINNING FIREWALL: three standing walls of flame fan out from a white-hot core in the middle of the square and turn. Every enemy a wall sweeps over burns (once per pass); with enemies close it spins faster, stands taller and burns hotter. It stays inside its four tiles and spares your squad. It needs all four powered too.
Four ice wave pylons in a square make an ICE GENERATOR: a cluster of ice crystals grows where they meet and frost spreads over the floor for 2.5 tiles round it. Enemies on the frost are slowed and chilled every half second, and every 4s it sends a pulse across the frost that roots them where they stand for a moment. Your squad crosses the frost freely. All four powered, as with the others.
Four toxic wave pylons in a square raise a TOXIC TOWER: a thick, boxy tower of four dark levels with lit grilles on every face, venting glowing exhaust from every level and a big plume from the roof. Its fumes haze the floor for 2.5 tiles round it: enemies in them are poisoned every half second and their armour is stripped; your clones in them are mended. It burns hotter the more enemies are near. All four powered.
Four core wave pylons in a square raise a SHIELD FORGE: a tall hexagonal crystal on a dais, filling with light from the bottom as the next shield cell forms, with cells orbiting it. It makes one every 2.5s (MK II faster) and sets it on the floor round its square, up to 4 at once — far more than its four pylons would make apart.

Hold a nest you control: 40% of its grid for 8 s of surging pylons.
⚡ POWER GRID — NEST → BATTERY → PYLON
This is how power works now. The nests you take are the source; your battery pylons store it; your pylons spend it. A generator or connector carries power from a nest to the batteries and pylons around it, and power runs down the chain: anything with no generator of its own in reach takes it from a lit pylon or battery within link range.
1 ChargeEvery battery wired to a nest charges from it by itself, 3 nest power a second, and stores twice what it takes (×2) — that is how batteries let you use more power than the nests alone could give. It only takes what a nest holds above 20%, so it banks the nest’s spare power and never its last. One battery holds 120.
2 SpendBatteries charge the pylons. A turret’s shot and a wave pylon’s draw come out of the batteries within 12 tiles first (any element) — the fullest one pays, so a bank drains evenly. A pylon beside charged batteries is powered even with no generator of its own. Only when every battery in reach is flat does the nest pay directly.
3 TierLinked batteries of one element also set that element’s network tier: 2 / 4 / 6 linked = tier I / II / III, full within 6 tiles of the bank and a step lower every 6 tiles further.
See itA battery’s four cells fill a quarter at a time and the next one blinks while it charges; a gold gauge sits over it (stored / 120 when you are near). A blue cable with charges runs into a bank while it fills, charge runs along the bank’s own cables, and a pylon fed by a battery gets a gold cable from it. The NETWORK panel’s ⚡ BATT line shows everything stored, a gold bar, and the flow — ▲ filling or ▼ draining, per second. The NESTS line above it is what the nests hold.
CapacityA nest holds 200 × (1 + its zone), so zone 1 holds 400 and zone 3 holds 800 (each zone of depth adds 1× the base), and every live nest creeps back up by 0.9 a second.
HomeThe home portal — the nest labelled CRYSTAL — has its own reserve of 300, what a new game runs on before you have taken anything. It feeds only the generators standing near it, within 6 tiles: a generator out in a zone has to be wired to a nest of its own. A generator or connector built near it links to it by itself.
Every nestEvery nest you have neutralised is a source you can draw from. Every generator and connector links itself automatically to the closest one within 6 tiles — the nest turns blue and a cable runs to it. A nest still spawning is not a source until its zone is cleared. Over every nest that holds power a percentage sits above a bar that empties; it turns red and blinks under 20% and reads EMPTY at zero.
OverchargeHold a nest you control → top of the ring: ⚡ OVERCHARGE. Every nest in its grid gives up 40% of what it holds, and for 8s every pylon on that grid surges: turrets fire 3× as often on rounds that cost nothing, support and disruption pylons work one network tier higher. Needs the grid at least 50% full; it recharges for 60s.
CostsA turret pays 1 per shot and nothing while idle; with nothing to pay it still fires plain rounds at 0.7×. An awake wave pylon pays 1.8 a second and nothing on standby; if everything in reach runs dry it shuts off (WAVE OFFLINE) until a battery or its nest is back to 0.25 full.
Read the wiring. Every cable runs along the floor like a pipe, with right-angle elbows, and its charges run the way the power goes — nest → generator → battery → pylon. No wire: nothing reaches it. Dim dashes: connected, drawing nothing. Lit and pulsing: power is moving. An Ultra turret or a formation takes one cable, at its anchor, not one to each of its four.
NEST GRIDS & THE NEST SWITCH
Nests share power. When a generator or connector linked to one nest stands within reach of a relay linked to another nest (a connector’s 14 tiles, or a plain link between two generators), the two nests become one grid, shown by a gold cable on the floor between the relays. A pylon still draws on its own nest first; when that nest is empty or switched off, the fullest nest in the grid pays instead. So you can carry the power of a nest you took earlier into the network of the next one.
In build mode, long-press a nest you hold → left: NEST OFF. Nothing draws on its battery while it is off — it keeps charging, and its gauge reads OFF — HELD — so you can save it for when the predators come. NEST ON puts it back.
➤ TELEPORT. Out of build mode, long-press any nest you hold — the home portal too — and the left button teleports you to your front nest, the one you hold closest to the enemy (it reads TELEPORT · ZONE N). The squad following you comes along; anyone on a job, holding a spot or hauling stays put. On the front nest itself it reads TELEPORT HOME.
They use the nests to travel. A follower you POSITION far away, or one that has fallen far behind you, walks to the nest you hold nearest it, teleports to the one nearest where it is going, and walks the rest — whenever that is quicker than walking the whole way.
Your followers teleport too. When any of them is fighting out past the home zone, every follower and clone standing in the home zone — fresh respawns, stragglers — teleports to the nest you hold nearest that fight and joins in from there. The ones beside you, on a job, holding a spot or hauling stay where they are.

In detail — clones
CLONE SUMMONING
A clone is a better version of what you killed. It fights with 4× the health and 4× the power of its species, and grows with the depth of your frontier the way the predators do — the power multiplier was always meant to be there, but only the summon applied it, so a clone that died once (or merely survived a wave) came back an ordinary predator for the rest of the game. All three now build the same thing.
They come back. A killed clone respawns at the Crystal after 20 seconds — several times a follower's wait, because it is worth that much more. The Crystal shows a green countdown for every clone on its way, soonest first, so you know whether to hold the line or fall back.
Telling them apart: an allied predator carries a green health bar with a green outline. On the ordinary bar the colour means health, so a wounded clone looked exactly like the enemy beside it; on the ally bar the colour means whose it is and the length still means health.
CLONE JOBS
Summon them from the CLONE BAY, opened by the turning DNA button at the top right of the HUD — it is the only way in. A badge on it counts the clones you could summon right now. Once out, clones appear in the CLONES tab of the bottom-left panel; command them like followers via the gesture system. Hold a clone's row there for TO WORK / TO LINE: it orders every clone of that species, the way holding an element row orders followers. Each row shows WORK or LINE.

In detail — how each zone escalates
◈ PREDATOR PROGRESSION — THE WAVE LADDER
The ladder. NYMPH → ANTS → BEETLES → SCORPIONS → MANTISES → MOTHS → SPIDERS → the chimeras. Every 2 waves it climbs a step, in every zone at once.
An odd wave (1, 3, 5…) is the tier it has reached, and 10% of what comes out is the next tier — and that one comes as a pair. An even wave is half and half. So wave 1 is nymphs with the odd pair of ants, wave 2 half nymphs and half ants, wave 3 ants with the odd pair of beetles.
NYMPHS are worth nothing: no charged mass (no shards), and they do not count toward the night’s kills — so a nymph-heavy wave (and a Swarm Tide night) asks for fewer kills, only the rest. But every nymph drops ant DNA, which is what the ant clones are made of.
ANTS are plain attackers — no special, no hauling, no pylon hunting. BEETLES are the haulers: a beetle carries charged mass from the floor to its wall nest, and every 20 paid in hatches a predator of the tier below.
It gets harder as you go. Zones 1 and 2 are as they were. From zone 3, every zone deeper (to zone 12) gives predators +25% health and +15% damage, adds about 1.5 kills to the night’s quota (at most 45), and sends more of them after your pylons (up to 60%).
The tunnel never ends. Past zone 12 the predators keep coming, and every zone deeper makes them tougher (×1.09 health, ×1.07 damage per zone), bigger (+5% size per zone, up to 2.5×) and richer (+10% shards and DNA per zone). A night’s kill quota stops growing past zone 18 — the predators grow instead.
◈ HUNTERS AND THE WALL NESTS
About 1 in 4 predators is a hunter. Left alone, it walks to a pylon of yours and chews it over to its side in about 25 seconds. Interrupt it and the pylon recovers about six times faster than it was taken. A taken pylon is lost until you reclaim it — nothing grows on the floor around it: there are no floor nests or cocoons any more.
Beetles feed their wall nest. An idle beetle picks up a lump of charged mass lying on the floor (charged or already neutralised), carries it to its zone’s nest on the wall as a glowing number over its head, and pays it in. Every 20 mass paid in hatches another predator there — an ant, the tier below. The wall nest shows how much it holds toward the next hatch.
You are racing them for the same lumps: mass left lying after a fight is shards for you or a new predator for them. Kill a carrier and the lump drops back on the floor.
Predators settle back into work at their own pace after a fight, so they do not all resume on the same frame when an alarm ends.
◈ TAKING A PYLON BACK
RECLAIM is the only way. Long-press a red pylon and pick it: a crew of up to 4 followers walks over and rebuilds it. The crew is drawn from your selected element first and then from whoever else is free — a squad whose elements no longer match the element tab is the normal state once the Crystal's modulation starts handing out something else, and it used to mean RECLAIM quietly did nothing.
A follower holding a POSITION order can be sent; one part-way through a real task is left to finish it. If the crew is killed the reclaim stops where it stood and you can simply order it again — an abandoned attempt does not lock the pylon.
You cannot UPGRADE an enemy pylon. It is not yours to upgrade — the radial says NOT YOURS and the option does nothing. Upgrading used to set the element and the turret without ever changing whose the pylon was, which handed it back for free and skipped the reclaim that is meant to be the price.
In detail — predators and your pylons
PREDATORS & YOUR PYLONS
Predators leave your pylons alone — an alarm or nightfall does not send them after one, and they no longer wander off to convert them. A predator only turns on a pylon that is switched on and hurting it: a turret that is shooting it, or a wave zone that has held it long enough. An idle or unpowered pylon is safe.
Electric pylons make your side faster. Followers and clones within 2.5 tiles of any lit electric pylon — turret or wave — or on the link between two, move 1.35× / 1.6× / 1.9× as fast at network tier I / II / III (2 / 4 / 6 linked electric pylons), with yellow speed streaks behind them while it lasts — the lane to send them back to the fight or out for shards. An enemy’s slow still wins over it.
A few predators hunt. About 1 in 4 is a hunter, chosen when it spawns: left alone and with no alarm up, it walks to your nearest pylon and works it over to its side, which is how nests start. The rest ignore pylons. The crystal heals slowly while no alarm is up (0.6 a second), so one bad night is not permanent.
Scouts leap to escape, not to attack. A scout’s LEAP only fires when it is below 40% health with a foe close by, it jumps away from that foe, and landing hurts nobody. A healthy scout never leaps.
Every new play starts with 100 shards to set things up with — a pylon and the wall power unit — before you have earned any. A restart grants them again.
In detail — thieves and machines
◆ BATTERY THIEVES & MACHINES
Once you hold a nest, quiet days are not safe. Every 40–60s of daytime with no alarm, each of the three enemy nests nearest your ground sends out a THIEF — as long as you have a battery for it to eat. It walks to your nearest battery and feeds on it (a dashed beam and an EATING bar) for 4s, then eats it: the battery is wrecked and its charge is gone (an ant clone rebuilds it as an offline frame and a core worker re-arms it). Every battery it eats makes it stronger — 1.5× its health (healed to full), 1.3× its bite, faster and bigger, shown as THIEF ⚡n — and it goes for the next, up to 3. Then it goes home, and its nest banks 5 toward a machine for every battery it ate. Hit it and it fights; kill it and it drops a bigger lump of charged mass for each battery it ate. A fed thief raids your nests. A thief that has eaten at least one battery goes for your nearest nest (a battery nearer than it is eaten first) and drains it for 4s — a banner warns you — then runs home carrying 4+ shards for every battery it ate. Kill it on the way and every shard comes back. A thief that has eaten nothing never touches your nests.
Shards that reach an enemy nest are banked there (◆ x/15 STOLEN on its wall). Every 15 builds a hostile machine in front of it — living goo, near-black with an oil-slick rainbow sheen — which grows up out of a puddle for 8s: first a SENTRY, a rooted mass of fused bubbles that throws out a pseudopod and spits at your squad, then a STRIDER, a see-through goo bell drifting on long ribbon tentacles that marches onto your nearest nest and drains a shard every few seconds. With both standing, more stolen shards hatch predators. Destroy a machine to salvage shards back; machines stay until you do. Being goo, they shrug off fire (a quarter damage, FIRE RESISTED) and electric tears through them (double damage, SHOCKED ×2) — bring your electric squad, turrets and Ultra turrets.

◈ HOME PORTAL — zone 0
Zone 0's back wall holds a green portal, not a hive. Tap it to open the Crystal — it is the second way in, alongside tapping the Crystal itself.
It was always inert: the spawn loop starts at zone 1 so it produced nothing, and the hack skips zone 0 so it raised no alarm. It simply looked exactly like the six hives trying to kill you, on the one tile you are safe on. It cannot be attacked, linked or destroyed, and it is no longer counted as a spawn mouth — it used to answer that it was one, which meant anything counting zones from zero would have poured predators out of your own doorway.
◉ WALL NEST
Hack it — stand in the green patch of floor in front of a nest for 3 seconds and you raise that zone's alarm deliberately, on your terms. There is no button and no hold: it is proximity, so the floor is lit to say where. The wall reads HACKING while the bar fills.
A tile is only lit if it genuinely works. A tile beside an un-hacked wall panel is left dark, because the panel takes the siphon first and the nest would never count — hack the panel, and the tile lights up. The home portal, a dead nest and any nest during an alarm show no zone at all.
A vortex in the back wall of each forward zone, standing in the wall plane rather than lying flat. It is the zone's only mouth — there used to be a second one in the floor, and there is no hole in the ground any more. 200 HP; it dims and shrinks as you hurt it, and collapses to a dark still swirl when destroyed.
Hack, don’t destroy — the only thing you do to a live nest is hack it, by standing in the lit patch of floor in front of it. Clearing the wave that raises puts the nest out and stops spawns from that zone. Fire meteor still deals 2× damage to nests.
◈ THE THREE STATES OF A NEST
A nest's colour is the only thing that says whose the zone is.
HostileOrange, turning, lit. Alive and spawning. This is every forward zone when you first reach it.
NeutralGrey, still and silent — the zone is yours. It reads ◇ NEUTRAL. It produces nothing, because its nest was the zone's only mouth.
ControlledBlue, turning again, with a blue cable running along the floor to one of your generators. It reads ◈ CONTROLLED. Blue and not green on purpose: green is home, and a zone you hold is not home.
Clearing a zone's wave neutralises it. The moment the ZONE n CLEARED message appears, that zone's nest goes out and stays out — through every wave that follows, and through a refresh. You do not have to go back and break it by hand.
Take control — build a generator or connector within 6 tiles of a neutral nest. It links to the nest automatically: the nest turns blue and a cable runs along the floor to it. There is no CONNECT button any more.
▦ WALL PANEL
Appear on back walls in forward zones (~18% chance per wall tile). Interact to activate.
Reward Panel (92%) — Awards 10–30 shards on activation, plus ammo. Each panel pays half as much the next time round, so a zone is worth farming once, not forever.
Decoy Panel (8%) — Triggers an alarm that makes nearby enemies immediately hostile, and raises the wave number. It used to be 40%, which meant five or six of them waiting in the first three zones and a new player escalating themselves several waves just by farming shards. After any alarm the panels reshuffle to exactly one decoy in the whole map, so the opening now matches the rest of the game. Three alarm types:
• Proximity — Enemies within 6 tiles become hostile
• Zone — All enemies in the same zone become hostile
• Facility — All enemies on the map become hostile
In a zone you have taken the panels are neutralised with the nest, and they turn blue to say so. There is nobody left in there to raise, so no panel can trip the alarm — not even the decoy. They still pay, at 40% of the usual shards, so a cleared zone is still worth walking back through without being as good as one you have not fought for. The one decoy is only ever placed in a zone that can still raise it.
◈ WHO CAN BE ATTACKED
You can take 3 hits. A predator's bite, sting, blast or shot that reaches you is one hit, however strong it is — and so is an acid pool, a vent blast or a ground zone. The third knocks you back to the Crystal with all three again. It is three hits in every zone, first to last. After a hit you have a moment where nothing lands, so one swarm's volley costs one hit, not three. The bar at the top is cut into thirds; a health pad charge gives one hit back.
Recruits are never attacked while they walk to the Crystal — not by predators, not by your clones, not by your followers' area attacks, and you cannot order an attack on one. A recruit is on the enemy's team until it arrives, which is why your own side used to cut them down on the way in.
Nor hurt by anything else. A recruit on its way in now takes no damage at all — hazards included. The predators' specials were never the problem: all seven land in full on a follower standing beside a recruit and do not scratch the recruit. What was killing them was something that never picks a target — the slow health decay that bleeds anything on the enemy's team. A recruit could arrive dead without a single predator having chosen to hurt it.

◧ MEMORY BANK
A hacked nest node — found in some zones. Capturing it marks the nest zone as player-controlled, enabling you to use it as a forward spawn point for the next wave.

RECRUITMENT
When an enemy reaches the Crystal and doesn't immediately attack, it converts into a follower. The conversion assigns:
Random personality (Aggressive / Cautious / Cunning / Stoic / Wild)
Random element from the unlocked element pool
Auto-assigned role (Brawler / Mage / Technician / Hybrid) based on stats
Random combat trait (Berserker or Opportunistic)
Random natural trait (Empathetic or Lone Wolf)
Random perk (Iron Will or Siphon)
A Crystal Modulator (dropped by bosses) lets you set an active element pair — all newly recruited followers draw from that element pool instead of random.
PROGRESSION — A CLEARED WAVE EARNS, THE CRYSTAL ACTIVATES
You start with one: ELECTRIC. Everything of an element — its followers, pylons, batteries, turrets and worker jobs — waits until you have it.
There is no shop. Clear a wave, earn an element — one unlock every two waves, and you pick which element. An unlock does nothing until you choose an element in the mix selector (the gold UNLOCK chip).

5 elements to earn, so the ladder is spent after 10 waves — ICE is the last, and after that clearing a wave earns nothing. Clearing another while one is still pending stacks a second — the reward is for the wave, so putting off the trip back does not forfeit it. The Crystal button flashes ELEMENT READY when something is waiting.
The predators follow the same order, zone by zone: zones 1–3 are ELECTRIC, 4–6 FLUX, 7–9 FIRE, 10–12 CORE, 13–15 TOXIC, 16–18 ICE, then round again. Each wears a ring of its element and its tag is in its colour, and the type chart runs on every hit, both ways — bring what beats it. An ELEMENTAL BREAKOUT night rolls one element at random and every predator takes it for the night.
ULTIMATEThe bar under your health is your own, and it is siphoned from followers near you — anything within 6 tiles sends a wisp of static across every couple of seconds, and the charge lands when the wisp arrives. So the rate depends on how many are with you: ten fill it in about 30 seconds, three take a few minutes, and none in reach means it does not fill at all — the bar reads NO SQUAD IN RANGE when that happens.
Brawlers close to 2 tiles and mages hold at 4, so they are drawn from as a matter of course. A technician anchored to a far-off pylon and a worker away on a chore are not — walk over to them, or keep fighters at your side, if you want the bar moving.
Tap the bar when it is full to spend the whole thing on an ARMY SURGE: every follower and clone healed, WILL refilled, its own ultimate charged, and 1.6× damage for 10 seconds. Nothing is siphoned while a surge is running, so a surge never pays for the next one.
The ⌁ switch at the right end of the bar turns the siphon off. Wisps already in the air still land. There is no cost to leaving it on — the switch is there so you can quiet the effect down.
Kills are still counted — the Crystal shows your lifetime total — but they no longer earn anything.
Activating a new element makes your modulation stale, and both the Crystal button and the modulation chip flash RE-MODULATE.
MODULATIONWhat your followers are made of. It lives in one place: the mix selector, the small MIX chip at the bottom left (the Crystal opens it too). Its MIX tab is a honeycomb of six cells, one for each element: tap a cell to add that element to the mix or drop it (a lit cell is in; at least one has to stay on), and the centre cell puts them all back. An untouched mix takes in every element you earn. Recruits take their element from the mix. A follower that dies respawns as the same element it was — unless you have since dropped that element from the mix, in which case it is re-rolled: it comes back as one that is still on, at half its level, and an evolved one comes back as an egg that evolves into its new element's form on its next level-up — so re-modulating gradually re-colours the squad as the old elements fall. The COMMAND tab holds the HOLD and COME orders and who they go to. RE-MODULATE: a gold ! on the chip tells you an element unlock is waiting.
An untouched mix means every element you have, so an element you bring online is in the mix the moment it is activated and nothing nags you about it. The Crystal only asks you to RE-MODULATE when a new element lands outside a mix you have narrowed by hand, and opening the tab puts that prompt down.
