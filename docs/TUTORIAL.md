# The facility game — inspection, plan, and phase reports

The facility is the game (`js/facilityplay.js`; the map is docs/FACILITY.md). The page opens the authored map in a blackout, with the Crystal, the player and three followers in the Crystal Chamber, and the tutorial director (js/tutorial.js) says what to do next. Everything in it is the one game's: the followers, the squad, the creatures, the Crystal and the alarm, the relays, the classes, the weapons and the one save. (It began as a play mode of an older game, whose systems it took over one at a time; that game is gone, and nothing here depends on it.)

## Phase 2: the tutorial blackout (done)

**What it does.** The game opens in a blackout. Every room is dark, the Crystal Chamber and the room the player stands in included, whether doors are open or shut.

- **The lamp.** The player's monitor is the light that moves: a dim pool (at most 55% light, within a tile, fading to nothing at 4.8 tiles) in the **visor's green** (`#0f8`, the eye-lines of the monitor face; red while the player is angry), drawn as a soft colour wash over what it lights. It lights only the room the player is in (a wall's far side is not lit through it).
- **The floodlights.** Emergency floodlights stay on: the 7 that were already in the arena and on the catwalks, plus **8 along the hallway and 5 more across the arena**, placed a tile or two off the walls, clear of every door, the steps and the paths (they reach 90% of the hallway and most of the arena). The exit signs (a small green glow each), the crystal's glow and the Security Room's two monitor-wall lights are on too. All of it is data: `FP_TUT` (js/facilitytutorial.js) and the `floodlight` props in js/facilitymap.js.
- **Enemies.** They are never dimmed (units are not part of the room lighting), and an enemy standing in the dark also gets a faint ember-red glow on the floor under it, which is gone where there is half light or more.
- The message "BLACKOUT — FACILITY POWER IS DOWN / YOUR MONITOR LAMP IS YOUR LIGHT" is said once, a second after the start.

**Rules kept.** A room is lit while the player is in it and dark when they leave it; an open door throws a ground pool; a stairway glows. A blackout adds one condition to each: a room can be lit (and a door can throw its pool) only once it has *power*. `facilityPower(roomId, true)` powers a room (the stair room goes with the hallway), `facilityPower("all", true)` powers everything and ends the blackout, and from then on the ordinary rule applies, exactly as before. `?facilityplay&power=on` starts the facility lit (for development and the ordinary lighting checks).

**Tests.** Six checks in tests/facilityplay.js: it starts in a blackout with nothing powered, 20 floodlights (8 or more in the hallway reaching 90% of it, 9 or more in the arena), the exit signs and the designated lights; the player's own room is dark, and the lamp is dim, smooth, follows the player and lights nothing in another room; the lamp is the visor's green (and red when angry) and is drawn only in a blackout; powering one room lights it only while the player is in it, a door throws its pool only from a powered room, powering everything ends the blackout and the ordinary rule returns; enemies get the glow only when hostile, alive and in the dark, and only with the blackout on; the message is said once. The existing lighting, door, wall and speed checks pass unchanged with the blackout off, the real-page walk through two doors passes with it on, the facility data checks pass with the new floodlights, and a 173,000-step random walk through the facility finds no wall or floodlight to walk through.

**Known issues.** The lamp is a circle, not a cone (the player has no facing). The glow under an enemy is a flat ellipse and does not follow the floor's height on the steps. A wall piece is lit as a whole from its middle, so a long wall at the edge of a light changes in one step. The lamp's wash is drawn over the scene (so it also tints a wall standing in front of the player).

## Phase 3: the pack (done)

**What it does.** `js/classes.js` holds the three classes as data (Gunslinger, Technomancer, Mutator: name, colour, tagline, rooms, start kit, skills) and `CLASS_RULES`: any class may *carry* another class's gear, only the right class may *use* it (both switches are data). The class picker opens when the tutorial is FINISHED (see "Classes come after the tutorial" below); `?class=technomancer` names a class from the start, for development. `js/inventory.js` holds a data-driven `ITEMS` table (name, category, type, stack, colour, icon, class restriction, description, lore, stats, `use`) and the pack window: press **I** (or the PACK button), tabs (ALL, GEAR, TOOLS, PARTS, SAMPLES), item cells with quantity and a LOCKED chip for gear your class cannot use, an item page with INSPECT (the lore and the numbers) and USE. A new item is one row, an icon and a `use`.

| Item | For | What it really does |
|---|---|---|
| Ammo pack | anyone | +10 rounds in the gun (kept when the gun is full) |
| Flare | Gunslinger | lights the ground where you stand, 6.5 tiles, for 30 seconds (a real light in the blackout) |
| Power cell | Technomancer | overcharges the monitor lamp for a minute; it is also what a relay repair costs (phase 5) |
| Mutagen sample | Mutator | raises the nearest follower within 8 tiles a level (and so toward evolution) |
| Relay kit | anyone | a reusable tool: lets any class repair a relay (phase 5) |

**Supplies.** Supply cases stand in the rooms each class works in (`pickups` in js/facilitymap.js; the Armory and Security Room for the Gunslinger, the labs and Teleport Room 1 for the Technomancer, the lab and the Crystal Chamber for the Mutator, the kit in the Chamber). Each shows its item's icon over a beacon, so it can be found in the dark; walking over one takes it (a case that will not fit stays).

## Phase 4: class stations and perks (done)

A **station** is a prop the player can use (`stations` in js/facilitymap.js, `STATION_KINDS` in js/stations.js): walk up and press **E**, tap the plate that appears, or tap the station (the player walks there first). Its ring shows in the class's colour for the class that can use it and dim for everyone else, who are refused with the reason. Each class earns a **perk** (`CLASS_PERKS`, read through `classStat(name, base)` as docs/PLAYER-CLASSES.md plans, so the rest of the game needs no class code):

| Class | Station | Perk | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gunslinger | the Armory's weapon locker | Machine gun | the belt-fed gun, equipped from the locker: four times the rate of fire, each round a little softer (`WEAPONS.machinegun`, js/weapons.js: 8 damage every 11 frames against the service gun's 10 every 45); the locker gives four ammo packs |
| Gunslinger | the Security Room's console | CCTV sweeps | every enemy in the facility is marked (a diamond, an arrow at the screen's edge) for 25 s, then a 60 s cooldown |
| Technomancer | the console in Teleport Room 1 | Tuned lamp | the lamp reaches 25% further, an overcharge lasts 50% longer |
| Technomancer | the Show Room's mech bay | Spider mech | built from four legs and a core (seven seconds at the pad): 40% faster, the lamp 30% further |
| Mutator | a test chamber in the lab | Sequenced samples | costs a sample; afterwards a sample raises a follower one level more |
| Mutator | the walk through the lab | Lab coat and goggles | kept for good: a sample raises a follower two levels |

The pack shows the class, its rank (1, plus one per perk) and the perks.

## Phase 5: the electrical network (done)

Six **relays** (`relays` in js/facilitymap.js) stand broken: the Crystal Chamber's (feeds two sentries that guard the door), the hallway's (powers the hallway and the stair room; feeds a sentry and the charging station), the lobby's (powers the lobby and the Security Room), the Experiment Lab's (the gap in the north bench line; powers the lab, the passage and both teleport rooms), the Show Room's (east wall) and the Armory's (west wall; powers the Armory, the Arena, the Observation Deck, the Catwalks and the Nest Area); between them they feed every room of the facility, and twelve power cells are in the facility outside the class cases to pay for them. A relay is a station: a repair runs with a progress bar over the box and needs **either a skill or a part**, a rule in `CLASS_RULES.relay` (js/classes.js):

- a class with the `repair` skill (the Technomancer): **one power cell**, under two seconds, and it **starts by itself** when they stand at a broken relay with a cell and keep still;
- anyone else: the **relay kit** (found in the Crystal Chamber) and **two power cells**, five seconds, started by tapping the relay. This is the explicit design rule that lets the other classes restore a relay at all (their cells are spent by the relay, they do not use them).

A repair that is walked away from stops (nothing is spent). When a relay comes online: its rooms have power (`facilityPower`: the blackout lifts there, they are lit while you are in them, open doors throw pools), the box turns green with a turning ring, a ring of light and a flash spread from it, the screen says POWER RESTORED, the sentries it feeds shoot the nearest enemy within 9 tiles (8 damage every 50 frames), and the hallway's charging station refills your gun (a Technomancer also gets a cell; 90 s cooldown). All six online end the blackout. Adding an electrical room is a `relays` row and a prop.

**The room tag and the network panel (js/facilitynet.js).** The tag at the top right names the room the player is in, at each moment (a doorway keeps the room just left): Crystal Chamber, Computer Lab (the Front Lobby), Main Hallway, Arena, Armory… The network panel under it is the power grid: each relay feeds a few rooms and is online once it is repaired. The dial's outer ring is the relays online, its inner ring the rooms that have power again, and the middle "online / relays"; the state is OFFLINE, PARTIAL or ONLINE (all six, which ends the blackout). Under the dial: one line that says whether the room you stand in has power (no list of rooms). Both read the same data as the rest of the facility: `F.relays`, `relayOnline` (js/stations.js) and the powered rooms (`_fp.tut.powered`).

## Phase 6: followers and the Crystal defence (done)

`js/tutorial.js` is a short list of objectives (`TUT_STEPS`) with a plate at the top right (on a phone, a mailbox: below): find supplies; restore the Crystal Chamber's relay; **recruit** a follower (an egg on a pad, or the roster's + RECRUIT: 30 crystals, `recruitFollower`); **post** two followers near the Crystal (HOLD: `f.stance`, `f.post`); **fire your gun**; **defend the Crystal**; evolve a follower. It uses the game's own systems and builds no new ones: levels are `gainXp`, evolution is `evoCheck` at level 10 (js/evolution.js), the enemies are ants made by `spawnCreature` (js/clone.js) that hunt the Crystal under an alarm (js/alarm.js), which the director keeps up while they live.

- The tutorial's followers (the three you start with and every recruit) are **level 9** (`TUT.followerLevel`), and the assault's enemies level 8 (`TUT.enemyLevel`), so one assault takes a follower to the evolution level.
- **On a phone the plate is a mailbox** (js/tutorial.js `facilityDrawObjective`; ASKED: "in the mobile version [...] make it to where the recent task is in a little mailbox thing with a little exclamation point if there's a new objective"). A phone is a screen 700 px wide or less (`objectiveCompact`, the same line as the bottom-left tool's phone layout, `mixToolWide`): the plate would cover a third of the picture, so it is folded away into a small envelope (54 × 46 px, in the plate's place and colour). While the objective is one the player has **not opened yet** the envelope wears a gold, breathing `!` (and rattles for about a second when the objective has just changed). A **tap on the envelope** unfolds the plate out of it (the objective, its text and its hint, exactly as on a bigger screen) and reads it: the `!` goes. A **tap on the plate** (it has a small arrow at its upper right) folds it away again. The next objective (the next tutorial step, the class's own, the closing line) puts the `!` back; an objective that comes while the plate is open is read at once and the plate stays open. The press is the mailbox's alone (`objectivePress` / `objectiveRelease`, js/input.js, before the targeting's): no move order, ring or long press starts under it, and a press let go off it does nothing. Where the objective ends is `_fpObjBottom` as before (the box's bottom while folded, the plate's while open, animated between), so the round button and the ammunition icon go under whichever is showing. What the mailbox remembers (`objKey`, `objSeen`, `objOpen`) lives on the tutorial's script and is not saved: a reloaded game shows the current objective as new. Any wider screen has the plate as it always was, and it takes no taps. (A phone on its side is wider than 700 px and gets the plate; the bottom-left tool covers it there, as before.) Tests: tests/mailbox.js.
- **The assault** (`TUT.waves`): a 20 second countdown, then four scouts from the east end of the hallway, then 25 seconds later three strikers. The alarm is up while they live. A wounded enemy that runs is made to press on. A Crystal below 15% is saved: the enemies are called off, it is mended to 60% and the assault comes again. After a minute past the last wave, what is left is called off ("THE REST FLED"). Beating it pays every follower 400 XP (`gainXp`), and the fighting earns more; followers that reach level 10 evolve as they always do.
- The tutorial finishes when a follower has evolved, and says so once.

A scripted run through every step (collect, relay, recruit, hold, the gun, the assault, evolution) with a Technomancer and a Gunslinger finishes with two evolved followers and the Crystal untouched at full health.

**Known issues.** The tutorial's enemies bleed 0.01 HP a frame like every creature (js/game.js), so a wounded one that keeps away can die on its own, without giving a kill's XP (the assault's reward covers it). A prop (a floodlight, a sentry) in an enemy's straight path can hold it for a moment until its unstick logic replans; the spawn rows keep clear of them. The sentries' shots credit no XP to anyone. The first assault is tuned by feel (two waves, level 8) and not yet by play-testing with a real player: `TUT` in js/tutorial.js is the table to change.

## Classes come after the tutorial (revision)

**The rule.** The tutorial has no classes. Only when its last objective is done does the class picker open ("TUTORIAL COMPLETE — CHOOSE YOUR CLASS"), and only once a class is chosen do that class's items enter the facility.

- **The tutorial without a class.** Six objectives: find supplies, restore the Crystal Chamber's relay, recruit, post followers, defend the Crystal, evolve a follower (the gun lesson, below, made it seven). Only five supply cases are in the facility (ammunition in the Armory and the Security Room, power cells in the lab and the lobby, the relay kit in the Crystal Chamber). With no class a relay is repaired with the kit and two power cells, the way every class without the Technomancer's skill does. The class stations show no ring and cannot be used.
- **The picker** opens by itself when the last objective (evolve) is done, absorbs taps until a class is chosen, and is never shown again.
- **Class items enter.** Choosing a class gives its start kit and lets that class's cases in: each arrives in a column of light, with the message "SUPPLIES FOR YOUR CLASS HAVE ARRIVED": the Gunslinger's flares in the Armory (2 cases), the Technomancer's cells in Teleport Room 1 (2), the Mutator's samples in the lab and the Crystal Chamber (3). A case for another class never enters (`class` on a `pickups` row in js/facilitymap.js; `facilityClassItemsEnter`).
- **Then the class objective:** go to the class's station and earn its gear (the objective plate turns to the class's colour), and the class's rules (the Technomancer's repair skill and its auto-repair, the Gunslinger's gun and CCTV, the Mutator's sequencer) apply from then on. It is the first step of the class's own stage (below).

## The class stages (the brief's step 9)

ASKED: *"Each class should have its own tutorial stage after the initial tutorial."* *"Integrate each class into its tutorial progression and test the full loops."*

Once a class is chosen, the objective plate runs **the class's stage** (`TUT_CLASS_STAGES`, js/tutorial.js): its gear first (`TUT_CLASS_STEP`, as before), then the class's loop, each step with its words and a hint, counted on the plate (*TECHNOMANCER · 3/4 · CHARGE YOUR GAUNTLET*). Every step uses what the game already has:

| Class | Steps |
|---|---|
| Gunslinger | 1. GET YOUR GEAR (the machine gun, the Armory's locker) · 2. SWEEP THE CAMERAS (the Security Room's console) · 3. LIGHT A FLARE (from the pack) · 4. HUNT IN THE ARENA (three creatures defeated, the squad helping) |
| Technomancer | 1. GET YOUR GEAR (the spider mech, the mech bay) · 2. RESTORE ANOTHER RELAY (one power cell, it starts by itself) · 3. CHARGE YOUR GAUNTLET (three punches) · 4. THROW THE ARC (two arcs from out of reach) |
| Mutator | 1. GET YOUR GEAR (the lab coat, the walk through the lab) · 2. GROW YOUR CLONE (the Clone Bay) · 3. UPGRADE YOUR CLONE (one rank) · 4. FIGHT BESIDE IT (three lashes) · 5. SPLASH A SERUM (the serum hood, then Q or the pill) |

- **What is counted** is counted from the moment the class is chosen: the class's own code reports it (`tutTally`: a strike that lands, by the weapon that landed it; an arc; a clone grown; an upgrade bought; a serum splashed; a flare lit), and the kills and the relays online are counted from what they were then. A step already done when it comes up is passed, as in the tutorial; each step done is said (*CHARGE YOUR GAUNTLET — DONE   NEXT: THROW THE ARC*), and the end (*TECHNOMANCER STAGE COMPLETE — YOUR CLASS IS YOURS*) gives the closing line the plate had before.

One that is killed pays as any kill and is replaced, up to six times a step; when the step is done it goes and pays nothing.
- **Saved:** the step, what has been counted and where the counts began (`cstep`, `tally`, `ck0`, `cr0` on the tutorial's script record). A game saved before the stages existed starts its stage at the gear and passes what is already done.
- Tests: tests/classstage.js plays each class's stage through with the game's own functions (the stations, the relay repair, the weapons through the dial, the Clone Bay, the serum hood), from the class picker to the closing line; tests/mailbox.js follows the plate through it.

## The gun lesson (revision)

The tutorial teaches the player's gun (js/gunfire.js): a seventh objective, **FIRE YOUR GUN**, comes after posting the followers and before the assault. A stray ant (level 3, plenty of health, grazing, made by `spawnCreature`) is put in view 4 to 6 tiles away in the main hallway and the player is given training rounds (the gun is topped up to 8). The objective counts the player's own shots (`playerShotsFired`) to three. The hints teach it in two steps: *get close to the ant: the gun takes aim by itself and a round button with the ant's picture appears* (see Targeting, below); then *click the button as often as you like, or hold it to keep firing; each shot costs a round*. A stray that dies is replaced (up to four times); when the lesson is done the stray is taken away. The assault's hint reminds the player that they can shoot too. The tutorial has seven objectives: supplies, relay, recruit, post, gun, defend, evolve.

## Class gear (revision)

Each class earns a **signature piece of gear** (`CLASS_SIGNATURE` in js/classes.js: the perk it is). The gear is seen on the player: it is modelled onto the same cube (design/evolutions/forms30.js, Nos. 840–843) and baked to a sheet of its own (img/hd/player_gun.png, player_gun_aim.png, player_mech.png, player_coat.png; `node design/evolutions/bake.js <three-dir> <out> <ids> 186 2.2`, then cropped to the box in `HD_BOX`). js/playergear.js picks the sheet (`playerGearSheet`); js/hdsprites.js draws from it.

- **Gunslinger — the machine gun.** Found in the **Armory** (the weapon locker). The cube still floats; two of its four cables are long tentacles that carry the big belt-fed gun low at its left side. When the weapon is **armed** they pull it up level beside the screen, looking down the sights, and the action frames fire it (recoil and a muzzle flash) for 14 frames after each shot. It turns to the locked enemy. Four times the rate of fire, each round a little softer (the `WEAPONS.machinegun` row), and the locker gives four ammo packs.
- **Technomancer — the spider mech.** It has to be **built in the Show Room**: the legs (two cases in Teleport Room 1 and the passage, two each) and the core (in the Show Room) go into the **mech bay**, a seven-second build at the pad (walk away and it stops, nothing is spent). An armoured walker with the cube seated in its cockpit, four thick jointed legs that walk with the player; 40% faster (`playerSpeed` ×1.4) and the lamp reaches 30% further.
- **Mutator — the lab coat and goggles.** Put on by walking **from Teleport Room 1 through the passage into the Experiment Lab**, in that order (any other walk starts again), and **kept for good**. A draped white coat open at the front, the four tentacles hanging out under its hem, with a collar, lapels, pockets, stains and a vial of mutagen, and safety goggles pushed up on the brow. A mutagen sample raises a follower two levels (`mutagenLevels` +1); the sequencer's perk adds one more.

**Known issues.** The gear sheets have no angry (red-eyed) variant: the plain cube's `player_angry` sheet is not used while gear is worn. Each sheet decodes to about 9–10 MB, loaded only when its gear is owned. The sheets are baked at 186 px cells over 4.4 units to hold the mech and the gun inside the cell at every facing.

## Saving (revision)

**One game, one key.** The facility keeps **`conduit_fp_save`** (js/facilitysave.js); the settings keep `conduit_fp_gfx` and `conduit_fp_perf` (js/config.js). While the game runs, `Storage.setItem` / `removeItem` let through only `conduit_fp_*` keys (js/facilityplay.js `FP_STORE_OK`, installed before the page boots), and the website's background writes nothing at all.

**What is saved:** the class and its perks, the pack and the ammunition (and shots fired, which the gun lesson counts), the weapon in hand, the weapons owned, their levels and the rifle's charge, which supply cases have been taken, which relays are online (their rooms are powered again, silently, and the blackout ends as usual when every room has power), how far through the tutorial the player is (the director's step, what it has already said, the followers it started with, the gun lesson's baseline), the followers (the roster record: level, XP, evolution, traits, the move picked, the room each holds; if none were saved the three to start are made), the DNA splices, the delay still to run on each empty follower spot, the Crystal's health, and where the player stood (if it is still floor). It is written every three seconds while playing and when the page is hidden or closed.

**What it does not save:** the enemies (an assault in progress starts again with its countdown), a repair or a mech build in progress (begun again; nothing was spent), cooldowns, the lamp's overcharge, the scrap cases on the floor, the stances of the followers. A save that cannot be read, or is of another version, is ignored.

**Items are obtained or not:** a case taken stays taken after a refresh, and the class's own cases are in the facility again once the class is (the ones taken are gone). A game left at the class picker (tutorial done, no class chosen) opens the picker again.

**NEW GAME** (SETTINGS on the dock, or the phone's round SETTINGS button, and the game-over card) asks first, then throws the save away and opens a fresh facility (`facilityNewGame`: a reload).

## The alarm, and the end (js/alarm.js)

An alarm is the state of a fight: while one is up the arena's creatures hunt instead of pottering about, the red triangle shows beside the room tag, and the tutorial's assault keeps it up until it is over. `triggerAlarm(type, x, y)` raises one: a PROXIMITY ALARM wakes the creatures within 6 tiles of its source, a FACILITY BREACH every creature there is; one lasts `ALERT_DURATION` (600 frames, 10 s) after the last thing that fed it, and `clearAlarm` sends a creature nobody is fighting back to its round. **The Crystal destroyed is the game over** (`showGameOver`): the overlay says CRYSTAL DESTROYED with the crystals you had, and its NEW GAME starts the facility again.

## The arena's creatures, what a kill pays, levels, and weapons (revision)

**Every creature is made by `spawnCreature(opts)`** (js/clone.js): `species` and `className` (js/species.js `SPECIES`; a species or class the table does not have makes nothing), `x` and `y` (tiles; the Crystal's when none is given), `level` (js/levels.js `setPredatorLevel`; the arena's `creatureLevel()` when none) and `state` (`wander` or `hunt`; by default it hunts while an alarm is up). It is its class's numbers, its species' body, its charge-up special (js/abilities.js), and its class's `shardDrop` and `dnaDrops`. The arena's spawner, the tutorial's assault and its stray ant all come through it.

**The arena's creatures (js/facilityspawn.js `FP_SPAWN`).** Four are there at the start, each a different kind, and one more arrives every 1200 frames (20 seconds) at a spot on the arena floor that is out of the player's sight (and at least 11 tiles off) and away from the others, up to seven alive at once, always the kind there are fewest of: ants, beetles, spiders, scorpions, mantises and moths, in a class by weight (scout 3, worker 3, striker 2, tank 1; never a nymph or a boss) at a level of 2 to 4. They wander the arena from spot to spot, 3 to 9 tiles at a time (`facilityWanderTarget`, which js/predator.js asks), and hunt only when provoked or when an alarm is up.

**A kill pays on the spot** (js/drops.js `onPredatorDeath`, from the death sweep in js/game.js, which also counts `lifetimeKills`). A creature you kill pays its class's `shardDrop` in crystals (the code's shards), said over it as "◆ n": an ant scout 3, an ant tank 8, a beetle scout 6, a moth boss 65. It drops its DNA splices for the Clone Bay (`addDNA` under species_class; a nymph's count as an ant scout's, so the first kills feed the ant clones) and, in the arena, a case of **scrap metal** where it fell (75% of the time, 1 to 3 scrap by class, lying for a minute), which the Armory's terminal turns into ammunition. A nymph is worth nothing in crystals.

**Levels (js/levels.js).** `creatureLevel()` is the arena's level: the top of `FP_SPAWN.levels`, never under `LEVEL_START` (5). A new clone comes out at it (js/clone.js `makeClone`); a recruit at `RECRUIT_BEHIND` (3) under it, never under 5 (`recruitLevel`); the tutorial's followers at `TUT.followerLevel` (9) and its assault at `TUT.enemyLevel` (8). There is no work XP: **XP comes from kills.** A creature killed is worth `XP_YIELD[its class] × its level / 5` (nymph 20, worker 60, scout 100, striker 140, tank 200, boss 600 at level 5); the unit that landed the kill gets `XP_KILLER_SHARE` (0.6) and your units within `XP_SHARE_RANGE` (4 tiles) share the rest; a kill with no unit's last blow (your gun, an effect) pays `XP_ASSIST_ONLY` (0.5) of it to the units near; a unit that dies loses `XP_DEATH_LOSS` (0.25) of its progress toward the next level, never a level. Total XP to reach level L is L³, the cap is 100, and a follower evolves at 10.

**Follower ultimates (js/elements.js `FOLLOWER_ULTIMATES`).** A double-tap on a follower whose bar is full fires its element's ultimate, and each works on what is within `ULT_REACH` (12 tiles) of the follower (the facility is rooms and halls; there is nothing larger to fill): **Volcanic Eruption** (fire: the biggest enemy in reach, then a crater), **EMP** (electric: strips the shields of the enemies in reach and speeds the followers in reach), **Blizzard** (ice: a field 12 tiles round the follower that keeps freezing the enemies in it), **Disorient** (flux: the creatures in reach turn on each other for 4 s), **Smoke Screen** (toxic: smoke creeps out `SMOKE_SCREEN_REACH` = 12 tiles; the enemies in reach lose accuracy for 8 s). A bonded pair with both bars full fires both at once (js/bonds.js). Core has no ultimate for now, and no bar: its Shield Grant (a shield on every follower) went with the followers' shield, as did its special, Radiant Pulse, and the core forms' moves, AEGIS BURST and AEGIS BLOOM (REPORTED: "Remove the bubble shield from the followers and all relevant abilities. We'll add a new look and iron out ability information"). The creatures' shields (a tank's, a boss's) are as they were, and EMP still strips them.

**Weapons (js/weapons.js).** One weapon in hand, from a table `WEAPONS`: the **service gun** (everyone), the **machine gun** (the Gunslinger's, from the Armory locker, which equips it; it can be put away and taken again), and the **laser rifle**, assembled from 15 scrap at the Armory terminal. Each has a level (1 to 3): a level hits 30% harder and fires 12% faster and costs scrap. The laser runs on **fuel cells** (12 charges each; it loads the next by itself): a beam that hits at once, through whatever stands behind the target (60% damage), range 12 tiles, and it says NO FUEL CELLS when it has none. **Fuel cells are in the computer lab (the Front Lobby) once its relay is online**: three cases arrive in columns of light with the relay (a pickup with `needs: "relay_lobby"`). The HUD button and the ring's caption show the weapon's own reading (AMMO n, or LASER ◆ charges +cells).

**The Armory terminal** (the computer desk in the Armory; it needs the Armory relay online) opens a window: **scrap → ammunition** (2 scrap = 1 ammo pack), **equip** a weapon you have, and **upgrade** or **assemble** one. What is in hand is what the player is seen holding: the machine gun and the laser rifle each have their own baked sheets (player_gun*, player_laser*; the laser's glows cyan and its beam flashes on the firing frames), and the mech and the coat keep the player when owned. The weapons, their levels and the rifle's charge are saved with the facility game; scrap drops are not.

## 3D props (revision)

The sentries, the potted plants, the arena's missile truck and liquid tanks, the Experiment Lab's tank and the catwalk bridges are real 3D models, baked to sprites like the player and the followers (docs/ART-PROCESS.md has the bake pipeline for characters; this is its sibling for props).

- **The models** are in `design/props/models/<family>.js` (sentry, plant, truck, tank, bridge): three.js code that builds a group with the footprint's centre at the origin and the floor at y = 0. **One world unit is one tile**: the game draws a tile step as 60 x 30 px (`TILE_W`, `TILE_H`), so the baker's camera is the game's 2:1 iso one at 84.85 px to the unit, and a height of H game pixels is `px(H)` units. `design/props/bake_props.js <three-dir> <families|all>` renders every variant, crops it, packs a sheet per family into `img/props/<family>.png` and writes where everything is (and each variant's *anchor*, the ground point under the model's origin, and its *marks*) to `design/props/out/<family>.json`, then `js/propatlas.js` (all of them as data: the page cannot fetch files).
- **The game** (`js/propsprites.js`) puts the sprite with its anchor at the prop's centre. Plants pick one of four healthy or two dead variants by seed (a third are dead, as before). A sentry has 16 sprites, one for each 22.5 degrees of where its head aims (`it.aim`); its lamp (cyan when fed, a blinking red when not) and muzzle flash are painted on the sprite's `lamp` and `muzzle` marks. **A big prop** (the truck, the tanks) is a set of one-tile cells that the game sorts by depth, and each cell draws the whole sprite clipped to its own tile's column (the diamond on the floor and the same lifted as high as the sprite goes), so a unit walking round it goes in front of the parts that are behind it and behind the parts in front; the floor under each cell is drawn first (a solid tile is not drawn by itself), and a round tank adds sprite-only cells round its rim, so its edge is not cut square.
- **In the dark** the sprite is dimmed the way the flat drawing was: its silhouette in the dark navy the colours fade to, drawn first, then the lit sprite over it at the room's light level (the dimmed pen carries that level in `ctx._k`). A prop whose sheet has not arrived is drawn the old, flat way.
- **The catwalk bridges** are map data: `bridges` in js/facilitymap.js, each `from` a round prop (a tank) `to` a room (the catwalks). `_psBridgeItems` lays a row of one-tile pieces from the tank's east rim to the catwalks' deck edge at the tank's y (a tank end, plain pieces, a deck end), at the arena floor's height; the sprite carries the deck's height (170 px above the arena floor: the arena is 1.8 tiles down, the catwalks 3.2 up, 34 px a tile). They are decoration, not floor: the catwalks stay blocked off.
- **To change one:** edit its model, `node design/props/bake_props.js /tmp/threepkg <family>`, look at it in the game (`?power=on` lights everything), and commit the png and `js/propatlas.js`. Two things the baker's colours need: unlit (`MeshBasicMaterial`) hex colours come out brighter than written (three r128 treats them as linear), so a model's emissive colours should go through a `flat(hex)` helper, as in the truck model.

## Every prop in 3D (revision)

After the first five (sentry, plants, truck, tanks, bridges) **every other prop is a 3D sprite too**, so the facility is one coherent 3D world. `design/props/BRIEF.md` is the brief the modelling was done from (scale, the shared style kit `design/props/models/_style.js`, the footprint and orientation rules, the variant naming) and `design/props/solids.json` lists every prop in the game. The sheets and what is on them:

- **office** (reception desk, the computer desks in five sizes, the armory workbench, consoles in four sizes, server racks), **lounge** (couches in two colours, the table, four beanbags, the ping-pong table, vending machines in three looks and a double, coolers, bins, chairs), **lab** (the lab benches, the fume hood, the two test chambers, carts, generators, toolboxes, speakers, the cable spool), **tech** (teleporter pads in cyan and violet, the charger and the relays each ON and OFF, the mech bay, lockers incl. the armoured gun locker, cabinets, display cases with a crystal, a drone or a headset, the telescope), **cargo** (four crates, two crate stacks, four barrels, the floodlight, the barrier, and the **supply case** the pickups are drawn as).

## Architecture shading (revision)

The walls, doors, glass, stairs, rock, scaffold and floors are shaded to match the 3D props (soft values, no bold ink outline). Rules (js/facilitystyle.js `FP_EDGE`, `_fpWallGrad`; js/facilitystyleall.js; js/facilitydecor.js):
- **Walls** (`_fpStyleWall`): flat bands as before, then ONE gradient over the wainscot and the wall face (darker at the floor, a shadow under the chair rail and under the cap), a light chair rail, creases under it, a skirting board, a recessed panel joint at every tile, a soft glow under the room-coloured accent strip, a light bevel on the top edge and a dark contact line at the foot. The slab on top has a light bevel and a soft far edge (no ink).
- **Doors** (`_fpStyleDoor`): brushed-steel leaves with two recessed insets (shadow on top and left, light on bottom and right), a vision panel with a reflection, a hazard-striped lead edge with a seal, screws, a frame with a lit edge, and a lintel with an emissive status strip (the room's colour open, amber shut). The header above the lintel takes the light of the wall beside it (the room that shows its face, and the pool of an open door), not the door's; a door in glass has no thick top.
- **Glass** (`_fpStyleGlass`): a pale sill, a tinted pane that is lighter at the top, reflection streaks, a steel rail, a steel mullion post and an accent strip along the sill.
- **Stairs** (`_fpStyleStair`): treads with a lit nosing, soft-shaded risers and a crease where they meet the floor. **Scaffold** (`_fpDrawFrame`, `_fpDrawRail`): round steel (a shaded side, the steel, a lit edge), every kind of line in one stroke. **Rock** (`_fpDrawRock`, `_fpDrawDeckCliff`): a dark crease and a pale lit edge on the lip, a dark foot.
- **Floors** (`_fpDrawOps`, `_fpStyleFloorFor`, `_fpFloorBuild`): an opaque tile base is painted 2 px wide so it covers the fringe of the tile sprite under it (no dark seams); each tile has a lit far edge, a shaded near edge, a faint sheen and a hair of tone variation; the floor is darkened along every wall's foot (`A` marks from `_fpWallEdges`, parallel to the edge). All of it is baked once into the tile's sprite.
- **Cost.** A gradient is made once per `key` (in the piece's own frame, on the real context) and reused by every piece and frame; pieces use screen coordinates computed once, no arrays per point; lines of one colour are one stroke. Measured in the page (best of 25 runs of facilityDrawItems' walls, ms): hallway 4.6 -> 6.1, lobby 1.7 -> 2.7, arena 3.2 -> 3.8, catwalks 2.4 -> 3.8.

## Frame cost (revision)

Measured with the real page in headless Chrome (software rendering, so absolute numbers are pessimistic): `render()` per frame in the lobby / arena / hallway / lab / chamber went from about 14 / 16 / 20 / 15 / 10 ms to about 9 / 10 / 11 / 16 / 8 ms with the changes below (the lab, with its glass walls and many sprites, is the heaviest). What was done and what was tried:

- **Big props are drawn from cached slices, not clips.** A multi-cell prop (the truck, the tanks, a bench, the reception desk) used to draw its whole sprite once per cell, clipped to the cell's tile column; the clip and its restore were a fifth of the frame. Now each cell's column is cut ONCE from the sprite into a small canvas (`_psSlice`, cropped to what is in it) and drawn with a plain `drawImage`.
- **Dim props are one image.** A prop in a dim room was its dark silhouette with the lit sprite over it, two blits. Now: nearly dark (light < 0.1) is the silhouette alone, in between is composed ONCE per light level (a twelfth steps) into a small canvas (`_psDimmed`), full light is the sprite.
- **Contact shadows only on a prop's outline** (an interior cell is under the sprite anyway), and **the prop sheets are loaded as the facility opens** (`facilityPropsPreload`, `decoding = "async"`) so a prop is never seen flat for a moment.
- **Slow devices:** the settings window's GRAPHICS switch (LOW) also drops the contact shadows and the soft glows in the facility, and the game drops to LOW by itself, once and for the session, if it runs under 28 frames a second for three readings in a row (`facilityPerfTick`).
- **Tried and dropped:** drawing every wall and stair piece once into a bitmap and blitting it. It made no difference to the frame time here (the cost is filling pixels, not the number of polygons) and cost 30 MB, so it is not in. Culling was already exact (300 to 500 items a frame are drawn, all on screen); the floor tiles were already stamped from cached sprites.
- **Memory** after a tour of every room: 23 MB of script heap, about 250 prop slices and 1,700 floor-tile sprites.

## Targeting (revision)

The gun finds its own target and a round button fires it (js/targeting.js; the long-press ring's ATTACK on a creature still locks on and fires too, js/commands.js):

- **Auto-lock.** Every 8 frames the nearest creature within 9 tiles that the player can see (no wall between: `_fpLos`) becomes the TARGET. The gun is armed without a message (`setPlayerAttackMode(true, true)`) and the red corners close in on it (js/gunfire.js `drawLockReticle`). A creature that is at least 1.6 tiles nearer than the target takes the lock over; the target is dropped (and the gun put away) at 12 tiles, or when it is out of sight, and with nobody in range the gun is put away.
- **The round button** sits at the top left, where the objective's plate (on a phone, its mailbox) ends (on a phone beside the menu hub): the target's picture in a ring that is its health (green, yellow, red), its level on the upper left, a **gun badge** at the lower right (it shows that the button fires; it is red while the trigger is held, amber while the gun cycles, grey with no ammunition) and an **INFO badge** at the upper right. Under it are the creature's name and the ammunition. It pops in when a target is acquired.
- **Fire.** A click on the button is a shot at the locked creature; holding it keeps firing at the gun's own rate, wherever the finger goes (`gunHold.button`). A click made while the gun cycles is kept and fires when it is ready. Out of ammunition the hold ends with one OUT OF AMMO.
- **INFO** opens a card beside the button (level, class, element, health, bite, speed, and what the species and the class are like); tapping it again, or Esc, closes it.
- **Switch.** A tap on another creature locks that one instead and keeps it (up to 18 tiles) even when another is nearer; the tap is only a lock, never a shot. A tap on the locked creature is the gun's (a click is a shot, hold to keep firing, as before).
- **Stow.** A tap on the ammunition icon (below) while the gun is armed puts it away, and it stays away while creatures are in range; with none near, auto-lock comes back (a tap on a creature also brings it back).
- **The ammunition** (js/targeting.js `_tgAmmoIcon`; ASKED: "move the information where it says ammo or armed up where the old little index UI shows up for the predators [...] make it subtle, like an icon with ammo and have a number on it") is a small cartridge (the laser's fuel: a cell) with the round count beside it, in a faint pill at the round button's place at the top left: under the button's name with a creature locked, in the button's place with none. A quiet blue-grey, amber and breathing a little while the gun is armed, red when it is empty. It replaced the big ARMED / AMMO chip that stood above the dock.
- **The bottom-left tool** (js/mixtool.js) is only RETREAT, HOLD and COME, with the element selector (WHO) above them (on a tablet or a desktop the facility has ONE ALL button there instead of the selector, and the enemy card and the round button are at the bottom right: see "The HUD: one group to a corner" below; a phone keeps what is described here). **It has no tabs:** every element is online from the first frame, so there is no ELEMENTS tab (nothing to bring online) and no tab row (a phone keeps a BATTLE title and a close button). The tool's orders go to EVERYONE unless an element or a selection says otherwise (`squadMode = "all"`).
- **The enemy card** (js/mixtool.js, js/targeting.js; ASKED: "whenever you get near an enemy, a little index pops up in that little corner as well, and you can have your followers attack that creature if you tap it, and depending on what creatures you have selected or what followers you have selected, they'll attack it"). A small card pops up above the tool's controls (on a phone, above the BATTLE chip, so it can be tapped without opening anything) for **the creature the gun is locked on**, so the round button and the card are always one creature; with the gun put away it is the nearest creature in range and in sight, and it keeps its creature while that one is near and in sight rather than jumping to a nearer one (`targetIndexSubject`). A tap on another creature makes that one the card's at once. The card has its picture (the round button's, as a data URL), its kind (`SPIDER STRIKER`: the name without the element), `LV 6 · ELECTRIC` in its colour, a health bar that moves with its health (the card is not rebuilt for it, so it pops up once and a press is never lost to a redraw) with `HP 40/100 · BITE 7`, and a strip that says who goes: `ATTACK · EVERYONE 4`, `ATTACK · FIRE 2` or `ATTACK · SELECTED 3`, then `ATTACKING · 3 OF 4` once they are sent.
  - **Who goes** is whoever the tool is set to, the same pool as RETREAT, HOLD and COME (`getCommandPool`): ALL, one element (WHO), or the roster cards that are selected (they win).
  - **An attack order** (`mixToolAttack` sets `attackOrder = { target, until }` on each; js/npc.js `attackOrderTarget`): the unit goes for THAT creature, whatever is nearer and whether it is calm or not (followers otherwise ignore a creature that has not been provoked), from any distance up to the leash, and fights it with its own role: a brawler closes in and orbits it, a mage goes to its firing distance and shoots, a technician leaves its post for it. It ends when the creature dies, after `BATTLE_ATTACK_FRAMES` (thirty seconds), when the creature is further than `BATTLE_ATTACK_LEASH` (22 tiles) from the player (you have moved on), or when the unit is given another order: RETREAT, HOLD, COME, or a stance order from the roster. A unit that is hurt below half still goes to heal first. Over its head the unit says ATTACKING.
  - **A creature that is pushed back stays in the building** (js/combat.js `updateStatusEffects`): a core follower's BASH and a flux pull push with a velocity. An old corridor clamp used to throw a pushed creature out of the facility, where it walked back through the void; a push now goes as far as the walls let it (`facilityConstrain`). It mattered here because an attack order is what sends the squad, and its core and flux members, at a creature.

## NPC flow (revision)

REPORTED: "followers getting stuck behind things and slowing down the frame rate significantly". Measured first (a squad of ten walking every room, hunters sent from random cells, the real assault, in the real page), then fixed:

- **Creatures could not walk north or south.** An old clamp in js/predator.js kept every creature's y to four rows (the lane of the game this one grew out of) in two places; `facilityConstrain` then put the step back, leaving only the east-west part. Every creature in the arena, every hunter of the assault, pushed at a wall on its row until the unstick teleported it to a cell centre, a hundred frames later, to do it again. The clamps are gone; a hunter now walks the route.
- **Wandering and hunting creatures steer themselves** (their goal is their own, not the player or the Crystal), so `facilityReroute` leaves them alone: it was taking a wanderer's step that ran into a prop for a step toward the player across the map and planning a route to him every other frame.
- **Hunters follow the route.** The hunt heads for the next waypoint of the route to the Crystal (`facilitySteer`), and weaves only where it can see the Crystal (and less: ±17 degrees, not ±40). The reroute that sat behind every unit (`facilityReroute`) only recognised a step that pointed straight (28 degrees) at its goal; a weave of 40 degrees was not recognised and the unit pushed at the wall. It now also reroutes a step that runs into a wall (or a unit that has been blocked for three frames) toward the goal the step points nearest to, within 70 degrees.
- **The arena's creatures never wandered.** The utility AI promoted a calm creature (`isWanderer` is off for them, so that an alarm can send them hunting) to the hunt state each frame, and the CALM rule put it back to wandering later in the same frame, after the wander code had been skipped: they stood where they were spawned, for good. A calm, unprovoked creature is no longer promoted. Two smaller things in the same code: on arriving at its spot a wanderer set its pause timer every frame it stood there, so it never ran out and it never went on (the pause is now set once, 20 to 60 frames), and a wander spot is walked to by the route (`facilitySteer`), with a creature shut in a pocket of props (no spot in sight) given one it can walk to (`facilityWanderTarget`).
- **No retreat.** A wounded creature (under about a third of its health) used to run away from its attacker in a straight line to heal at a nest, a thing the facility never had: it ran into the nearest wall, or, with the hunt score close behind, flipped between running and hunting every few frames and went nowhere (the assault's creatures piled up at the east end of the hallway for a thousand frames). The retreat state is gone from js/predator.js: a creature stands and fights, wandering or hunting (the tutorial still turns a straggler back to the hunt, in case).
- **Routes are kept** (`facilitySteer`, `FP_NAV`). A route was replanned whenever the goal moved to the next cell: a squad following you planned ten routes at once, many times a second, which was most of the stalls. Now a route is replanned only when it no longer works (the next waypoint has dropped out of sight, the goal has moved 2.5 tiles or a third of the way to it, or it is 360 frames old); a goal that creeps is followed by turning the last leg; a goal that stands still (the Crystal) is planned once, so two routes of the same length are no longer a coin toss for a unit that then walks neither. At most three plans a frame (the rest go on with the old route, or straight on, and are planned in the next frames), and a goal with no way to it is searched for at most every 30 frames (and is refused at once when it is on another stretch of floor: the floor is labelled into stretches the first time a route is asked for, `_fpComps`).
- **The floor checks are five times cheaper.** `facilityWalkable`, `facilitySolidAt`, `facilityRoomAt` and `facilityCanStep` (js/facility.js) scanned every wall and solid of the building (132 and 150) for every question; a route is hundreds of them, and every unit asks every frame. They now look at a grid of 4 x 4 tile buckets (`_facIndex`: built once per map, a non-enumerable property, the items in their old order, so the answers are exactly the same, which a test checks over five thousand random probes). A walkable check went from 1.6 to 0.3 microseconds, a line of sight from 47 to 7, a step from 2.4 to 0.5.
- **Measured** (ten followers walking all thirteen rooms, three thousand steps): routes planned 8,900 to 3,200 (the rest are real: the goal moved), time in the planner 3.8 s to 0.5 s, steps over 15 ms 83 to a handful; the assault's creatures reach the Crystal (they stopped 30 tiles short before). One consequence to watch: creatures that could not move now reach and fight the squad, so the fights (and the assault's balance) are real for the first time.
