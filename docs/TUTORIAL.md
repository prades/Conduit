# The playable tutorial milestone — inspection, plan, and phase reports

The tutorial is an extension of the facility mode (`?facilityplay`, docs/FACILITY.md), not a rewrite. The facility already runs on the strip game's player, followers, enemies, Crystal, wave and power code (`facilityPlayStart` resets the strip game and puts the Crystal, the player and three followers in the Crystal Chamber; the strip game's save is never touched).

### Conflicts to settle before phase 3

1. **(Settled: the three classes were built as rows of the registry; see phase 4.)** "Maintain the three existing player classes" named classes that were not in the project. Phases 3 to 5 depend on them (class-restricted equipment, who can repair a relay, who uses biological samples). Nothing has been invented. A decision is needed: build the three (Gunslinger, Technomancer, Mutator) as the first rows of the planned class registry in docs/PLAYER-CLASSES.md (the registry is data, so they would sit beside the planned classes), or map them onto planned classes.
2. **Relays and the existing power system.** The strip game's relays are pylons on a lane with nests and batteries; the facility needs relay *stations* in rooms. Phase 5 should add a thin facility layer (a relay station object, a room's power set, `facilityPower(room, on)`, which phase 2 already provides) that the strip game's power code can feed, not a second power simulation.
3. **The old tube-lane HUD** ("NETWORK OFFLINE", "TAP HERE TO BOOT THE POWER UNIT") is still shown in the facility; the tutorial will have to reuse or hide it in phase 5.

## Phase 2: the tutorial blackout (done)

**What it does.** `?facilityplay` opens in a blackout. Every room is dark, the Crystal Chamber and the room the player stands in included, whether doors are open or shut.

- **The lamp.** The player's monitor is the light that moves: a dim pool (at most 55% light, within a tile, fading to nothing at 4.8 tiles) in the **visor's green** (`#0f8`, the eye-lines of the monitor face; red while the player is angry), drawn as a soft colour wash over what it lights. It lights only the room the player is in (a wall's far side is not lit through it).
- **The floodlights.** Emergency floodlights stay on: the 7 that were already in the arena and on the catwalks, plus **8 along the hallway and 5 more across the arena**, placed a tile or two off the walls, clear of every door, the steps and the paths (they reach 90% of the hallway and most of the arena). The exit signs (a small green glow each), the crystal's glow and the Security Room's two monitor-wall lights are on too. All of it is data: `FP_TUT` (js/facilitytutorial.js) and the `floodlight` props in js/facilitymap.js.
- **Enemies.** They are never dimmed (units are not part of the room lighting), and an enemy standing in the dark also gets a faint ember-red glow on the floor under it, which is gone where there is half light or more.
- The message "BLACKOUT — FACILITY POWER IS DOWN / YOUR MONITOR LAMP IS YOUR LIGHT" is said once, a second after the start.

**Rules kept.** A room is lit while the player is in it and dark when they leave it; an open door throws a ground pool; a stairway glows. A blackout adds one condition to each: a room can be lit (and a door can throw its pool) only once it has *power*. `facilityPower(roomId, true)` powers a room (the stair room goes with the hallway), `facilityPower("all", true)` powers everything and ends the blackout, and from then on the ordinary rule applies, exactly as before. `?facilityplay&power=on` starts the facility lit (for development and the ordinary lighting checks).

**Files changed.** js/facilitytutorial.js (new), js/facilitystyle.js (the light update, `_fpTileLight`, `_fpItemLight`, `_fpDarkFill`), js/facilityplay.js (starts the tutorial, ticks the message, the dark amount), js/game.js (the enemy glow under a unit, the lamp's colour after the scene), js/facilitymap.js (13 floodlights), game.html (the script tag), tests/facilityplay.js, this file.

**Tests.** Six checks in tests/facilityplay.js: it starts in a blackout with nothing powered, 20 floodlights (8 or more in the hallway reaching 90% of it, 9 or more in the arena), the exit signs and the designated lights; the player's own room is dark, and the lamp is dim, smooth, follows the player and lights nothing in another room; the lamp is the visor's green (and red when angry) and is drawn only in a blackout; powering one room lights it only while the player is in it, a door throws its pool only from a powered room, powering everything ends the blackout and the ordinary rule returns; enemies get the glow only when hostile, alive and in the dark, and only with the blackout on; the message is said once. The existing lighting, door, wall and speed checks pass unchanged with the blackout off, the real-page walk through two doors passes with it on, the facility data checks pass with the new floodlights, and a 173,000-step random walk through the facility finds no wall or floodlight to walk through.

**Known issues.** The lamp is a circle, not a cone (the player has no facing). The glow under an enemy is a flat ellipse and does not follow the floor's height on the steps. A wall piece is lit as a whole from its middle, so a long wall at the edge of a light changes in one step. The lamp's wash is drawn over the scene (so it also tints a wall standing in front of the player). Phase 3 needs the class decision above.

## Phase 3: the pack (done)

**What it does.** `js/classes.js` holds the three classes as data (Gunslinger, Technomancer, Mutator: name, colour, tagline, rooms, start kit, skills) and `CLASS_RULES`: any class may *carry* another class's gear, only the right class may *use* it (both switches are data). The class picker opens when the tutorial is FINISHED (see "Classes come after the tutorial" below); `?class=technomancer` names a class from the start, for development. `js/inventory.js` holds a data-driven `ITEMS` table (name, category, type, stack, colour, icon, class restriction, description, lore, stats, `use`) and the pack window: press **I** (or the PACK button), tabs (ALL, GEAR, TOOLS, PARTS, SAMPLES), item cells with quantity and a LOCKED chip for gear your class cannot use, an item page with INSPECT (the lore and the numbers) and USE. A new item is one row, an icon and a `use`.

| Item | For | What it really does |
|---|---|---|
| Ammo pack | anyone | +10 rounds in the gun (kept when the gun is full) |
| Flare | Gunslinger | lights the ground where you stand, 6.5 tiles, for 30 seconds (a real light in the blackout) |
| Power cell | Technomancer | overcharges the monitor lamp for a minute; it is also what a relay repair costs (phase 5) |
| Mutagen sample | Mutator | raises the nearest follower within 8 tiles a level (and so toward evolution) |
| Relay kit | anyone | a reusable tool: lets any class repair a relay (phase 5) |

**Supplies.** Nine supply cases stand in the rooms each class works in (`pickups` in js/facilitymap.js; the Armory and Security Room for the Gunslinger, the labs and Teleport Room 1 for the Technomancer, the lab and the Crystal Chamber for the Mutator, the kit in the Chamber). Each shows its item's icon over a beacon, so it can be found in the dark; walking over one takes it (a case that will not fit stays).

## Phase 4: class stations and perks (done)

A **station** is a prop the player can use (`stations` in js/facilitymap.js, `STATION_KINDS` in js/stations.js): walk up and press **E**, tap the plate that appears, or tap the station (the player walks there first). Its ring shows in the class's colour for the class that can use it and dim for everyone else, who are refused with the reason. Each class earns a **perk** (`CLASS_PERKS`, read through `classStat(name, base)` as docs/PLAYER-CLASSES.md plans, so the rest of the game needs no class code):

| Class | Station | Perk | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gunslinger | Armory weapon locker | Service rifle | shots hit 60% harder and 25% faster (the player's shot reads `classStat`) |
| Gunslinger | Security Room console | CCTV sweeps | every enemy in the facility is marked (a diamond, an arrow at the screen's edge) for 25 s, then a 60 s cooldown |
| Technomancer | Teleport Room 1 console | Tuned lamp | the lamp reaches 25% further, an overcharge lasts 50% longer |
| Mutator | a test chamber in the lab | Sequenced samples | costs a sample; afterwards a sample raises a follower two levels |

The pack shows the class, its rank (1, plus one per perk) and the perks.

## Phase 5: the electrical network (done)

Three **relays** (`relays` in js/facilitymap.js) stand broken: the Crystal Chamber's (feeds two sentries that guard the door), the hallway's (powers the hallway and the stair room; feeds a sentry and the charging station) and the lobby's (powers the lobby and the Security Room). A relay is a station: a repair runs with a progress bar over the box and needs **either a skill or a part**, a rule in `CLASS_RULES.relay` (js/classes.js):

- a class with the `repair` skill (the Technomancer): **one power cell**, under two seconds, and it **starts by itself** when they stand at a broken relay with a cell and keep still;
- anyone else: the **relay kit** (found in the Crystal Chamber) and **two power cells**, five seconds, started by tapping the relay. This is the explicit design rule that lets the other classes restore a relay at all (their cells are spent by the relay, they do not use them).

A repair that is walked away from stops (nothing is spent). When a relay comes online: its rooms have power (`facilityPower`: the blackout lifts there, they are lit while you are in them, open doors throw pools), the box turns green with a turning ring, a ring of light and a flash spread from it, the screen says POWER RESTORED, the sentries it feeds shoot the nearest enemy within 9 tiles (8 damage every 50 frames, through the strip's own projectiles), and the hallway's charging station refills your gun (a Technomancer also gets a cell; 90 s cooldown). All three relays do not end the blackout: the other rooms stay dark. Adding an electrical room is a `relays` row and a prop.

## Phase 6: followers and the Crystal defence (done)

`js/tutorial.js` is a short list of objectives (`TUT_STEPS`) with a plate under the FACILITY · EXIT tab: find supplies; earn your class's perk; restore the Crystal Chamber's relay; **recruit** a follower (the roster's + RECRUIT, 30 shards: `recruitFollower`); **post** two followers near the Crystal (the roster's HOLD: `a.stance`); **defend the Crystal**; evolve a follower. It uses the strip's systems and builds no new ones: recruits are `recruitFollower`, levels are `gainXp`, evolution is `evolutionTick` at level 10, the enemies are the strip's ants hunting the Crystal under an alarm.

- The tutorial's followers (the three you start with and every recruit) are **level 9**, and the assault's enemies level 8, so one assault takes a follower to the evolution level.
- **The assault**: a 20 second countdown, then four scouts from the east end of the hallway, then 25 seconds later three strikers. The alarm is up while they live. A wounded enemy that runs is made to press on. A Crystal below 15% is saved: the enemies are called off, it is mended to 60% and the assault comes again. After a minute past the last wave, what is left is called off ("THE REST FLED"). Beating it pays every follower 400 XP (`gainXp`), and the fighting earns more; followers that reach level 10 evolve as they always do.
- The tutorial finishes when a follower has evolved, and says so once.

A scripted run through every step (collect, perk, relay, recruit, hold, the assault, evolution) with a Technomancer and a Gunslinger finishes with two evolved followers and the Crystal untouched at full health.

**Files added or changed in phases 3 to 6.** js/classes.js, js/inventory.js, js/stations.js, js/tutorial.js (new); js/facilitytutorial.js, js/facilityplay.js, js/facilitystyle.js, js/facilitystyleall.js, js/facilitydecor.js (none), js/facility.js, js/facilitygrow.js, js/facilitymap.js (pickups, stations, relays, relay/sentry/charger props), js/game.js, js/ui.js, js/input.js (the pack, the picker, stations, the shot reads `classStat`), game.html, tests/facility.js, tests/facilityplay.js.

**Known issues.** The tutorial's enemies bleed 0.01 HP a frame like every enemy in the strip, so a wounded one that keeps away can die on its own, without giving a kill's XP (the assault's reward covers it). A prop (a floodlight, a sentry) in an enemy's straight path can hold it for a moment until its unstick logic replans; the spawn rows keep clear of them. The relays' sentries use the strip's follower projectiles, so they do not credit XP to anyone. The first assault is tuned by feel (two waves, level 8) and not yet by play-testing with a real player: `TUT` in js/tutorial.js is the table to change.

## Classes come after the tutorial (revision)

**The rule.** The tutorial has no classes. Only when its last objective is done does the class picker open ("TUTORIAL COMPLETE — CHOOSE YOUR CLASS"), and only once a class is chosen do that class's items enter the facility.

- **The tutorial without a class.** Six objectives: find supplies, restore the Crystal Chamber's relay, recruit, post followers, defend the Crystal, evolve a follower. Only five supply cases are in the facility (ammunition in the Armory and the Security Room, power cells in the lab and the lobby, the relay kit in the Crystal Chamber). With no class a relay is repaired with the kit and two power cells, the way every class without the Technomancer's skill does. The class stations show no ring and cannot be used.
- **The picker** opens by itself when the sixth objective (evolve) is done, absorbs taps until a class is chosen, and is never shown again.
- **Class items enter.** Choosing a class gives its start kit and lets that class's cases in: each arrives in a column of light, with the message "SUPPLIES FOR YOUR CLASS HAVE ARRIVED": the Gunslinger's flares in the Armory (2 cases), the Technomancer's cells in Teleport Room 1 (2), the Mutator's samples in the lab and the Crystal Chamber (3). A case for another class never enters (`class` on a `pickups` row in js/facilitymap.js; `facilityClassItemsEnter`).
- **Then the class objective:** go to the class's station and earn its perk (the objective plate turns to the class's colour), and the class's rules (the Technomancer's repair skill and its auto-repair, the Gunslinger's rifle and CCTV, the Mutator's sequencer) apply from then on.

## The gun lesson (revision)

The tutorial teaches the player's gun (js/gunfire.js): a seventh objective, **FIRE YOUR GUN**, comes after posting the followers and before the assault. A stray ant (level 3, plenty of health, grazing) is put in view 4 to 6 tiles away and the player is given training rounds (the gun is topped up to 8). The objective counts the player's own shots (`playerShotsFired`) to three. The hints teach it in two steps: *get close to the ant: the gun takes aim by itself and a round button with the ant's picture appears at the top left* (see Targeting, below); then *click the button as often as you like, or hold it to keep firing; each shot costs a round*. A stray that dies is replaced (up to four times); when the lesson is done the stray is taken away. The assault's hint reminds the player that they can shoot too. The tutorial now has seven objectives: supplies, relay, recruit, post, gun, defend, evolve.

## Class gear (revision)

Each class earns a **signature piece of gear** (`CLASS_SIGNATURE` in js/classes.js: the perk it is). The gear is seen on the player: it is modelled onto the same cube (design/evolutions/forms30.js, Nos. 840–843) and baked to a sheet of its own (img/hd/player_gun.png, player_gun_aim.png, player_mech.png, player_coat.png; `node design/evolutions/bake.js <three-dir> <out> <ids> 186 2.2`, then cropped to the box in `HD_BOX`). js/playergear.js picks the sheet (`playerGearSheet`); js/hdsprites.js draws from it.

- **Gunslinger — the machine gun.** Found in the **Armory** (the weapon locker). The cube still floats; two of its four cables are long tentacles that carry the big belt-fed gun low at its left side. When the weapon is **armed** they pull it up level beside the screen, looking down the sights, and the action frames fire it (recoil and a muzzle flash) for 14 frames after each shot. It turns to the locked enemy. Four times the rate of fire, each round a little softer (`shotCooldown` ×0.25, `shotDamage` ×0.8), and the locker gives four ammo packs.
- **Technomancer — the spider mech.** It has to be **built in the Show Room**: the legs (two cases in Teleport Room 1 and the passage, two each) and the core (in the Show Room) go into the **mech bay**, a seven-second build at the pad (walk away and it stops, nothing is spent). An armoured walker with the cube seated in its cockpit, four thick jointed legs that walk with the player; 40% faster (`playerSpeed` ×1.4) and the lamp reaches 30% further.
- **Mutator — the lab coat and goggles.** Put on by walking **from Teleport Room 1 through the passage into the Experiment Lab**, in that order (any other walk starts again), and **kept for good**. A draped white coat open at the front, the four tentacles hanging out under its hem, with a collar, lapels, pockets, stains and a vial of mutagen, and safety goggles pushed up on the brow. A mutagen sample raises a follower two levels (`mutagenLevels` +1); the sequencer's perk adds one more.

**Known issues.** The gear sheets have no angry (red-eyed) variant: the plain cube's `player_angry` sheet is not used while gear is worn. Each sheet decodes to about 9–10 MB, loaded only when its gear is owned. The sheets are baked at 186 px cells over 4.4 units to hold the mech and the gun inside the cell at every facing.

## Saving, and the two games (revision)

**The facility is the game now.** The page opens the pre-made map and its tutorial by default. The old strip game is the **CLASSIC GAME**: the chip top left (CLASSIC GAME ▸) switches to it, and in the classic game a chip (▶ FACILITY) switches back. The choice is remembered (`conduit_mode` in localStorage); `?classic` in the address forces the classic game for that visit, `?facilityplay` the facility. A switch is a page reload.

**The two games keep separate saves.** The classic game keeps its own keys (`tubecrawler_*`, and the rest), exactly as before, and the facility never writes them: while the facility is played, `Storage.setItem` / `removeItem` let through only `conduit_fp_*` and `conduit_mode` (js/facilityplay.js `FP_STORE_OK`, installed before the page boots). The facility keeps one key, **`conduit_fp_save`** (js/facilitysave.js).

**What the facility saves:** the class and its perks, the pack and the ammunition (and shots fired, which the gun lesson counts), which supply cases have been taken, which relays are online (their rooms are powered again, silently, and the blackout ends as usual when every room has power), how far through the tutorial the player is (the director's step, what it has already said, the followers it started with, the gun lesson's baseline), the followers (the strip's own roster record: level, XP, evolution, traits; if none were saved the three to start are made), the Crystal's health, and where the player stood (if it is still floor). It is written every three seconds while playing and when the page is hidden or closed.

**What it does not save:** the enemies (an assault in progress starts again with its countdown), a repair or a mech build in progress (begun again; nothing was spent), cooldowns, the lamp's overcharge, the stances of the followers. A save that cannot be read, or is of another version, is ignored.

**Items are obtained or not:** a case taken stays taken after a refresh, and the class's own cases are in the facility again once the class is (the ones taken are gone). A game left at the class picker (tutorial done, no class chosen) opens the picker again.

**NEW GAME** (the chip beside CLASSIC GAME) asks first, then throws the save away and opens a fresh facility.

**Tests.** Every suite that loads the real page for the classic game now opens `game.html?classic` (the sandbox's `window.location.search` is `?classic` too).

## The arena's creatures, weapons, and the network (revision)

**The zone tag and the network panel (js/facilitynet.js).** In the facility the tag at the top right (the classic game's "Zone: Home") names the room the player is in, at each moment (a doorway keeps the room just left): Crystal Chamber, Computer Lab (the Front Lobby), Main Hallway, Arena, Armory… The network panel under it is the power grid: **each relay is one zone** (there are now six: the Crystal Chamber, the Hallway, the Lobby, the Lab, the Show Room, the Armory, and between them they feed every room of the facility), and a zone is online once its relay is repaired. The dial's outer ring is the zones online, its inner ring the rooms that have power again, and the middle "online / zones"; the state is OFFLINE, PARTIAL or ONLINE (all six, which ends the blackout). Under the dial: one line that says whether the room you stand in has power (no list of rooms or zones). The three new relays are in the Experiment Lab (the gap in the north bench line; powers the lab, the passage and both teleport rooms), the Show Room (east wall) and the Armory (west wall; powers the Armory, the Arena, the Observation Deck, the Catwalks and the Nest area). More power cells are in the facility to pay for them (twelve in all outside the class cases).

**The arena's creatures (js/facilityspawn.js).** Four are there at the start, each a different kind, and one more arrives every 20 seconds at a spot on the arena floor that is out of the player's sight and away from the others, up to seven alive at once, always the kind there are fewest of: ants, beetles, spiders, scorpions, mantises and moths (the strip's species, in classes of their own: scout, worker, striker, tank). They wander the arena from spot to spot (js/predator.js asks `facilityWanderTarget`: the strip's wander is a lane at y 0 to 3) and hunt only when provoked or when an alarm is up. A creature that dies drops **scrap metal** where it fell (a case for a minute).

**Weapons (js/weapons.js).** One weapon in hand, from a table `WEAPONS`: the **service gun** (everyone), the **machine gun** (the Gunslinger's, from the Armory locker, which now equips it; it can be put away and taken again), and the **laser rifle**, assembled from 15 scrap at the Armory terminal. Each has a level (1 to 3): a level hits 30% harder and fires 12% faster and costs scrap. The laser runs on **fuel cells** (12 charges each; it loads the next by itself): a beam that hits at once, through whatever stands behind the target (60% damage), range 12 tiles, and it says NO FUEL CELLS when it has none. **Fuel cells are in the computer lab (the Front Lobby) once its relay is online**: three cases arrive in columns of light with the relay (a pickup with `needs: "relay_lobby"`). The HUD button and the ring's caption show the weapon's own reading (AMMO n, or LASER ◆ charges +cells).

**The Armory terminal** (the computer desk in the Armory; it needs the Armory relay online) opens a window: **scrap → ammunition** (2 scrap = 1 ammo pack), **equip** a weapon you have, and **upgrade** or **assemble** one. What is in hand is what the player is seen holding: the machine gun and the laser rifle each have their own baked sheets (player_gun*, player_laser*; the laser's glows cyan and its beam flashes on the firing frames), and the mech and the coat keep the player when owned. The weapons, their levels and the rifle's charge are saved with the facility game; scrap drops are not.

## 3D props (revision)

The sentry turrets, the potted plants, the arena's missile truck and liquid tanks, the Experiment Lab's tank and the catwalk bridges are real 3D models, baked to sprites like the player and the followers (docs/ART-PROCESS.md has the bake pipeline for characters; this is its sibling for props).

- **The models** are in `design/props/models/<family>.js` (sentry, plant, truck, tank, bridge): three.js code that builds a group with the footprint's centre at the origin and the floor at y = 0. **One world unit is one tile**: the game draws a tile step as 60 x 30 px (`TILE_W`, `TILE_H`), so the baker's camera is the game's 2:1 iso one at 84.85 px to the unit, and a height of H game pixels is `px(H)` units. `design/props/bake_props.js <three-dir> <families|all>` renders every variant, crops it, packs a sheet per family into `img/props/<family>.png` and writes where everything is (and each variant's *anchor*, the ground point under the model's origin, and its *marks*) to `design/props/out/<family>.json`, then `js/propatlas.js` (all of them as data: the page cannot fetch files).
- **The game** (`js/propsprites.js`) puts the sprite with its anchor at the prop's centre. Plants pick one of four healthy or two dead variants by seed (a third are dead, as before). A sentry has 16 sprites, one for each 22.5 degrees of where its head aims (`it.aim`); its lamp (cyan when fed, a blinking red when not) and muzzle flash are painted on the sprite's `lamp` and `muzzle` marks. **A big prop** (the truck, the tanks) is a set of one-tile cells that the game sorts by depth, and each cell draws the whole sprite clipped to its own tile's column (the diamond on the floor and the same lifted as high as the sprite goes), so a unit walking round it goes in front of the parts that are behind it and behind the parts in front; the floor under each cell is drawn first (a solid tile is not drawn by itself), and a round tank adds sprite-only cells round its rim, so its edge is not cut square.
- **In the dark** the sprite is dimmed the way the flat drawing was: its silhouette in the dark navy the colours fade to, drawn first, then the lit sprite over it at the room's light level (the dimmed pen carries that level in `ctx._k`). A prop whose sheet has not arrived is drawn the old, flat way.
- **The catwalk bridges** are map data: `bridges` in js/facilitymap.js, each `from` a round prop (a tank) `to` a room (the catwalks). `_psBridgeItems` lays a row of one-tile pieces from the tank's east rim to the catwalks' deck edge at the tank's y (a tank end, plain pieces, a deck end), at the arena floor's height; the sprite carries the deck's height (170 px above the arena floor: the arena is 1.8 tiles down, the catwalks 3.2 up, 34 px a tile). They are decoration, not floor: the catwalks stay blocked off.
- **To change one:** edit its model, `node design/props/bake_props.js /tmp/threepkg <family>`, look at it in the game (`?power=on` lights everything), and commit the png and `js/propatlas.js`. Two things the baker's colours need: unlit (`MeshBasicMaterial`) hex colours come out brighter than written (three r128 treats them as linear), so a model's emissive colours should go through a `flat(hex)` helper, as in the truck model.

## Every prop in 3D (revision)

After the first five (sentry, plants, truck, tanks, bridges) **every other prop is a 3D sprite too**, so the facility is one coherent 3D world. `design/props/BRIEF.md` is the brief the modelling was done from (scale, the shared style kit `design/props/models/_style.js`, the footprint and orientation rules, the variant naming) and `design/props/solids.json` lists every prop in the game. The sheets and what is on them:

- **office** (reception desk, the computer desks in five sizes, the armory workbench, consoles in four sizes, server racks), **lounge** (couches in two colours, the table, four beanbags, the ping-pong table, vending machines in three looks and a double, coolers, bins, chairs), **lab** (the lab benches, the fume hood, the two test chambers, carts, generators, toolboxes, speakers, the cable spool), **tech** (teleporter pads in cyan and violet, the charger and the relays each ON and OFF, the mech bay, lockers incl. the armoured gun locker, cabinets, display cases with a crystal, a drone or a headset, the telescope), **cargo** (four crates, two crate stacks, four barrels, the floodlight, the barrier, and the **supply case** the pickups are drawn as).
- A variant is named `kind[.WxH|.rNN][.on|.off][.sN]` and the game picks the most specific one that exists (`js/propsprites.js` `_psResolve`): the size of the prop in tiles, its state (a relay is ON once repaired, the charger once its relay feeds it), and a look chosen by the prop's id. A prop with no variant is drawn the old flat way. A test checks that every prop in the map resolves to a variant.
- **Contact shadows**: the sprites carry no ground shadow, so each cell of a prop gets a soft shadow stamp on the floor under it (`_psShadow`) between the floor and the sprite.
- To change a prop: edit its family's model file, `node design/props/bake_props.js /tmp/threepkg <family>`, look at it (`node design/props/preview.js <family>` for a contact sheet; the game with `?power=on`), commit the png, `design/props/out/<family>.json` and `js/propatlas.js`.

## Architecture shading (revision)

The walls, doors, glass, stairs, rock, scaffold and floors are shaded to match the 3D props (soft values, no bold ink outline). Rules (js/facilitystyle.js `FP_EDGE`, `_fpWallGrad`; js/facilitystyleall.js; js/facilitydecor.js):
- **Walls** (`_fpStyleWall`): flat bands as before, then ONE gradient over the wainscot and the wall face (darker at the floor, a shadow under the chair rail and under the cap), a light chair rail, creases under it, a skirting board, a recessed panel joint at every tile, a soft glow under the room-coloured accent strip, a light bevel on the top edge and a dark contact line at the foot. The slab on top has a light bevel and a soft far edge (no ink).
- **Doors** (`_fpStyleDoor`): brushed-steel leaves with two recessed insets (shadow on top and left, light on bottom and right), a vision panel with a reflection, a hazard-striped lead edge with a seal, screws, a frame with a lit edge, and a lintel with an emissive status strip (the room's colour open, amber shut).
- **Glass** (`_fpStyleGlass`): a pale sill, a tinted pane that is lighter at the top, reflection streaks, a steel rail, a steel mullion post and an accent strip along the sill.
- **Stairs** (`_fpStyleStair`): treads with a lit nosing, soft-shaded risers and a crease where they meet the floor. **Scaffold** (`_fpDrawFrame`, `_fpDrawRail`): round steel (a shaded side, the steel, a lit edge), every kind of line in one stroke. **Rock** (`_fpDrawRock`, `_fpDrawDeckCliff`): a dark crease and a pale lit edge on the lip, a dark foot.
- **Floors** (`_fpDrawOps`, `_fpStyleFloorFor`, `_fpFloorBuild`): an opaque tile base is painted 2 px wide so it covers the fringe of the tile sprite under it (no dark seams); each tile has a lit far edge, a shaded near edge, a faint sheen and a hair of tone variation; the floor is darkened along every wall's foot (`A` marks from `_fpWallEdges`, parallel to the edge). All of it is baked once into the tile's sprite.
- **Cost.** A gradient is made once per `key` (in the piece's own frame, on the real context) and reused by every piece and frame; pieces use screen coordinates computed once, no arrays per point; lines of one colour are one stroke. Measured in the page (best of 25 runs of facilityDrawItems' walls, ms): hallway 4.6 -> 6.1, lobby 1.7 -> 2.7, arena 3.2 -> 3.8, catwalks 2.4 -> 3.8.

## Frame cost (revision)

Measured with the real page in headless Chrome (software rendering, so absolute numbers are pessimistic): `render()` per frame in the lobby / arena / hallway / lab / chamber went from about 14 / 16 / 20 / 15 / 10 ms to about 9 / 10 / 11 / 16 / 8 ms with the changes below (the lab, with its glass walls and many sprites, is the heaviest). What was done and what was tried:

- **Big props are drawn from cached slices, not clips.** A multi-cell prop (the truck, the tanks, a bench, the reception desk) used to draw its whole sprite once per cell, clipped to the cell's tile column; the clip and its restore were a fifth of the frame. Now each cell's column is cut ONCE from the sprite into a small canvas (`_psSlice`, cropped to what is in it) and drawn with a plain `drawImage`.
- **Dim props are one image.** A prop in a dim room was its dark silhouette with the lit sprite over it, two blits. Now: nearly dark (light < 0.1) is the silhouette alone, in between is composed ONCE per light level (a twelfth steps) into a small canvas (`_psDimmed`), full light is the sprite.
- **Contact shadows only on a prop's outline** (an interior cell is under the sprite anyway), and **the prop sheets are loaded as the facility opens** (`facilityPropsPreload`, `decoding = "async"`) so a prop is never seen flat for a moment.
- **Slow devices:** the settings window's GRAPHICS switch (LOW) now also drops the contact shadows and the soft glows in the facility, and the game drops to LOW by itself, once and for the session, if it runs under 28 frames a second for three readings in a row (`facilityPerfTick`).
- **Tried and dropped:** drawing every wall and stair piece once into a bitmap and blitting it. It made no difference to the frame time here (the cost is filling pixels, not the number of polygons) and cost 30 MB, so it is not in. Culling was already exact (300 to 500 items a frame are drawn, all on screen); the floor tiles were already stamped from cached sprites.
- **Memory** after a tour of every room: 23 MB of script heap, about 250 prop slices and 1,700 floor-tile sprites.

## Targeting (revision)

In the facility the gun finds its own target and a round button fires it (js/targeting.js; the classic game keeps the long-press ring):

- **Auto-lock.** Every 8 frames the nearest creature within 9 tiles that the player can see (no wall between: `_fpLos`) becomes the TARGET. The gun is armed without a message (`setPlayerAttackMode(true, true)`) and the red corners close in on it (js/gunfire.js `drawLockReticle`). A creature that is at least 1.6 tiles nearer than the target takes the lock over; the target is dropped (and the gun put away) at 12 tiles, or when it is out of sight, and with nobody in range the gun is put away.
- **The round button** sits at the top left, where the objective's plate ends (on a phone beside the menu hub): the target's picture in a ring that is its health (green, yellow, red), its level on the upper left, a **gun badge** at the lower right (it shows that the button fires; it is red while the trigger is held, amber while the gun cycles, grey with no ammunition) and an **INFO badge** at the upper right. Under it are the creature's name and the ammunition. It pops in when a target is acquired.
- **Fire.** A click on the button is a shot at the locked creature; holding it keeps firing at the gun's own rate, wherever the finger goes (`gunHold.button`). A click made while the gun cycles is kept and fires when it is ready. Out of ammunition the hold ends with one OUT OF AMMO.
- **INFO** opens a card beside the button (level, class, element, health, bite, speed, and what the species and the class are like); tapping it again, or Esc, closes it.
- **Switch.** A tap on another creature locks that one instead and keeps it (up to 18 tiles) even when another is nearer; the tap is only a lock, never a shot. A tap on the locked creature is the gun's (a click is a shot, hold to keep firing, as before).
- **Stow.** The ammunition chip puts the gun away and it stays away while creatures are in range; with none near, auto-lock comes back (a tap on a creature also brings it back).
- **The bottom-left tool** (js/mixtool.js) is only RETREAT, HOLD and COME, with the element selector (WHO) above them.

## NPC flow (revision)

REPORTED: "followers getting stuck behind things and slowing down the frame rate significantly". Measured first (a squad of ten walking every room, hunters sent from random cells, the real assault, in the real page), then fixed:

- **Creatures could not walk north or south.** js/predator.js clamped every predator's y to the strip's four rows (0 to 3) in two places; the facility's `facilityConstrain` then put the step back, leaving only the east-west part. Every creature in the arena, every hunter of the assault, pushed at a wall on its row until the unstick teleported it to a cell centre, a hundred frames later, to do it again. The clamps are off in the facility (the second one, in the separation step, and the blind stagger's); a hunter now walks the route.
- **Wandering and hunting creatures steer themselves** (their goal is their own, not the player or the Crystal), so `facilityReroute` leaves them alone: it was taking a wanderer's step that ran into a prop for a step toward the player across the map and planning a route to him every other frame.
- **Hunters follow the route.** In the facility the hunt heads for the next waypoint of the route to the Crystal (`facilitySteer`), and weaves only where it can see the Crystal (and less: ±17 degrees, not ±40). The reroute that sat behind every unit (`facilityReroute`) only recognised a step that pointed straight (28 degrees) at its goal; a weave of 40 degrees was not recognised and the unit pushed at the wall. It now also reroutes a step that runs into a wall (or a unit that has been blocked for three frames) toward the goal the step points nearest to, within 70 degrees.
- **The arena's creatures never wandered.** The utility AI promoted a calm creature (`isWanderer` is off for them, so that an alarm can send them hunting) to the hunt state each frame, and the strip's CALM rule put it back to wandering later in the same frame, after the wander code had been skipped: they stood where they were spawned, for good. A calm, unprovoked creature in the facility is no longer promoted. Two smaller things in the same code: on arriving at its spot a wanderer set its pause timer every frame it stood there, so it never ran out and it never went on (the pause is now set once, 20 to 60 frames, in the facility; the strip's wander is as it was), and a wander spot is walked to by the route (`facilitySteer`), with a creature shut in a pocket of props (no spot in sight) given one it can walk to (`facilityWanderTarget`).
- **No retreat without a nest.** A wounded creature (under about a third of its health) ran away from its attacker in a straight line to heal at a nest. The facility has none, so it ran into the nearest wall, or, with the hunt score close behind, flipped between running and hunting every few frames and went nowhere (the assault's creatures piled up at the east end of the hallway for a thousand frames). In the facility (`predatorMayRetreat`, js/predator.js) the retreat score is 0 without a nest, a worker's own "a hurt one runs" rule (js/abilities.js) does not send it, and a creature left in the retreat state goes back to wandering (or hunting if it is provoked or the alarm is up): it stands and fights.
- **Routes are kept** (`facilitySteer`, `FP_NAV`). A route was replanned whenever the goal moved to the next cell: a squad following you planned ten routes at once, many times a second, which was most of the stalls. Now a route is replanned only when it no longer works (the next waypoint has dropped out of sight, the goal has moved 2.5 tiles or a third of the way to it, or it is 360 frames old); a goal that creeps is followed by turning the last leg; a goal that stands still (the Crystal) is planned once, so two routes of the same length are no longer a coin toss for a unit that then walks neither. At most three plans a frame (the rest go on with the old route, or straight on, and are planned in the next frames), and a goal with no way to it is searched for at most every 30 frames (and is refused at once when it is on another stretch of floor: the floor is labelled into stretches the first time a route is asked for, `_fpComps`).
- **The floor checks are five times cheaper.** `facilityWalkable`, `facilitySolidAt`, `facilityRoomAt` and `facilityCanStep` (js/facility.js) scanned every wall and solid of the building (132 and 150) for every question; a route is hundreds of them, and every unit asks every frame. They now look at a grid of 4 x 4 tile buckets (`_facIndex`: built once per map, a non-enumerable property, the items in their old order, so the answers are exactly the same, which a test checks over five thousand random probes). A walkable check went from 1.6 to 0.3 microseconds, a line of sight from 47 to 7, a step from 2.4 to 0.5.
- **Measured** (ten followers walking all thirteen rooms, three thousand steps): routes planned 8,900 to 3,200 (the rest are real: the goal moved), time in the planner 3.8 s to 0.5 s, steps over 15 ms 83 to a handful; the assault's creatures reach the Crystal (they stopped 30 tiles short before). One consequence to watch: creatures that could not move now reach and fight the squad, so the fights (and the assault's balance) are real for the first time.

## Follower types (revision)

ASKED: "remove the ability to change the crystal element modulation [...] it should be where you find the followers and they have their type and their element already. Like they have their class already and you just have to evolve them to make them better."

- **The element mix is gone.** The CRYSTAL tab of the battle tool (js/mixtool.js) is now the **ELEMENTS** tab: six hex cells showing which elements are online (lit), which are locked (dark) and, when a cleared wave has earned one, a gold UNLOCK cell (tap it: that is how an earned element is brought online, as before). Tapping a lit cell does nothing and there is no ALL cell. `modulationMask`, `modulationDirty`, the toggles, the RE-MODULATE prompt and the saved mix are removed (an old save with a `modulation` list still loads). Every element online makes followers, an even draw (`modulationPick`, js/clone.js).
- **The recruit crystal is gone too** (the turning prism in the roster that picked the element of a recruit, js/recruitcrystal.js): the player does not choose a follower's type or element.
- **A follower is a type and an element from the start, and keeps them.** The type is its role: **BRAWLER** (aggressive or wild personality), **MAGE** (the code's "sniper": cautious or cunning) or **TECHNICIAN** (the code's "camper": stoic), `FOLLOWER_TYPES` in js/species.js (`personalityForType` picks a personality of the type, so its stats give exactly that role). A follower found in the world is a draw of every personality (js/world.js, as before); **+ RECRUIT** (30 shards, the roster) comes out at the Crystal as an even draw of the three types and an even draw of the elements online, and the toast says what it turned out to be ("MAGE · ICE RECRUITED!"). A follower that dies comes back as the same personality and element. You make a follower better by **evolving** it (level 10, js/evolution.js), not by changing what it is.
- **RE-ROLL keeps the class.** A re-rolled follower (the ring's RE-ROLL, RE-ROLL ALL) is made again with new stats, traits and perk, but stays the same type and the same element (`wantType` and `keepElement`, js/mass.js startFollowerReroll, read by the arrival block in js/npc.js). Nothing lets the player change a follower's type or element.
- The tutorial's RECRUIT step says all this in its hint. The boss "Crystal Modulator" drop (js/drops.js) was already inert (nothing activated it) and is left alone.
