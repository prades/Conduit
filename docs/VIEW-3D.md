# The game in 3D (the 3D view)

**Status:** Stage 1 **implemented** on branch `claude/start-game-project-HSh7Z`, build `2026-11-16a` (its light the game's own dark since `2026-11-16b`); stage 2 **in progress**: the lab's other floors built in `2026-11-16c`, the arena a draft (`2026-11-16d`; the map round it reshaped for fewer nooks in `2026-11-16e`), **behind a switch** (`?engine=3d`). The camera turns a quarter at a time and zooms (`2026-11-17a`). Verified in a headless browser with software WebGL (`tests/view3d.js`). **Not seen on an iPad.** Without the switch the game is exactly as before, and that is what players get until the owner says otherwise.

## 1. The decision

The owner, 2026-11-16, having picked round 2's CRYSTAL LAB 3D (`STYLE-EVOLUTION.md`) and tried it on the test page (`lab3d/`): **"Can we build the full game up to this point?"** Asked which way, they chose **"The real game in 3D"**. The option read:

> Your actual game, drawn in this 3D look, built area by area. Its rules, squad, creatures, cards and saves stay exactly as they are; only the drawing changes. It runs behind a switch while it grows, so today's 2D game keeps working and stays what players get until you say otherwise. This is the "move to real 3D" decision: with the switch on, the game loads the 3D engine. I start now with the real game's lab floor.

This answers the open question "Real 3D or 2.5D?" with real 3D, and supersedes "Stay 2D for now" (2026-11-09) in part: the 2D game stays the default (`DECISION-LOG.md`).

## 2. What it is

- **The same game, drawn in 3D.** The rules, the squad, the creatures, the cards, the saves, the panels and every tap are the game's own and are not touched. Only the world and the bodies in it are drawn by a 3D view, in the chosen look.
- **The switch:** `?engine=3d` in the address (`js/config.js` `VIEW3D_ON`), on `play.html` or `game.html?dev`. Without it nothing of the view is fetched or run.
- **Built area by area.** Where the view has built the room you are in, it draws the frame's world; anywhere else the frame is the 2D game's, as before.
- More in the address, as on the test page: `?ink=0`, `?shadows=0`, `?sharp=1.5` (more pixels; the game's own canvas has one a point). The game's own `?lit=` (its lit room's level, 0.4 to 1) works here too.

## 4. Stage 1: what it draws

### 4.1 The camera
**The game's own camera**: the same angle (2:1, 30° down), the same scale (a tile `TILE_W` px across) and centred on the player, riding the player's floor as the game's does (`js/facilityplay.js` `_fpP`, `_fpCam`). A point of the world is on the screen exactly where the game's drawing puts it (within a pixel, tested), so every 2D drawing laid over the picture lies where it did: bars, plates, effects, marks, the start screen's panels, and every tap.

**Turned and zoomed** (build `2026-11-17a`). The owner, 2026-11-17, asked what next: **"there was so much more in the demo than there is now there was a one key thing of note is the fact that I could rotate the camera and zoom in zoom out"**; asked how to turn, given the 2D still drawn over the picture: **"90° steps"**.
- **A quarter turn at a time**: the camera swings round the vertical to it in 0.4 s. Everything the game lays out on the screen from the world goes through one turn (`js/config.js`: `VIEW_TURN`, `isoSX`, `isoSY` for a place, `isoDX`, `isoDY` for a way, `isoBack` for a tap, `isoDepth` for what is in front): the bars, plates and numbers, the effects, the floor's marks, the taps, the order things are drawn in, and the creatures it still draws in 2D and the way they face. So it lines up in each of the four views; while the camera swings, the 2D over the picture waits (it is wiped, and the floating words and the floor's marks are not drawn).
- **Zoom**, 0.6 to 1.8 times (the agent's range, provisional): the camera and the world's 2D drawing are scaled about the screen's middle together (`js/view3d.js` `view3dWorldBegin`: while the world is drawn, a part that sets its own transform is taken as within the zoom); taps come back through it; zoomed out, more of the world is drawn.
- **The walls that sink** are those in front of the room you are in, and those that could hide someone in it, as the turned camera sees them: each wall piece knows its rooms for each of the four turns (`design/style-evolution/live/floor.js`).
- **The controls**: four round buttons under QUESTS (turn left, turn right, zoom out, zoom in; their place and look are the agent's); on an iPad, two fingers pinched to zoom and twisted to turn: the room goes round with the fingers as they twist (past 8° of twist, so that a pinch does not wobble it; meanwhile the 2D over it waits, as in a swing), and let go it settles on a quarter turn, the next one the way it went once 20° past one, else back (both the agent's, provisional; since `2026-11-17b`, the owner: "It won't allow me to use my fingers to rotate the screen. I have to press the button on the top, which I don't like." Until then a twist turned nothing before 40° and then a quarter at once). The first finger's press becomes the twist's: no tap, hold, shot or lock from it, and a ring its hold opened closes; on a computer, the wheel zooms, `[` and `]` turn, `-` and `=` zoom. While the dialogue's box is up (it hangs under QUESTS too) the buttons are under it; with a panel open over the world (the pack, the clone bay, the armoury, the class picker, the quest box, INFO, Settings) they are not drawn over it, nor take its taps, and fingers, the wheel and the keys leave the camera alone.
- **Only while the 3D view shows**: the 2D game keeps its one angle and scale (outside the rooms the view has built, the turn is the game's own).
- When turned, the rubble a fire technician drills is drawn as its drawn pile (its picture is made for the game's one angle).

### 4.2 The world
- **The rooms** (`view3d/gamemap.js` `VIEW3D_ROOMS`: the eight of the lab's ground floor, then its other floors) read from the running game when the view is made, built as the test page builds them (`design/style-evolution/live/floor.js`, round 2's `chamber.js` for each room's props, wall fittings and floor marks; the props from the game's own models).
- **The walls in front of the room you are in sink** into the floor (the test page's way; the game's 2D walls cut down). **And whatever could hide someone in it** (build `2026-11-16i`; the owner, 2026-11-16: **"Yes, the rock that is obscuring the player should sink in"**): a piece of wall or rock, or a thin wall, sinks with every room it could hide someone standing in, from the camera, as far back as its top climbs on the screen from that room's floor (a rock's skyline at its tallest, from its back corner). Measured on every floor tile of the rooms the view builds: no standing wall or rock covers anyone (`tests/view3d.js`). Before, it covered a body on 4 tiles behind rock and 64 behind plain walls (the arena's south-east corner behind the catwalks' end wall, the Experiment Lab's east side behind Teleport Room 1's). About the same draws (six more groups of pieces that sink together). A wall more than a tile thick (the arena's north wall, two tiles since `2026-11-16e`) sinks all through: a block with a room on its camera side stands in front of the room behind the wall too, straight or diagonally (`design/style-evolution/live/floor.js`, live only; the test page is as it was). With it, a front corner or two of the lab's rooms (the Crystal Chamber's, Teleport Room 1's) now sink with their room.
- **The doors** slide open and shut as the game's do (`facilityDoorsTick`).
- **Every floor at its own height** (`2026-11-16c`): a room's floor, its props and fittings, its doors and windows stand on its level; a staircase's treads each at theirs, with risers. A wall between two floors stands from the lower up a wall's height over the higher, as the game's does, and its stub (when the walls in front of your room sink) over the higher; a group of walls sinks as deep as it is tall. A deck's railing (Teleport Room 2's edge) is a low railing of posts and two rails (its height, 0.62 of a tile, the agent's).
- **The Crystal** turns and bobs in its tank as the game's does, amber when it is hurt, red when it is failing (the game's three bands).

### 4.3 The light
The game's own light (`js/facilitystyle.js`): the room you are in is lit, the others are dark, an open door throws a pool of light, a blackout leaves only the emergency lights. A map of the tiles is written from the game's levels each frame (only when they change is it sent on) and laid on every surface of the world by the tile in front of it.

**More lit up than the 2D game** (build `2026-11-16h`): the owner, 2026-11-16, of the 3D arena shown four ways (its rock back in sand, the room brighter, both, or as it was): **"No, I like the rock color the way it is I think the facilities should be more lit up"**. The room you are in is at full light (the game's lit level counts as full: the look's own palette); a room you are not in is as dark as the room you were in was (the game's night laid on at `darkAmt × (1 − lit)`); a door's pool in between; a blackout as dark as the game's own, since it is play. The 2D game is unchanged. *(Superseded in 3D: dark mode, below.)*

*(Superseded, `2026-11-16h`:)* **As dark as the 2D game** (build `2026-11-16b`): the dark is laid on as the game lays it on, so the room you are in is at the game's lit level, two fifths darker than the look's palette (the owner, 2026-11-09, `FP_LIT`), and a dark room is as dark as the game's. The owner, 2026-11-16, having tried both on an iPad (the room you are in as bright as the chosen pictures, the agent's first choice; and as dark as the game's, then `?dark=game`): **"Definitely liking the dark mode more"**. *(Superseded: the bright room, build `2026-11-16a`, and its `?dark=game`.)*

### 4.4 The bodies
- **The cube, the eggs and the evolved forms** are their own 3D models: the ones their sprites are baked from (`design/evolutions`), built as the bakers build them (`design/evolutions/bake.js`, whose table of jobs goes into the view), at the sprites' scale, turned to the sprites' row and posed for the sprites' frame (`js/hdsprites.js`, `js/evolution.js` `_evoFrame`): idle breaths, walking, signature moves, special blocks, the class gear, red eyes when angry, the evolved forms growing as they molt.
- **The ants** are the ant model at the game's own measures (`js/bugs3d.js`), each class at its width, turned to its heading, lunging forward on a bite with its jaws wide, its legs in step while it walks, every foot down while it stands.
- **A body stands where the game has the unit**: its x and y, on its floor. The 2D drawing put a unit's feet half a tile up the screen from there (the top corner of its tile), so while the view shows, the game draws what goes over a unit (its bars, plate, rings) half a tile lower, with the body (`js/view3d.js` `view3dFeet`; `js/game.js`; `js/input.js` for tapping a follower).
- **Still the game's 2D drawing:** the other creatures, an ant that is a clone, an ally, a nymph or a boss, a form still cracking out of its egg, one under the floor, a ghost, one in smoke form, and anyone standing outside the built rooms.

### 4.5 What is still drawn in 2D, over the picture
- Everything over a unit, the effects, the numbers and words, the panels and cards.
- **The floor's marks that are play** (the evolved forms' pools, fire, acid, the serum's pool, the Frost Patch, the Mage's aura, THORNWHIP's thicket and seedfall, HEXFIRE, the meteor craters, the long-press ring) are the game's own 2D drawing, made into a layer the view lays on its floor, under all that stands on it.
- **The facility's items the view does not build:** pickups, a station's ring, a follower's spot, a stairwell down (the Experiment Lab's to the Cold Storage), the rubble a fire technician drills (on the wall where the view's wall is). **The wall's things it has no model for are not drawn while it shows** (a shelf, a clock, a sign; in the arena the banners, the scoreboard and its bulbs, beams, chains, claws, cracks, moss, veins, ooze drips, lamps, plates, ladders, the two garage doors), nor the arena's runoff grates with their sight glass and mist (build `2026-11-16g`; the owner, 2026-11-16: "a lot of like random signage and props that aren't converted already and look kind of bad ... So try to remove those little bits of texture, and then we'll work on 3D versions after"). The 2D game still draws them all.

### 4.6 Not in stage 1
- The battle view's zoom: off while the 3D view shows.
- Tilting the camera (turning and zoom are built: §4.1).
- A body's flash when it is hit (its bars and numbers show the hit).
- The stairs' floor marks follow the room's floor, not each tread.
- A relay's or charger's look as it powers up (built as "on").
- Props that move or are taken away in play: none in the lab's eight rooms.

## 5. How it works

| Piece | File |
|---|---|
| The switch; fetching three.js and the view behind the start screen; whether a frame is the view's; the floor-marks layer; a unit's offset | `js/view3d.js`, `js/config.js` (`VIEW3D_ON`; the game's canvas see-through with the switch on), `js/init.js` (`_suAssets`) |
| The hooks in the game's frame | `js/game.js` (`clearFrame`, the draw list, `drawFloorTileMarks`), `js/facilityplay.js` (`facilityDrawItems`), `js/draw.js` (the bodies), `js/input.js` (tapping a follower) |
| The game's map, read from the running game | `view3d/gamemap.js` (the test page's exporter uses it too) |
| The drawing: the renderer, the world merged so it is cheap, the ink line, the shadows, an ant's body | `view3d/core.js` (the test page uses it too) |
| The view: camera, light, doors, Crystal, bodies, the floor marks laid on | `view3d/gameview.js` |
| One script of all that, with the forms' and props' models and the bakers' table | `dist/view3d.js`, made by `node tools/view3d.js` (the stamp runs it); never edit it by hand |
| three.js r128 (MIT, `vendor/three.LICENSE.txt`), shared with the test page | `vendor/three.min.js` |

- **The view's own canvas** lies under the game's. While the view shows, the game's frame is cleared to nothing where the world is, and draws no floor tile, wall or prop.
- **A failure never stops the game**: if three.js or the view does not load or throws, the game is drawn in 2D as ever, and `VIEW3D.error` says why.
- **Cost** in the test browser (software WebGL, which says nothing of an iPad): about 420 draws and 350,000 triangles a frame in the Crystal Chamber with its squad, and 380 to 670 in the other rooms; the view's script is 1.6 MB and three.js 0.6 MB, fetched only with the switch on; the view is made in a few seconds behind the start screen.

## 6. Tests

`tests/view3d.js`: the files (the view's script fresh from its sources; every evolved form, the cube and every egg in the bakers' table; three.js r128 with its licence; fetched only through `js/view3d.js` behind the switch; the page loads it before the boot check). Then, in a real browser: **off**, nothing of it is fetched or made and the game's canvas is opaque; **on**, it is made with no error and shows in the Crystal Chamber with a picture that is not blank; **the camera** is the game's projection to the pixel, on the floor, up a wall, at the gem and down the stairs; **the bodies** (the cube, an egg, an evolved form, an ant; a beetle left to the game; what is over a unit drawn with its body); **the world** (a door as open as the game's; light and dark rooms; a blackout; the floor marks laid on; the items kept); **away** in the arena it hides and the frame is the 2D game's; **a failure** leaves the game in 2D. **Turned and zoomed**: in three quarter turns and at three zooms the view's camera and the game's own 2D layout both match a turned and zoomed projection written in the test, to the pixel; a tap there comes back to its tile; nothing standing hides anyone in any of the four turns.
