## 1. The decision

The owner, 2026-11-16, having picked round 2's CRYSTAL LAB 3D (`STYLE-EVOLUTION.md`) and tried it on the test page (`lab3d/`): **"Can we build the full game up to this point?"** Asked which way, they chose **"The real game in 3D"**. The option read:

> Your actual game, drawn in this 3D look, built area by area. Its rules, squad, creatures, cards and saves stay exactly as they are; only the drawing changes. It runs behind a switch while it grows, so today's 2D game keeps working and stays what players get until you say otherwise. This is the "move to real 3D" decision: with the switch on, the game loads the 3D engine. I start now with the real game's lab floor.

This answers the open question "Real 3D or 2.5D?" with real 3D, and supersedes "Stay 2D for now" (2026-11-09) in part: the 2D game stays the default (`DECISION-LOG.md`).

## 2. What it is

- **The same game, drawn in 3D.** The rules, the squad, the creatures, the cards, the saves, the panels and every tap are the game's own and are not touched. Only the world and the bodies in it are drawn by a 3D view, in the chosen look.
- **What an address gets** (build `2026-11-17ct`; the owner, 2026-11-23: "I'd like to make the cards and the 3D the actual game now"; one rule, `js/config.js` `enginePlan`, read by `VIEW3D_ON` and by the card battles):
  - **the plain address over the web** (https, as `game.html`'s own send-on to `play.html` has "the web"): the 3D view, with card battles. That is the game players get.
  - **`?engine=2d`**: the game as it was, drawn in 2D with its fights in real time. The way back, to compare, or for a device that cannot run the view.
  - **`?engine=3d`**: the view, its fights in real time unless `?cards` (which also brings the test cards) or `?battles=cards` is with it. What is spelled out means what it meant before.
  - **`?battles=cards`, `?battles=live`**: the fights, said outright (card battles need the view).
  - **the website's background (`?attract`)** keeps the 2D picture, and **off the web** (the tests, a file on disk, a local server) the plain address is 2D as before: there the players' game is `?engine=3d&battles=cards`.
  - Without the view nothing of it is fetched or run. With it, a player's first load fetches three.js and the view on top of the game's own script: about 0.69 MB more over the wire (149 KB and 543 KB gzipped, beside the game's 775 KB).
  - **Where the view has not built the room, or where it fails** (no WebGL), the frame is the 2D game's and the fights are in real time. At `2026-11-17ct` it has built 19 of 38 rooms; **not built: the Armory, the Ant Nest (14 rooms), the electrical domain (2), the subway (2)** (`tests/engineplan.js` prints the list). Walking into one of those, the picture changes to the 2D game's.
- **Built area by area.** Where the view has built the room you are in, it draws the frame's world; anywhere else the frame is the 2D game's, as before.
- More in the address, as on the test page: `?ink=0`, `?shadows=0`, `?sharp=1.5` (more pixels; the game's own canvas has one a point). The game's own `?lit=` (its lit room's level, 0.4 to 1) works here too.

### 4.1 The camera
*(A card battle, `?cards`, build `2026-11-17d`: the camera eases to the fight, lower, turned and nearer, while the 2D over the picture waits: `CARD-BATTLES.md` §9.)*

**The game's own camera**: the same angle (2:1, 30° down), the same scale (a tile `TILE_W` px across) and centred on the player, riding the player's floor as the game's does (`js/facilityplay.js` `_fpP`, `_fpCam`). A point of the world is on the screen exactly where the game's drawing puts it (within a pixel, tested), so every 2D drawing laid over the picture lies where it did: bars, plates, effects, marks, the start screen's panels, and every tap.

**Turned and zoomed** (build `2026-11-17a`). - **Zoom**, 0.6 to 1.8 times (the agent's range, provisional): the camera and the world's 2D drawing are scaled about the screen's middle together (`js/view3d.js` `view3dWorldBegin`: while the world is drawn, a part that sets its own transform is taken as within the zoom); taps come back through it; zoomed out, more of the world is drawn.
- **The walls that sink** are those in front of the room you are in, and those that could hide someone in it, as the turned camera sees them: each wall piece knows its rooms for each of the four turns (`design/style-evolution/live/floor.js`).
- **The controls**: four round buttons under QUESTS (turn left, turn right, zoom out, zoom in; their place and look are the agent's); on an iPad, **one finger swiped sideways turns** (since `2026-11-17c`; the owner: "let's just do it where it's one finger will allow you to rotate the camera. **How it works** (`view3d/attacks.js`, made into `dist/view3d.js` with the rest of the view):
- **A registry by form.** Each form that has its attacks in 3D has an entry, keyed by the game's form id (`maw`), with its **body's motion** (called after the view poses the body from its sheet's column, it plays the attack's anticipation, strike and follow-through from the game's own state: `attackAnim`, `_chompAt`, the Ultimate's effect) and its **effects**, keyed by the game's own effect types (`elementEffects[].type`: `impact`, `mawchomp`, `abszero`; and `icicle`, an actor's `icicleAttack`). ### 8.2 Approved design

- A perspective camera behind and above the player, looking down at a point a little ahead of them and following that point smoothly as they move.
- The ground bends away from the camera like a small round world ("rolling world"): the farther ahead along the view, the lower it is drawn; the sides stay level.
- It is built behind a switch, in stages; without the switch the game is exactly as today.

### 8.5 How it works (`view3d/lowcam.js`, `view3d/gameview.js`)

- **The knobs** are `LOWCAM` in `view3d/lowcam.js`, all in one place, the drafts' values, **provisional** (open question 3): view 40° (perspective, vertical), pitch 30° down, 9.5 tiles from the aim, the aim 2.2 tiles ahead of the player and 0.55 over their floor (zoomed in, both shrink with the distance, ×1/zoom: at the drafts' 2.2 tiles, zoom 1.8 put the player half off the foot of the picture; zoomed out they stay as they are), the aim following at 6/s (the share of the way closed in a frame is 1 − e^(−6·dt)), curve k = 0.0065 (a vertex d tiles ahead of the player along the camera's level forward is drawn k·d² lower; the drafts' 0.013 until the owner's feedback, §8.14), the visible distance `see` 22 tiles and another room's `seeRoom` 12, long pieces cut to `seg` 1.5 tiles (§8.14).
- **The camera:** `place()` works out the game's turn and the battle's ease as before; with the switch, `placeLow()` gives them to `LOW.place()`. The zoom divides the distance (and, zoomed in, the aim's lead and height: above).
- **The battle's camera is today's** (open question 1): as a battle eases in (`cardBattleCam().k`), the view narrows (40° to 0.05°, geometrically) while the camera pulls back so the picture's height at the aim goes from the follow camera's to today's battle camera's; the pitch, the turn and the aim go to the battle's; the curve goes to nothing. At the full ease it is today's orthographic battle picture to within a pixel (checked). Easing out, the same back.
- **The curve** is in the vertex shaders: every material the view draws is wrapped once (`wrapMat`: the materials three writes, the sprites, and the view's own shaders: the attacks', the fields', the water), new ones as they are made (a body when built, the rest every 30 frames). The shadows' map is drawn from the world unbent and each surface reads it from its unbent place, so a shadow lands on what it falls on.
- **Culling after the bend:** the camera's own frustum test moves each thing's bounding sphere where the bend puts it (three's `Frustum`, patched only with the switch on; the light's frustum is as it was).
- **The ink line:** its depth edges at a slant (`dK` 0.03 instead of 0.008); in a battle's long lens its depth is counted from near the aim (`dOff`), so the line is as today's.
- **The light's shadow square** is laid ahead of the player on the floor the camera sees (on the battle in a battle, as today).
- **The void beyond the map** is today's background colour (`#0d1d26`): B has no sky (open question 4).

Without the switch that maths is the path, unchanged (`ISO_CAM` in `js/config.js` is null for good).

**The two functions** (`view3d/lowcam.js`; asked through `js/view3d.js`):
- **World to screen:** `view3dToScreen(x, y, h)`: where the frame's camera draws the point (x, y) of the world h px of height up, the bend included (`LOW.toScreen`).
- **Screen to world:** `view3dToWorld(sx, sy)`: the camera's ray through that point of the screen met with the bent floor at the height the floor has there (`LOW.toFloor`, a few rounds); null over the horizon.
- `view3dScaleAt(x, y, h)`: the camera's px for one of the 2D's px there (1 at today's camera).

### 8.10 The walls that sink under the low camera (stage 3)

- **The rule kept.** The walls in front of the room you are in sink and leave their stubs, by the camera's quarter turn, as ever (`design/style-evolution/live/floor.js` `hides`, worked out for the game's own camera). The low camera looks the same way round and at the same 30° down, with you in the middle of its view, so that rule still names every wall that covers you: measured with you on every floor tile of the 19 rooms built, in the four turns (13,480 places), no other wall covers your body.
- **Added, from the active camera** (`view3d/gameview.js` `lowSinks`, `coverGroups`): a line from each body in your room (you and the squad; its feet and its middle, 0.15 and 0.6 of a tile up) to where the camera is, bent as the picture is, tried against the standing walls' faces and tops; a wall it passes through sinks too. Judged every 4 frames; the wall eases down in 0.4 s as the others do. With squad bodies two and three tiles to your sides (4,520 places) it found nothing the rule had not already sunk: it is a net under the rule, for the perspective at the picture's sides. A wall it sinks that the rule never sinks in any turn has no stub built for it (it would go wholly; not seen to happen).
- **In a card battle** (the battle's camera at more than half its ease): today's rule alone.
- **The guard** (`tests/view3d.js`): the game's own camera's ("nothing standing hides anyone") is as it was; the low camera's is its own check, by the test's own maths (the body-to-camera line against every standing wall's triangles), in all four turns, and once as it runs with the squad.
- **What still reads badly from the side** (seen in the pictures; nothing changed for it, the walls' heights being an open question):
  - **Walls between the camera and your room that are not "in front of your room".** The camera stands about 8 tiles behind you, often over or behind a neighbouring room; that room's standing walls fill a bottom corner of the picture as large blank faces (the Crystal Chamber turned once, the hallway's end). They cover no body in your room, but they cover floor and props.
  - **Stubs near the camera are big.** A sunk wall's stub in the foreground is drawn large by the perspective, as a dark slab across the bottom of the picture.
  - **Side walls show a long blank inner face,** slanting away; today's camera showed only their tops and a thin face.
  - **A body not judged can be covered:** a creature, or a squad member in another room, behind a standing side wall. Only you and the squad in your room are judged.
  - **At a room's far wall** the camera's aim is past the wall: the top half of the picture is the void.

### 8.11 The cost, and the horizon (stage 4)

**Horizon culling** (`view3d/lowcam.js`; `LOWCAM.horizon`, on). The curve bends the floor down and away, so past a point every floor ahead is under the line from the camera that just grazes the bent floor: hidden by the floor in front. Each frame, in the camera's upright plane:
- **The floor it grazes is the lowest the built world has** (the lowest room's floor, less 1 tile for a channel's or a pit's bed: `gameview.js` `lowBox`, worked out once), not the floor you stand on: from a deck the floors below are in plain sight, and no higher floor hides less.
- **The horizon** is where that line touches the bent floor, `st = sc + √(sc² + hc/k)` ahead of the pivot (the camera `sc` along and `hc` over that floor). A thing wholly under the line past `st` (its bounding sphere, moved and grown as the bend moves it) is left out.
- **The visible distance:** nothing built stands higher than the tallest thing in the world plus 3 tiles (a body in the air, an effect); past `st + √((top − floor)/k)` even that is under the line, so anything farther ahead is left out whole (`H.far`).
- **The 2D follows it:** a place under the horizon with all that stands 1.5 tiles over it is put far off the screen by `isoSX`/`isoSY` (`screenLow`), so no bar, plate or tag is drawn over a place the floor hides.
- Checked in the Crystal Chamber turned once (the test): fewer draws and triangles with it, and the picture differs from the one with everything drawn in no more than 0.05% of its pixels; the horizon and the visible distance within the test's bounds (5 to 40 tiles ahead; under 80).
