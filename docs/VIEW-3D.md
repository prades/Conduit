# The game in 3D (the 3D view)

**Status:** Stage 1 **implemented** on branch `claude/start-game-project-HSh7Z`, build `2026-11-16a` (its light the game's own dark since `2026-11-16b`); stage 2 **in progress**: the lab's other floors built in `2026-11-16c`, the arena a draft (`2026-11-16d`; the map round it reshaped for fewer nooks in `2026-11-16e`), **behind a switch** (`?engine=3d`). The camera turns a quarter at a time and zooms (`2026-11-17a`). Verified in a headless browser with software WebGL (`tests/view3d.js`). **Not seen on an iPad.** Without the switch the game is exactly as before, and that is what players get until the owner says otherwise.

## 1. The decision

The owner, 2026-11-16, having picked round 2's CRYSTAL LAB 3D (`STYLE-EVOLUTION.md`) and tried it on the test page (`lab3d/`): **"Can we build the full game up to this point?"** Asked which way, they chose **"The real game in 3D"**. The option read:

> Your actual game, drawn in this 3D look, built area by area. Its rules, squad, creatures, cards and saves stay exactly as they are; only the drawing changes. It runs behind a switch while it grows, so today's 2D game keeps working and stays what players get until you say otherwise. This is the "move to real 3D" decision: with the switch on, the game loads the 3D engine. I start now with the real game's lab floor.

This answers the open question "Real 3D or 2.5D?" with real 3D, and supersedes "Stay 2D for now" (2026-11-09) in part: the 2D game stays the default (`DECISION-LOG.md`).

## 2. What it is

- **The same game, drawn in 3D.** The rules, the squad, the creatures, the cards, the saves, the panels and every tap are the game's own and are not touched. Only the world and the bodies in it are drawn by a 3D view, in the chosen look.
- **The switch:** `?engine=3d` in the address (`js/config.js` `VIEW3D_ON`), on `play.html` or `game.html?dev`. Without it nothing of the view is fetched or run.
- **Built area by area.** Where the view has built the room you are in, it draws the frame's world; anywhere else the frame is the 2D game's, as before.
- More in the address, as on the test page: `?ink=0`, `?shadows=0`, `?sharp=1.5` (more pixels; the game's own canvas has one a point). The game's own `?lit=` (its lit room's level, 0.4 to 1) works here too.

## 4. Stage 1: what it draws

### 4.1 The camera
*(A card battle, `?cards`, build `2026-11-17d`: the camera eases to the fight, lower, turned and nearer, while the 2D over the picture waits: `CARD-BATTLES.md` §9.)*

**The game's own camera**: the same angle (2:1, 30° down), the same scale (a tile `TILE_W` px across) and centred on the player, riding the player's floor as the game's does (`js/facilityplay.js` `_fpP`, `_fpCam`). A point of the world is on the screen exactly where the game's drawing puts it (within a pixel, tested), so every 2D drawing laid over the picture lies where it did: bars, plates, effects, marks, the start screen's panels, and every tap.

**Turned and zoomed** (build `2026-11-17a`). The owner, 2026-11-17, asked what next: **"there was so much more in the demo than there is now there was a one key thing of note is the fact that I could rotate the camera and zoom in zoom out"**; asked how to turn, given the 2D still drawn over the picture: **"90° steps"**.
- **A quarter turn at a time**: the camera swings round the vertical to it in 0.4 s. Everything the game lays out on the screen from the world goes through one turn (`js/config.js`: `VIEW_TURN`, `isoSX`, `isoSY` for a place, `isoDX`, `isoDY` for a way, `isoBack` for a tap, `isoDepth` for what is in front): the bars, plates and numbers, the effects, the floor's marks, the taps, the order things are drawn in, and the creatures it still draws in 2D and the way they face. So it lines up in each of the four views; while the camera swings, the 2D over the picture waits (it is wiped, and the floating words and the floor's marks are not drawn).
- **Zoom**, 0.6 to 1.8 times (the agent's range, provisional): the camera and the world's 2D drawing are scaled about the screen's middle together (`js/view3d.js` `view3dWorldBegin`: while the world is drawn, a part that sets its own transform is taken as within the zoom); taps come back through it; zoomed out, more of the world is drawn.
- **The walls that sink** are those in front of the room you are in, and those that could hide someone in it, as the turned camera sees them: each wall piece knows its rooms for each of the four turns (`design/style-evolution/live/floor.js`).
- **The controls**: four round buttons under QUESTS (turn left, turn right, zoom out, zoom in; their place and look are the agent's); on an iPad, **one finger swiped sideways turns** (since `2026-11-17c`; the owner: "let's just do it where it's one finger will allow you to rotate the camera. And you swipe it. You swipe whichever direction you want to go. Or opposite, however it's working now"): on a press the game leaves plain (not on a creature or its card's buttons, the gun's shot, the dialogue's box, an aim or a ring), once the finger has gone 22 px more sideways than up or down (the game's own drag: a tap stays a tap, and a hold still opens the ring), the room goes round with it, 0.25° a px, like a turntable: the floor in front goes the way the finger goes (meanwhile the 2D over it waits, as in a swing); let go, it settles on a quarter turn, the next one the way it went once 20° past one, or one on if it was flicked (faster than 0.5 px a ms at the end), else back. 22 px, 0.25°, 20° and 0.5 are the agent's, provisional; the direction can be turned round. **Two fingers pinch to zoom, and only that.** (Before: in `2026-11-17b` two fingers twisted turned the room with them, after the owner found the `2026-11-17a` twist, a quarter at 40°, did nothing: "It won't allow me to use my fingers to rotate the screen. I have to press the button on the top, which I don't like.") On a computer a drag sideways turns as a swipe does, the wheel zooms, `[` and `]` turn, `-` and `=` zoom. While the dialogue's box is up (it hangs under QUESTS too) the buttons are under it; with a panel open over the world (the pack, the clone bay, the armoury, the class picker, the quest box, INFO, Settings) they are not drawn over it, nor take its taps, and fingers, the wheel and the keys leave the camera alone.
- **Only while the 3D view shows**: the 2D game keeps its one angle and scale (outside the rooms the view has built, the turn is the game's own).
- When turned, the rubble a fire technician drills is drawn as its drawn pile (its picture is made for the game's one angle).

### 4.4 The bodies
- **The cube, the eggs and the evolved forms** are their own 3D models: the ones their sprites are baked from (`design/evolutions`), built as the bakers build them (`design/evolutions/bake.js`, whose table of jobs goes into the view), at the sprites' scale, turned to the sprites' row and posed for the sprites' frame (`js/hdsprites.js`, `js/evolution.js` `_evoFrame`): idle breaths, walking, signature moves, special blocks, the class gear, red eyes when angry, the evolved forms growing as they molt.
- **The ants** are the ant model at the game's own measures (`js/bugs3d.js`), each class at its width, turned to its heading, lunging forward on a bite with its jaws wide, its legs in step while it walks, every foot down while it stands.
- **A body stands where the game has the unit**: its x and y, on its floor. The 2D drawing put a unit's feet half a tile up the screen from there (the top corner of its tile), so while the view shows, the game draws what goes over a unit (its bars, plate, rings) half a tile lower, with the body (`js/view3d.js` `view3dFeet`; `js/game.js`; `js/input.js` for tapping a follower).
- **Still the game's 2D drawing:** the other creatures, an ant that is a clone, an ally, a nymph or a boss, a form still cracking out of its egg, one under the floor, a ghost, one in smoke form, and anyone standing outside the built rooms.
- **A body that fails** to build or pose is drawn by the game in 2D, that form alone, and the view goes on (`view3d/gameview.js` `follower`; since `2026-11-17d`). Until then any error turned the whole view off for the rest of the game: a Thunderhead in the squad did (the owner, 2026-11-17: "it only switches back when I have a certain follower, and it doesn't switch back to 3D now that I have the follower"). Its cause: building a form, the view merges the parts that never move into one, and took the merged parts out, so a part found by its place was no longer there (the Thunderhead's shards fade their edges as their group's second part; with the core taken out, there was none). The merged parts of a form are now hidden where they were (`view3d/core.js` `mergeStill`, `keep`); a room's are still taken out (memory). `tests/view3d.js` builds every form and egg as a follower's body is and puts it in every pose the game can ask of it.

### 4.7 The attacks in 3D (stage 5; the sample: the Frost Maw's three)
*(No build yet · branch `claude/battle-anims`. The owner, 2026-11-18: "I need to begin work animating every single attack in the new battle phase"; chosen: followers and enemies, each attack its body's own motion and its effect rebuilt in 3D, the Frost Maw's three first. Every look and number below is the agent's, provisional, until the owner approves the sample.)*

**How it works** (`view3d/attacks.js`, made into `dist/view3d.js` with the rest of the view):
- **A registry by form.** Each form that has its attacks in 3D has an entry, keyed by the game's form id (`maw`), with its **body's motion** (called after the view poses the body from its sheet's column, it plays the attack's anticipation, strike and follow-through from the game's own state: `attackAnim`, `_chompAt`, the Ultimate's effect) and its **effects**, keyed by the game's own effect types (`elementEffects[].type`: `impact`, `mawchomp`, `abszero`; and `icicle`, an actor's `icicleAttack`). Each effect says whether a given effect of the game's is its form's (`claim`) and draws it from its age.
- **Claimed for the frame.** An effect is drawn in 3D only while its maker's body is the view's; the view then claims it for that frame, and the game leaves out its 2D drawing of exactly that effect (`js/view3d.js` `view3dOwnsFx`, asked by `js/elements.js` `drawElementEffects` and, for the Maw's icicles, `js/draw.js`). Every other effect is drawn in 2D as before.
- **Nothing of the game's is written.** The view reads units and effects; what it keeps of an effect (its first life, where the swallowed stood) is in its own memo, keyed by the effect. Each look is worked out from the effect's age, so drawing a frame again changes nothing (tested). An effect the game has ended is played on to its own end in 3D (the snow settling, the spikes sinking) from its memo.
- **A fault** in a form's 3D attacks turns that form's entry off: its effects are the game's 2D drawing again and the view goes on (as a body's, §4.4).
- **Cheap.** Made once and reused: two pools of points (powder snow, with a cold blue rim so it shows on a white floor; and four-pointed glints), one instanced set of ice crystals with the ink line round each (one draw), five rings and three discs on the floor. **Budget** (the agent's): 140 powder points, 90 glints, 64 crystals a frame; **LOW graphics halves them** (`gfxLow`). Nothing made in a frame but a memo when an effect first appears. Nothing of it is fetched or run without `?engine=3d`. **Measured** in the test browser (software WebGL, 1180 × 820, a card battle in the Crystal Chamber; says nothing of an iPad): at their busiest 58 powder points, 12 glints and 24 crystals; the attacks' own work 0.2 to 0.9 ms of a frame (one reading 3 ms with two browsers sharing the machine); 3 more draws (the two point pools and the crystals) and the rings that show. Not measured on an iPad.
- **In a card battle** the game's 2D drawing over the picture is wiped while the battle's camera is up (§4.1, `view3dWorldEnd`), so until now an attack's effects were **not seen at all** in a battle, only the bodies; the 3D ones are drawn by the view and show.

**The Frost Maw's three** (the looks the agent's proposal, in his look: powder snow, ice, the cold blue `#7fd6ff`; the crystals in the game's crystal shape with the look's ink line):
- **His bite** (BASIC ATTACK; the game's ice hit, an `impact` at the enemy, and his `icicleAttack`). *Body:* his sheet's lunge-bite played smoothly: a short wind-back with his mouth opening, thrown forward past the sheet's lunge as his jaws snap shut, squashed as he lands, back with a small wobble (the game's 17 frames; the hit lands as it begins, as before). *Effect:* three frames in, a white snap at the enemy, eight shards of ice thrown out away from him (tumbling, falling, shrinking), a puff of powder and a few glints, 36 frames. The game's 2D icicles out of his body are left out (his jaws are the bite).
- **CHOMP** (the game's `mawchomp`: his walk up, the rear at 0 to 15, the bite at 16). *Body:* his sheet's rear-up played smoothly and eased, a tremble at the top, the drop in two frames, held down 4 frames, squashed, swelling back (the sheet's second block, eased) and a settle of 12 frames. *Effect:* while he rears, frost motes drawn up into his open mouth (the warning); at the slam, a white ring and a cold blue ring of snow thrown out along the floor to the bite's slowing reach (the game's `MAW_SLOW_R`, 1.6 tiles), 28 lumps of snow thrown out and settling, eight ice spikes bursting up round the enemy and three in it (up in 4 frames, held, sinking by 36), a frost haze hanging and fading (62 frames).

## 5. How it works

| Piece | File |
|---|---|
| The switch; fetching three.js and the view behind the start screen; whether a frame is the view's; the floor-marks layer; a unit's offset | `js/view3d.js`, `js/config.js` (`VIEW3D_ON`; the game's canvas see-through with the switch on), `js/init.js` (`_suAssets`) |
| The hooks in the game's frame | `js/game.js` (`clearFrame`, the draw list, `drawFloorTileMarks`), `js/facilityplay.js` (`facilityDrawItems`), `js/draw.js` (the bodies), `js/input.js` (tapping a follower) |
| The game's map, read from the running game | `view3d/gamemap.js` (the test page's exporter uses it too) |
| The drawing: the renderer, the world merged so it is cheap, the ink line, the shadows, an ant's body | `view3d/core.js` (the test page uses it too) |
| The view: camera, light, doors, Crystal, bodies, the floor marks laid on | `view3d/gameview.js` |
| The attacks in 3D (stage 5): the registry by form, the body motions, the effects, their pools (§4.7) | `view3d/attacks.js` (the registry, the pools, the Frost Maw); `view3d/attacks_fire.js` (the fire line); the game's side `js/view3d.js` `view3dOwnsFx`, `js/elements.js`, `js/draw.js` (the icicles, the orbiting fireballs), `js/game.js` (the shots) |
| One script of all that, with the forms' and props' models and the bakers' table | `dist/view3d.js`, made by `node tools/view3d.js` (the stamp runs it); never edit it by hand |
| three.js r128 (MIT, `vendor/three.LICENSE.txt`), shared with the test page | `vendor/three.min.js` |

- **The view's own canvas** lies under the game's. While the view shows, the game's frame is cleared to nothing where the world is, and draws no floor tile, wall or prop.
- **A failure never stops the game**: if three.js or the view does not load or throws, the game is drawn in 2D as ever, and `VIEW3D.error` says why.
- **Cost** in the test browser (software WebGL, which says nothing of an iPad): about 420 draws and 350,000 triangles a frame in the Crystal Chamber with its squad, and 380 to 670 in the other rooms (90 to 160 in the sewers); the view's script is 1.6 MB and three.js 0.6 MB, fetched only with the switch on; the view is made in a few seconds behind the start screen.
