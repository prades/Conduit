# The permanent facility (authored map)

**Status: traced, validated, shown in an inspection map, and THE GAME (`js/facilityplay.js`).** The page opens the facility (js/init.js): its tutorial (docs/TUTORIAL.md), its creatures (see *Play* below) and its one save. There is no other game mode.

Source: the hand-drawn top-down floor plan and `conduit_facility_map_spec.json`. The plan is traced into `js/facilitymap.js` (data only); `js/facility.js` compiles it into collision and
navigation, validates it, and draws the inspection map. Edit the data file to change the facility; no gameplay code names a room or a coordinate.

## Theme
A **rundown facility that used to be a 2010s tech startup's headquarters**. The **Front Lobby** is the big-headquarters entrance: a big sign on its north wall and a reception desk area that leads (through a sliding door past the desk's east end) to the hallway at the back, which looks onto the glass Experiment Lab. The **Show Room** is a glass case showcasing a range of products (blank for now). The **Crystal Chamber** and both **Teleport Rooms** are walled off (solid, no glass). The Crystal Chamber has the **power pack area** (a wall fixture, `power_pack_area`) on its north wall.

## Furniture and glass
- **Computer lab = the Front Lobby:** rows of computer desks (keyboards, amber-screened monitors) along the north wall and server racks (blinking amber/red LEDs) down the west wall and in the north-east corner, beside the reception desk. The lobby was made two tiles taller to fit the rows; the doors, the bathroom door and a walkable aisle all stay clear.
- **Science lab = the Experiment Lab:** a long bench on each of the north and south walls (steel tops with beakers, flasks, a microscope and sample racks), a fume hood with a glass front, two glass test chambers (liquid, bubbles) beside the big tank, and a 2.5-tile aisle between the benches from the passage door to the tank.
- **Glass windows:** a window now draws a solid sill, glass with a reflection streak, a top rail and a mullion post every two tiles. The **Observation Deck** looks down into the arena through glass on its whole east wall (solid to movement, like every window). The **Show Room's west window** (`show_window_west`) names BOTH rooms, as the lab's windows do, so the hallway's own pieces of that edge are glass too: they used to be an opaque wall standing in front of the glass, with a one-tile top running over the show room's floor. The walls, glass and doors are drawn procedurally (`_fpDrawWall`, js/facilitystyle.js); the furniture began as flat drawings (`_fpDrawProp`) and is now 3D models baked to sprites (docs/TUTORIAL.md, "3D props" and "Every prop in 3D").
- Props are data (`props` in `facilitymap.js`: `kind`, `rect` / `circle`): add a piece by adding a line. A new `kind` needs a model in its family (design/props/BRIEF.md) or, failing that, a flat drawing in `_fpDrawProp`.

## Data (`FACILITY_MAP`)
- Coordinates are **sketch pixels** (the photo, origin top-left). `scale.px_per_tile` (20) turns them into world tiles: calibrating is one number. The **main hallway round the lab is 6 tiles wide** (120 px), wide enough to fight in: the lab keeps its size and every other room was pushed outward to make room for it (the passage to Teleport Room 1 is 2 tiles wide and 6 long). The sketch grid is not a tile size.
- `rooms`: explicit polygons with a `level` and a `status` (`traced` / `approximate` / `unconfirmed`). Every edge is a wall unless a CONFIRMED opening is cut into it.
- `props`: solid things in rooms (benches; the arena's **semi-truck carrying three missiles**; and the two circular structures, which are **giant reserves of ooze or liquid**: solid tanks, deliberately small enough to leave walkways: about 3 tiles between them and 5 to the right-hand side, tested).
- The **Nest Area** (a room beyond the arena, kept by its sketch name) is one completely open room; the long double lines in the sketch are its outside edges (its walls), not passages. Its bottom wall is provisional (the sketch stops drawing). Nothing outside every room is walkable. (`outer_path` is still supported by the logic for a separate boundary band, and is `null` now.)
- **Teleport Room 1 / 2** are placeholders for a teleport feature to be added later; no teleport object is in the data.
- **Every door is a sliding access door** (`door_type`); a confirmed opening is one.
- The deck to the right of the arena (the **catwalks**) is `blocked_off_for_now`: not being developed; the player can reach its edge, not enter. A follower can be thrown up onto it by a Kinetic Launcher set up at the arena's **launcher site** (`launchSites`), and comes back down through its broken section, one way (docs/TECHNICIANS.md); nobody walks up.
- **Rubble** (`rubble`): a pile of broken rock in the arena's south rock, on the west half of its straight stretch (its exact middle is on a slant; the east half carries the red banner). It is drawn on the rock's face like the walls' decor (`a`-`b`, js/facilitydecor.js) and a fire technician drills it from `work` (docs/TECHNICIANS.md, the Thermite Drill). Drilled through it is a dark hole, sealed for now: no wall changes and nothing walks in. The validator checks it lies on a wall of its room and its work spot is floor in sight of it.
- `openings`, `connections`, `ramps`: **confirmed only.** All doors are sliding access doors; wall positions are approximate until checked on the inspection map:
  - **Crystal Chamber ↔ Teleport Room 1**, and **Teleport Room 1 ↔ Observation Deck** (the deck is treated as ground level for now, so no ramp is needed);
  - the **Front Lobby** has **two doors to the hallway, one each side of the reception desk**; the **Security Room** (once taken for a bathroom: the room beside the show room) has a door to the lobby and a door to the hallway; it is lined with banks of TV monitors (camera feeds, `monitor_bank` wall decor on the north and west walls), with a console desk, a chair, a run of filing cabinets (three drawers each), a locker, a cooler and a bin;
  - the **Armory** (level -1, the arena's floor) is an **interior** under the **Observation Deck** with two ways in: DOWN the stairs through the stairwell door in an alcove in the deck's west wall, or through the steel door in an alcove in the arena's west cave wall just south of the two garage doors (where the Armory's own door used to be). Inside, two alcoves in its north wall lead back out (see *Interiors and portals* below). The arena's two garage doors stay as scenery;
  - the **glass Show Room** has a door in its west glass onto the hallway;
  - the **arena's door** at its top-left corner onto the hallway, which runs round the lab and ends at a **door into the Crystal Chamber**;
  - the **Teleport Stair Room** (raised, door to Teleport Room 2) and its **curved staircase down to the hallway** (an open stair mouth, no door);
  - the **narrow passage** (`lab_passage`) joining the **Experiment Lab** to **Teleport Room 1** through two doors (the lab's only way in).
  The catwalks (blocked off) and the Nest Area are the only rooms with no door (and the Armory has a portal, not a door).
- **The lab is windowed off:** the Experiment Lab is an enclosed room inside the gridded floor; its walls facing the hallway are `windows` (glass: see-through, solid to movement). Its only way out is the passage to Teleport Room 1. **The nook where the Crystal Chamber, the hallway and the lab passage met is gone:** Teleport Room 1 took it in as a tidy alcove, so the hallway's north-west end is a clean dead end at the chamber's door. The hallway is a **C** (the lab and passage cut the ring open on the west): arena door, east side, north side, chamber door. The hallway is at least 2.5 tiles wide on every side of the lab.
- `candidates`: what the sketch suggests (14 connections, 4 ramps/stairs) with the evidence. They do nothing until moved into the lists above by hand.
- `levels`: ground < raised (observation deck, catwalks). Heights are unknown.
- `external_exits`: the sketch's arrows leaving the map (further dungeons, the front entrance, the "further nests" arrow below the Nest Area) and the BLOCKED PATH. None is a connection.
- `unresolved`: the list of what still needs the user.

## Rules enforced (`facilityValidate`)
no overlapping rooms; nothing outside every room walkable; the arena and the Nest Area have a wall on every edge and agent walks from inside them never leave; a flood of the floor from each room reaches nothing
that no confirmed connection joins; a connection between two levels needs a ramp; an opening with no connection is flagged.
Queries the game uses: `facilityWalkable`, `facilityCanStep`, `facilityMove` (slides along walls), `facilityRoomAt`, `facilityComponents`, `facilityReachable`.

## Inspection map
The **MAP** button on the dock opens an isometric map for looking the facility over (ASKED: "get rid of the facility maps debugging features and just have it for just the facility inspection"; chosen from a pop-up: rooms, names and levels). It is the authored map drawn with the game's own projection on its own canvas (`facilityInspectDraw`, js/facility.js): each room's outline with its name and level (a raised room is lifted by its height; one that is blocked off says so), the arena, and the outer boundary. A drag pans, the wheel or a pinch zooms, **FIT** fits the whole facility on the screen and **✕ CLOSE** puts it away. The builder's debug view is gone: its layer switches (walls, props, collision, doors and the doors nobody has confirmed, elevation, the navigation graph), the readout and the rule check, the PLAY button, **F3** and `?facility`. The validator (`facilityValidate`) stays, and the tests run it.

## Still unconfirmed in the sketch
see `FACILITY_MAP.unresolved`: the scale, every doorway, elevation heights and ramps, what the liquid reserves contain and whether their stairs lead to a rim, the Nest Area's entrances and bottom wall, the room names. The game plays on the snapped copy of the data regardless (below).

## Play (js/facilityplay.js)
The page opens the facility (`facilityPlayStart`); `?facilityplay&power=on` starts it lit (for development and the ordinary lighting checks), `?attract` is the website's background (below). It keeps one save key, `conduit_fp_save` (docs/TUTORIAL.md, "Saving").
- **World:** one floor tile per walkable cell, from the **snapped** copy of the map (`facilityBuild({ snap: true })`: every corner moved onto the tile grid, doors at least two tiles wide; the authored data is never changed).
- **Movement:** a tap finds a path through the doors (A\* over tile centres, smoothed by line of sight); the player follows its waypoints; **every mover** (player, followers, clones, creatures) goes through `facilityConstrain`, so walls, glass, the desk, the truck and the vats hold; followers and clones steer round walls to the next waypoint (`facilitySteer`, hooked into `followPlayer`, `_walkStep` and `_moveToward`). A body keeps 0.3 tiles from a wall.
- **The navigation layer (how the squad moves):** the AI still walks in straight lines toward what it wants; after each unit moves, `facilityReroute` works out what it was heading for (you, the crystal, its job's target, its enemy, a foe or friend near) and, if walls stand between, takes the same step along the planned route instead. Routes are planned with a wider margin (0.45) than the walls enforce (0.3) so a unit never grazes a corner or a door post; a waypoint is passed only when the next one is in sight; a unit that is stuck for 40 frames replans and for 100 is moved onto the nearest open cell of its route. A follower is "near" you only if it can SEE you (a wall between means it keeps walking the route however close it measures), the EMPATHETIC lean is ignored where it would aim behind a wall, and a follower far from you hurries (up to ~4x) so the squad keeps up across the big map. A follower holding a room and a unit on an order of its own do not hurry. The details and the measurements are in docs/TUTORIAL.md, "NPC flow".
- **Drawing:** wall pieces (solid, rock, glass), sliding-door frames, prop cells (desk, semi-truck with its missiles, liquid vats, the lab tank) and the two wall fixtures are depth-sorted into the game's own draw list; a wall between the camera and the player turns see-through.
- **Start:** you and three followers start in the Crystal Chamber beside the Crystal, in a blackout (docs/TUTORIAL.md, phase 2).
- **Creatures:** every hostile creature is made by **`spawnCreature(opts)`** (js/clone.js: `species`, `className`, `x`, `y`, `level`, `state`), the one place a creature is made. The arena's spawner (js/facilityspawn.js `FP_SPAWN`) puts four there at the start, each a different kind, and one more every 1200 frames (20 s) at a spot out of the player's sight, up to seven alive at once, always the kind there are fewest of (ants, beetles, spiders, scorpions, mantises and moths; scouts, workers, strikers and tanks by weight; levels 2 to 4). They wander the arena from spot to spot (`facilityWanderTarget`) and hunt when provoked or when an alarm is up (js/alarm.js). The tutorial's assault spawns its ants the same way (js/tutorial.js). A creature killed pays its worth in crystals, DNA and, in the arena, scrap (js/drops.js; docs/TUTORIAL.md, "The arena's creatures").

## Elevation (drawing only)

`levels` in the map give each level a height in tiles: the arena is sunken (-1.8), the ground is 0, the stair room and Teleport Room 2 are raised (1.6), and the observation deck and catwalks are higher still (3.2). A `ramp` may carry a `zone` (a rectangle, the axis it slopes along, and the two coordinates where the height equals `between_levels[0]` and `[1]`); `facilityHeightAt(x, y)` returns the room's height, or the slope inside a `zone`. A `zone` with `steps: N` is a little staircase: the height is quantised into N flat treads, and each tread tile draws a prism from the lower floor up to the tread (so the risers show). The arena door has 5 steps down, the deck door 5 steps up; the curved stair room staircase stays smooth. Every connection across levels names its ramp (validated), and the validator rejects a level with no height or a bad `zone`.

Movement stays flat. Only drawing is lifted: floors, actors and facility items rise by `height * 34 px`, the camera rides the player's floor, taps are corrected for the lift (`facilityPickLift`), and a wall is drawn up to the higher of the two floors it separates. All lift hooks are guarded by `facilityPlay`.

Known limits: effects, projectiles and health bars still use the flat projection (they sit a little off on a sloped floor).

## The dressing (floors, decor, furniture)

The rooms are filled out so the facility does not read as a hollow shell. Everything here is data in `js/facilitymap.js` (`floors`, `decor`, and more `props`), compiled and validated by `js/facility.js`, and drawn by `js/facilitydecor.js`. It is paint and light plus solid furniture: nothing else about movement changes.

- **`floors`**: each room's floor covering (carpet, plate, tile, labtile, concrete, grate, polished, wood, sand), painted over the game's floor tile by the tile's own draw (`facilityDrawFloorOn`), so a unit standing on a tile is always drawn over it. A tile's static marks are painted once into a small sprite and stamped; steady lights are baked in, and only flickering lights are painted live.
- **`decor`, on the floor**: `rug`, `hazard` stripes, `lane` lines, `arrows`, `cables`, `ring`, `stain` / `puddle` / `scorch`, `papers` / `debris`, `light` pools, `tint`. Floor decor is not snapped to the grid.
- **`decor`, on a wall**: `poster`, `vent`, `pipe`, `wall_light`, `screen`, `board`, `whiteboard`, `exit_sign`, `clock`, `graffiti`, `panel`, `banner`, `mirror`, `shelf`, `sign`, `hazard_sign`, `cable_run`, `window_blind`, and the rock / steel kinds `crack`, `vein`, `beam`, `chain`, `claw`, `ooze_drip`, `lamp`, `ladder`, `scoreboard`, `plate`, `moss`, `bulbs`. The camera sees a wall's +y / +x face, so a piece of wall decor names the room on THAT side (the lobby's south wall faces the hallway). The validator checks the side, that it is on a real solid wall (not glass or a gap), and that it is not across a door (an exit sign, pipe, banner, light or sign may be). Wall decor sorts just after the wall pieces under it (`depth`).
- **Furniture** (solid, in `props`): plant, vending, cooler, couch, table, pingpong, crate, crate_stack, barrel, locker, cabinet, bin, sink, stall, pallet, barrier, generator, console, display_case, telescope, floodlight, teleporter, beanbag, cart, desk, rail. Small pieces draw the floor under them (their tile is solid, so the game draws none).
- **Authoring.** Positions are sketch pixels like everything else; on the 20 px grid a tile cell k is [20k-10, 20k+10]. The dressing is authored on the snapped tile grid the game plays on, so the decor checks run on the snapped copy (`facilityValidateDressing`). A piece that stands in an aisle or a door's clear space fails the existing walkability and doorway tests, and a flood from the crystal must still reach nearly every floor tile of every room.
- **What is placeholder:** the products in the show room's display cases (a coloured glow), the wording on signs (a few bright bars), the poster art, and the lobby sign.

### The arena's walls and the catwalks

The arena's rock edge is diagonal on the tile grid, so decor on it is given on the wall line itself (not snapped); the validator takes the camera-facing side of any wall (towards larger x + y) and checks the room is there. The north rock faces the arena and carries moss, cracks, glowing veins, claw marks, chains, a timber support, caged lamps, string lights and the arena scoreboard. The south and east rock face away from the arena, but the camera sees that outer face in front of the arena, so it is dressed too: set `outside: true` and the validator checks the room is BEHIND the wall instead. The catwalks are scaffolding, not a room (see below): they carry painted lanes and arrows, light, a control booth (consoles, a chair), PA speakers, floodlights, cable drums and a generator on the deck, but no wall decor.

### Floor under the walls, and walls that do not fade

- **No holes.** Floor tiles are drawn only for walkable cells, so where a wall met the floor on a diagonal (the arena's rock) a black gap showed. `_fpFillCells` (js/facilitydecor.js) adds drawing-only FILL cells: any cell a room even partly covers gets that room's floor, cut to the room's outline (`clip`: past a rock edge the camera sees side-on nothing would hide it, and the Cold Storage's pale floor showed as white teeth in the void); and the pocket of void behind the arena's north rock (between the rock teeth and the hallway, up to 3 tiles) gets the arena's floor. The void beyond the south and east rock is NOT filled: it is in front of those walls. Fill cells are never walkable, are drawn as `fill` items sorted just before the walls (`depth`), and carry the room's floor marks. A wall whose camera-facing side looks onto a lower filled floor reaches down to it (`drop`, the skirt).
- **Walls never turn translucent.** A wall in front of the player used to fade out. It is now cut down, opaque, to a low parapet as the player comes within about 3 tiles (`_fpCut`: 0 beyond 3.2 tiles or behind the player, 1 within 2), so nothing behind it is hidden. Wall decor and fixtures on a wall that is cut down are not drawn while it is. Glass is left as it is.

### Rock formations; walls never react (supersedes the parapet and tall-rock rules above)

- **Walls do not react to the player.** They never fade, shrink or cut down when the player comes close. (An earlier version faded them, then cut them to a parapet, then towered the rock; all removed. A wall in front of the player can hide them, and that is accepted.) The floor-fill and skirt rules above still apply.
- **Rock** (`_fpDrawRock`) is a brown face with strata, boulders and cracks, and a ragged rocky top edge with a lighter lip. **Back rock** (the camera sees the arena in front of it: the north rock) stands 120-170 px, its top in shadow as if it overhangs. **Front rock** (the arena is behind it: the south and east rock) is lower, 70-130 px, and the rock carries on DOWN below its base, fading to black, so the void beyond it is rock too. Heights come from the position only (`_fpRockHeights`), so the skyline is ragged but identical every frame and for every viewer.
- **The cap (a burrow).** Back rock has a 3-D top: a flat rock roof running back, away from the camera, from the rocky lip to the wall behind it (`it.cap`, found in `_fpRockSides` by marching back until a room is reached: the hallway's south wall for the north teeth). Its far edge meets that wall at the wall's top, so the pocket behind the rock reads as covered, a burrow; a thin spur with the arena on both sides gets a short cap. Front rock has no cap.
- **Caps all round.** Front rock (the south and east rock) has a cap too: a 1-tile-thick rock roof running back toward the arena from its craggy lip, with the same pits and a dark seam on the inner edge. Every rock wall piece has a cap.

### The catwalks are scaffolding

The catwalks are not a room but an open steel deck at the top of the arena's east side, on legs. The map keeps their polygon (and so the collision) and marks the room `structure: "scaffold"`; the drawing then treats it as scaffolding (`_fpScaffoldMarks`, `_fpDrawRail`, `_fpDrawFrame` in js/facilitydecor.js):
- each wall piece of the catwalks is drawn as a deck edge: a fascia under the floor, a kickboard, a post every tile and two rails (yellow top rail), not a solid wall; so there is no wall decor on them (it was removed);
- legs: an open frame (posts every two tiles, beams, X braces) runs down from every deck edge to the arena floor, except the west edge, which the arena's own `railing_wall` carries: that wall is drawn as the same open frame from the arena floor up to the deck, so you see through it;
- the deck's floor, lanes, light and furniture are unchanged. The catwalks are still blocked off for now.

### The deck's cliff and the rock beside it

The observation deck is cut into the rock: the arena's own pane of the deck window (the lower glass the arena owns, seen below the deck's glass) is drawn as a THICK ROCK FACE (`_fpDrawDeckCliff`), from the arena floor up to the underside of the deck, with strata, boulders, a crack and a lit lip under the deck. The arena rock at each end of the window rises to the deck's wall top (`it.dk`, tapering back within 1.5 tiles), so there is no gap between the deck and the rock.

The cliff face is a continuous cavern wall of stacked rock ledges (strata). Every ledge swells and thins along the wall (smooth noise of the position along it, shared by all the wall's pieces so the rock runs on unbroken), its lit top, shaded face and shaded underside giving the relief; fissures, a hanging tooth under the deck's lip and the odd rock at the foot finish it. No outlines except the lip under the deck. (Two earlier attempts, outlined faceted lumps, read as brown crystals.)

### Speed

The hallway draws about twice the wall pieces of the arena, so it was the slow place. Walls are now drawn with flat fills and no outline pass (each band runs a hair past both ends so neighbours overlap), one fewer composited glow stroke per piece, per-room colour strings built once, and a wall whose real extent (`_fpWallBounds`) cannot touch the screen is not drawn at all (`_fpWallOnScreen`, called from `facilityDrawItems`). Posters and signs in a dark room are skipped. Measured in a software-rendered browser (a rough guide only): the hallway went from about 12.7 to 16 frames a second, the lobby from 20.7 to 25. A per-piece sprite cache was tried and removed: it was no faster and used about 100 MB. Wall pieces stay 0.5 tile long on purpose: longer ones sort wrongly against units standing at a wall.

### Heights

Walls stand `FP_WALL_H` = 122 px (they were 74); doors stand `FP_DOOR_H` = 108 px, about two tiles, with the wall continuing above them as a header (three bands and the ink line, drawn by the door). The header is the WALL's, not the door's: a door is lit from both of its rooms so it can be found, but its header is drawn with the light of the room that shows the wall's face (`lroom`, the same rule the wall pieces beside it use) plus the pool an open door throws, so it is never a bright plank over a dark wall (`_fpWithLight`, `_fpWallLightAt`). Decor that hangs near the top of a wall (pipes, wall lights, exit signs, banners, bulbs, cables, chains, drips, cracks, blinds, lamps, clocks, vents) rises with it (`FP_HIGH_DECOR`, `FP_WALL_X`); the rock stands the same amount higher. Posts, doors and walls all read their height from these constants, so one number changes them.

### Thick walls: the building is spaced out (js/facilitygrow.js)

`js/facilitymap.js` is the sketch layout, where rooms touch and a wall is a line. `js/facilitygrow.js` runs once at load, before the map is compiled, and builds it: rooms are slid apart (the `SHIFT` table, in tiles) so that every solid wall between two rooms is a band of solid ONE tile thick; every room keeps its interior size (the hallway is still six tiles wide), so the facility is a little bigger. The hallway, the lab and the passage are the hub and do not move; Teleport Room 1 loses the nub it had beside the lab, the passage is a tile shorter, and the stair room is a row shorter (so no stub of wall stands beside the steps). A door between two rooms is a one-tile cut through the band into one room's outline (`tunnel()`), so the existing door logic is unchanged. Glass and the open stair mouth stay a thin line. Props, decor, fixtures, windows and ramps move with their room; the doors and the two flights of steps are re-placed by hand in the table.

Drawing: each styled wall piece draws a slab over its band (`_fpWallTop`, `FP_WALL_T` = 1 tile): a north or west wall's slab runs away from the camera, a south or east wall's towards it, so a band between two rooms is covered twice. A south/east piece whose band has another room's wall on the far side draws only its slab (`nbBand`): the neighbour's own face stands in front. A door's header slab covers its cut. A door in glass (`thin`: both ends of its opening are on a window; only the show room's door) has no cut and no thick top: the glass beside it has a rail and nothing more, so its header is a band at the rail's height (a thick slab there floated over the hallway like a plank, joined to nothing).

## Interiors and portals (js/facilityportals.js)

ASKED: "little rooms you can go into that load in [...] a room under the observation deck, and that would be the armory, through a doorway that you have to go into, and it loads in a new part of the map."

- A flat plan cannot put one room under another, so a room that lies **under or inside** another is an **interior**: `interiors` in js/facilitymap.js (`{ id, under, offset }`). The data draws it where it belongs (the Armory's polygon is still under the deck, so it reads as "under the deck"); the building pass (js/facilitygrow.js) carries it `offset` sketch pixels away (the Armory 64 tiles east, the Cold Storage 64 tiles west, the Sewers 64 west and 64 south: each a part of the map of its own, with nothing in sight of it, even on a wide screen). It has no door onto any other room and no path from anywhere else.
- A **portal** (`portals`, in pairs; `kind` is `stairs_down`, `stairs_up` or `door`: stairs are drawn as a stairwell door on dark steps, a `door` as a steel sliding door with `flat: true` on its wall decor) is its doorway: `rect` the alcove's floor (two tiles by one: an alcove cut into the room's wall, so the room's outline carries it), `arrive` a spot in front of it where you are put when you come out of the OTHER end, `to` the partner, `label` what the other end is (shown over the doorway as "▼ ARMORY" / "▲ OBSERVATION DECK" when you are near). A `portal_door` wall decor (a steel stairwell door standing ajar on dark steps, a lit chevron over it, a hazard stripe at its foot: js/facilitystyleall.js) closes the alcove. The pair also has a connection (`portal:` instead of `opening:`), so the two rooms count as joined for the validator; the stairs are not a ramp, so a portal may join two levels. A portal with `well: true` is a STAIRWELL in the floor instead of an alcove (the Experiment Lab's down to the Cold Storage, the Cold Storage's flight up): its `rect` is a patch of the room's floor, and a `stairwell` floor decor over the same rectangle draws it (`_fpDrawStairwell`: going down, an opening with its treads dropping out of sight, a cold glow, a hazard lip and a rail round three sides; going up, a flight standing on the floor up to a dark doorway). A `portal_door` with `cold: true` is the Cold Storage's freezer door (`_fpColdDoor`), with `culvert: true` the Sewers' culvert (`_fpCulvertDoor`, js/facilitysewers.js).
- **Walking into the alcove goes through it** (js/facilityportals.js): the screen fades to black (14 frames; nobody is told to walk meanwhile), you are put in front of the other doorway with the camera snapped there, the room's name and LOADING show while it is black (14 frames), and it fades back (18). The doorway at the arrival end does not take you straight back for 70 frames. The room tag follows.
- **The squad comes too.** Followers that are following you (no post, no order) and within 16 tiles are put round you at once. Any other follower on a different stretch of floor than yours walks to the doorway that comes out beside you and goes through it (`_fpPortalGoal`, which `followPlayer` in js/npc.js walks to; scanned every 15 frames, `_fpComps` says which stretch each is on). Creatures cannot use a portal.
- **An interior can have several doorways** (the Armory has two pairs: the deck's stairs and the arena's door). Each is its own pair, with its own connection; a way out leads back to the doorway it is paired with, so you leave by the door you came in by. A follower walks to the doorway nearest it that comes out on your floor.
- **Rules enforced** (`facilityValidate`): portals come in pairs, each in its own room with a doorway on that room's floor and an arrival spot in it; the pair has a connection; an interior has no opening onto another room and at least one portal; the furniture test reaches an interior from its own doorway.

## The website's background (js/attract.js)
`index.html` (the landing page) has the facility running behind it: `game.html?attract`, in a fixed iframe that takes no clicks, under a dark scrim. ASKED: "it loads in the map for conduit and it keeps it all dark or dimly lit and has the evolved followers every now and again just rummaging through and searching the abandoned halls". `?attract` is the facility with nobody playing it:
- **Nothing is kept.** No storage is read or written (`_fpNoStorage` refuses every key, so a visitor's own save is untouched), `facilitySaveLoad()` is null, `_fp.saveReady` is false. No tutorial director, class picker, pickups, stations, portals, assault, creatures (the arena's spawner is off) or default squad.
- **Dim.** No blackout; `attractRoomLight` sets every room to `ATTRACT.lit.base`, the camera's room a little higher, a room with a searcher in it between; some rooms' lamps breathe and stutter. `_fpDarkAmt()` is `ATTRACT.dark`. Each searcher carries a small pool of floor light (`attractPools`).
- **The camera** is the player, hidden (`drawList` leaves it out): it glides between rooms (`ATTRACT.cam`), stays still while a searcher walks in to where it is looking, follows one that has come into view, and stays where it is while one leaves.
- **The searchers** are EVOLVED followers (any of the forms, level 10, made with `spawnFollowerAtCrystal` then evolved at once), one at a time and sometimes two, every `ATTRACT.gap` frames. One walks in from out of the picture by a short route, goes through 3 to 5 stops (a spot beside a prop most of the time, else a spot of a room), stands at each turning its head (longer beside a prop), then walks out of the picture and is removed (fading, if it reaches its goal in sight). They walk by ordinary `move` jobs, so the navigation layer (js/facilityplay.js) routes them. A form's sheet is shown only once it has arrived over the network (one at a time, as wanted), so no egg is ever drawn.
- **Cheap.** LOW graphics, the canvas no bigger than about `ATTRACT.pixels` (scaled up by the browser), a frame drawn at most every 30 ms while the game still steps at 60 (`attractSkip`), no interface drawn, and nothing at all for a visitor who prefers reduced motion or has a data saver (`index.html` keeps its dark gradient). The page loads the frame after its own `load`, fades it in when it posts `{conduit: "attract-ready"}`, and drops it if that never comes.
- Tests: `tests/attract.js` (the flag, every hook, the page, then the real page stepped for 150 seconds of game).

## Steps with no picture (frame rate on a slow device)
`gameLoop` (js/game.js) keeps the game at 60 steps a second; a device that draws 15 frames a second owes four steps a refresh, and all but the last are run with **no picture** (`_simStep(false)`, `_simGhost`). They used to build and draw the whole facility (every floor tile, wall, door and prop) into a null context: ~7 ms each, three of them a frame, which lengthened the frame and so owed more of them. **A step with no picture now builds no tile list and no item list** (`const _fpGhost` in `render()`, an early return in `facilityDrawItems`, no cable or ground-pool layout). What stays, because logic lives there: the camera height (`_fpCam`, read by input and targeting), the doors' easing (`facilityDoorsTick`, moved out of `_fpStyleDoor` so it runs once a *step*, for every door), the room light (`_fpLightUpdate`) and the facility's tick (`facilityTutorialTick`). `tests/ghost.js` proves it: two copies of the facility run in lock step from one seed, one with a normal `render()` every step and one with `_simStep(false)` (and a 60 Hz against a 15 Hz `gameLoop`), in the dark, lit with a fight, and as the website's background, and every module-level variable is compared (only the draw-only caches are allow-listed, each checked for its readers).
The Settings PERFORMANCE readout splits a frame: logic, draw, catch-up and the browser's own time, the steps a frame, the canvas size, and whether the facility is LIT or DARK. It also shows how the page started (when the clock began, the time of each stage, the sheets) and the spread of the last 120 gaps between pictures: docs/STARTUP.md. The clock itself is started once, by `gameLoopStart` (js/game.js), after the start screen's work is done (js/init.js).
