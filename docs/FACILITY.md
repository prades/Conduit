# The permanent facility (authored map)

**Status: traced, validated, shown in an inspection map, and THE GAME (`js/facilityplay.js`).** The page opens the facility (js/init.js): its tutorial (docs/TUTORIAL.md), its creatures (see *Play* below) and its one save. There is no other game mode.

Source: the hand-drawn top-down floor plan and `conduit_facility_map_spec.json`. The plan is traced into `js/facilitymap.js` (data only); `js/facility.js` compiles it into collision and
navigation, validates it, and draws the inspection map. Edit the data file to change the facility; no gameplay code names a room or a coordinate.

## Theme
A **rundown facility that used to be a 2010s tech startup's headquarters**. The **Front Lobby** is the big-headquarters entrance: a big sign on its north wall and a reception desk area that leads (through a sliding door past the desk's east end) to the hallway at the back, which looks onto the glass Experiment Lab. The **Show Room** is a glass case showcasing a range of products (blank for now). The **Crystal Chamber** and both **Teleport Rooms** are walled off (solid, no glass). The Crystal Chamber has the **power pack area** (a wall fixture, `power_pack_area`) on its north wall.

## Furniture and glass
- **Computer lab = the Front Lobby:** rows of computer desks (keyboards, amber-screened monitors) along the north wall and server racks (blinking amber/red LEDs) down the west wall and in the north-east corner, beside the reception desk. The lobby was made two tiles taller to fit the rows; the doors, the bathroom door and a walkable aisle all stay clear.
- **Science lab = the Experiment Lab:** a long bench on each of the north and south walls (steel tops with beakers, flasks, a microscope and sample racks), a fume hood with a glass front, two glass test chambers (liquid, bubbles) beside the big tank, and a 2.5-tile aisle between the benches from the passage door to the tank.
- **Glass windows:** a window now draws a solid sill, glass with a reflection streak, a top rail and a mullion post every two tiles. The **Observation Deck** looks down into the arena through glass on its whole east wall (solid to movement, like every window). The **Show Room's west window** (`show_window_west`) names BOTH rooms, as the lab's windows do, so the hallway's own pieces of that edge are glass too: they used to be an opaque wall standing in front of the glass, with a one-tile top running over the show room's floor. The walls, glass and doors are drawn procedurally (`_fpDrawWall`, js/facilitystyle.js); the furniture began as flat drawings (`_fpDrawProp`) and is now 3D models baked to sprites (docs/TUTORIAL.md, "3D props" and "Every prop in 3D").
- Props are data (`props` in `facilitymap.js`: `kind`, `rect` / `circle`): add a piece by adding a line. A new `kind` needs a model in its family (design/props/BRIEF.md) or, failing that, a flat drawing in `_fpDrawProp`.

## Data (`FACILITY_MAP`)

The **main hallway round the lab is 6 tiles wide** (120 px), wide enough to fight in: the lab keeps its size and every other room was pushed outward to make room for it (the passage to Teleport Room 1 is 2 tiles wide and 6 long). The sketch grid is not a tile size.
- `rooms`: explicit polygons with a `level` and a `status` (`traced` / `approximate` / `unconfirmed`). Every edge is a wall unless a CONFIRMED opening is cut into it.
- `props`: solid things in rooms (benches; the arena's **semi-truck carrying three missiles**; and the two circular structures, which are **giant reserves of ooze or liquid**: solid tanks, deliberately small enough to leave walkways: about 3 tiles between them and 5 to the right-hand side, tested).
- The **Ant Nest** (`nest_area`; build 2026-11-07ll, docs/NEST.md) is an **interior** beyond the arena's south rock: a big egg cavern, sixteen tiles by twelve, reached through the hole a fire technician drills in the arena's south rubble (see "Interiors and portals"). It replaced the sketch's **Nest Area** there, one completely open room nobody could reach, its bottom wall a guess (superseded: the owner placed the nest beyond the rubble, 2026-11-07). Nothing outside every room is walkable. (`outer_path` is still supported by the logic for a separate boundary band, and is `null` now.)

## Rules enforced (`facilityValidate`)
no overlapping rooms; nothing outside every room walkable; the arena has a wall on every edge and agent walks from inside it never leave (the Nest Area was walked too until it became the Ant Nest, an interior); a flood of the floor from each room reaches nothing
that no confirmed connection joins; a connection between two levels needs a ramp; an opening with no connection is flagged.
Queries the game uses: `facilityWalkable`, `facilityCanStep`, `facilityMove` (slides along walls), `facilityRoomAt`, `facilityComponents`, `facilityReachable`.

## Inspection map
The **MAP** button on the dock opens an isometric map for looking the facility over (ASKED: "get rid of the facility maps debugging features and just have it for just the facility inspection"; chosen from a pop-up: rooms, names and levels). It is the authored map drawn with the game's own projection on its own canvas (`facilityInspectDraw`, js/facility.js): each room's outline with its name and level (a raised room is lifted by its height; one that is blocked off says so), the arena, and the outer boundary. A drag pans, the wheel or a pinch zooms, **FIT** fits the whole facility on the screen and **✕ CLOSE** puts it away. The builder's debug view is gone: its layer switches (walls, props, collision, doors and the doors nobody has confirmed, elevation, the navigation graph), the readout and the rule check, the PLAY button, **F3** and `?facility`. The validator (`facilityValidate`) stays, and the tests run it.

## Play (js/facilityplay.js)
The page opens the facility (`facilityPlayStart`); `?facilityplay&power=on` starts it lit (for development and the ordinary lighting checks), `?attract` is the website's background (below), and `?fluxlab` starts the game in the Flux test room (build `2026-11-06g`: a room of its own for trying FLUX MOVE, an interior under the Crystal Chamber reached by a stairwell in its floor; the room and the stairwell are in the map only with the flag; no tutorial; nothing read from or written to storage; js/fluxlab.js, docs/FLUX-MOVEMENT.md §9.1). It keeps one save key, `conduit_fp_save` (docs/TUTORIAL.md, "Saving").
- **World:** one floor tile per walkable cell, from the **snapped** copy of the map (`facilityBuild({ snap: true })`: every corner moved onto the tile grid, doors at least two tiles wide; the authored data is never changed).
- **Where a tap may send you (2026-11-01):** the room you are in, and through a doorway of it a little way into the next (`FP_MOVE.doorReach`); a tap elsewhere is ignored with a small ✕ (`facilityTapAllowed`, `facilityTapRefused`); a portal's doorway lies in its own room; the game's own walks (`facilityWalkTo`: stations, launcher pads, bodies, the lash) are not limited. **The combat step** (a prototype, as the owner shaped it: "if there's no enemies nearby, there's no limit. But if they run into an enemy, they stop if they're on a run, and then they're limited to moving 5 spaces"): a tap with no enemy near is a free run; a free run that runs into one stops (`facilityRunCheck`, a "!"); with one near, a tap goes `FP_MOVE.capTiles` tiles at most (3 since the owner's playtest of 2026-11-01: five was "too long"). Near: within `FP_MOVE.near` tiles in sight, or `FP_MOVE.chase` if it is after you or the squad, or a hit on you within `FP_MOVE.hitWindow` frames (`facilityEnemyNear`, `facilityUnderPressure`). Settings → COMBAT STEP turns it off.
- **Movement:** a tap finds a path through the doors (A\* over tile centres, smoothed by line of sight); the player follows its waypoints; **every mover** (player, followers, clones, creatures) goes through `facilityConstrain`, so walls, glass, the desk, the truck and the vats hold; followers and clones steer round walls to the next waypoint (`facilitySteer`, hooked into `followPlayer`, `_walkStep` and `_moveToward`). A body keeps 0.4 tiles from a wall (`FP_BODY`; this read 0.3 until 2026-11-06, when a check against the code found 0.4).
- **The navigation layer (how the squad moves):** the AI still walks in straight lines toward what it wants; after each unit moves, `facilityReroute` works out what it was heading for (you, the crystal, its job's target, its enemy, a foe or friend near) and, if walls stand between, takes the same step along the planned route instead. Routes are planned with a wider margin (0.45, `FP_PLAN`) than the walls enforce (0.4, `FP_BODY`) so a unit never grazes a corner or a door post; a waypoint is passed only when the next one is in sight; a unit that is stuck for 40 frames replans and for 100 is moved onto the nearest open cell of its route. A follower is "near" you only if it can SEE you (a wall between means it keeps walking the route however close it measures), the EMPATHETIC lean is ignored where it would aim behind a wall, and a follower far from you hurries (up to ~4x) so the squad keeps up across the big map. A follower holding a room and a unit on an order of its own do not hurry. The details and the measurements are in docs/TUTORIAL.md, "NPC flow".
- **Objects that move (build `2026-11-06f`; docs/FLUX-MOVEMENT.md):** a prop with a `flux` field in `FACILITY_MAP.props` (the arena's loose crates, crate stack, barrels and barriers) can be moved in play by the Flux technician's FLUX MOVE (js/fluxmove.js). The move is one transaction, `facilitySolidMove` (beside `facilitySolidOpen`, which only opens a solid): the solid's outline in the map and its planning copy and both bucket indexes, the walkable cells and the floor tiles, the floor's paint for the changed cells (`facilityFloorRedo`), the prop's draw pieces, the stretches of floor (relabelled where they changed) and every route in flight. Moved objects are saved (`moved`).
- **What an object does besides blocking walking (build `2026-11-06h`; the rules in `COMBAT.md` §8):** each solid gets two traits from its kind when the map is built (js/facility.js `FAC_COVER`, `FAC_LOW`): **cover** (a shot that reaches it stops there: barriers, crates and crate stacks, barrels, the desks, consoles and lab benches) and **low** (sight passes over it: waist high or less, water, barred gates). Every solid still blocks walking for everyone. Sight is its own line test (`facilitySees`: walls and solids that are not low hide what is behind them); walking keeps its own (`facilityCanStep`, `_fpLos`). A wall is every edge of a room, glass included: it stops shots and sight; a doorway does not.
- **Drawing:** wall pieces (solid, rock, glass), sliding-door frames, prop cells (desk, semi-truck with its missiles, liquid vats, the lab tank) and the two wall fixtures are depth-sorted into the game's own draw list; a wall between the camera and the player turns see-through.
- **Start:** you and three followers start in the Crystal Chamber beside the Crystal, in a blackout (docs/TUTORIAL.md, phase 2).
- **Creatures:** every hostile creature is made by **`spawnCreature(opts)`** (js/clone.js: `species`, `className`, `x`, `y`, `level`, `state`), the one place a creature is made. The arena's spawner (js/facilityspawn.js `FP_SPAWN`; WHAT it makes is js/population.js `SPAWN_TABLES.arena`: ants, the Nest's, since 2026-11-01, up out of the tanks' overflow grates when one will do) puts four there at the start, and one more every 1200 frames (20 s) at a spot out of the player's sight, up to seven alive at once, always the kind there are fewest of (ants, beetles, spiders, scorpions, mantises and moths; scouts, workers, strikers and tanks by weight; levels 2 to 4). They wander the arena from spot to spot (`facilityWanderTarget`) and hunt when provoked or when an alarm is up (js/alarm.js). The tutorial's assault spawns its ants the same way (js/tutorial.js). A creature killed pays its worth in crystals, DNA and, in the arena, scrap (js/drops.js; docs/TUTORIAL.md, "The arena's creatures").

A draw above the reserve is paid even when it ends under it, so a fallen follower can take it from 19% to 9%. With CRYO GENETICIST worn (a Mutator's Player card, build `2026-11-02j`, `UNIVERSAL-AND-PLAYER-CARDS.md`), a follower's return costs nothing and never waits at the reserve.

When the player returns to the crystal, they may join the squad automatically"; then "Keep the 10% floor", and COME does not reach them: "walk back to the Crystal"). The mark is saved. The agent's, provisional: the cost by the energy cards in a straight line; "returns to the crystal" read as coming into the Crystal Chamber.

- **The network meter** (js/facilitynet.js): the dial's outer ring is the charge (a tick at the reserve, the percent in the middle); a tap on the panel (`facilityNetTap`, js/input.js) is `crystalBuyCharge`: `charge.price` ◆50 for `charge.amount` +75%, up to the full; refused when full or short, nothing paid.

Each is a **sleek laser sentry** (build `2026-11-09ww`; the owner: "make the sentries in the facility look less tough ... make it just look like a sleek laser sentry", and of the draft, "Use it"): a pale plinth and slender column, a smooth white pod of a head with a lit cyan visor band, and one slim emitter with a glowing lens (design/props/models/sentry.js; it was an armoured twin-cannon turret). Each has `SENTRY.hp` (500, provisional "sturdy"; measured with nobody defending and you away, fourteen raids, eight random and six built around tanks: the six without a tank were killed by the hallway turret alone, which lost 6 to 158; of the eight with a tank, six wrecked it after 35 to 45 s of biting and two left it at 55, and the Crystal Chamber's two finished what was left; the Crystal was not bitten in any); a raider's bite wears it down (js/predator.js `bite` on a `sentry` target, `sentryDamage`); a bar shows over a hurt one (`drawSentryWear`) and at 0 it is **wrecked** (`sentryBreak`): it stops, smokes and sparks, and a station of kind `sentry_repair` stands on it, a build of `SENTRY.repair` frames (ten seconds), free, that walking away stops; done, the turret is whole and the station gone (`sentryRepairDone`). ## Elevation (drawing only)

`levels` in the map give each level a height in tiles: the arena is sunken (-1.8), the ground is 0, the stair room and Teleport Room 2 are raised (1.6), and the observation deck and catwalks are higher still (3.2). A `ramp` may carry a `zone` (a rectangle, the axis it slopes along, and the two coordinates where the height equals `between_levels[0]` and `[1]`); `facilityHeightAt(x, y)` returns the room's height, or the slope inside a `zone`. A `zone` with `steps: N` is a little staircase: the height is quantised into N flat treads, and each tread tile draws a prism from the lower floor up to the tread (so the risers show). The arena door has 5 steps down, the deck door 5 steps up; the curved stair room staircase stays smooth. Every connection across levels names its ramp (validated), and the validator rejects a level with no height or a bad `zone`.

Movement stays flat. Only drawing is lifted: floors, actors and facility items rise by `height * 34 px`, the camera rides the player's floor, taps are corrected for the lift (`facilityPickLift`), and a wall is drawn up to the higher of the two floors it separates. All lift hooks are guarded by `facilityPlay`.

Known limits: effects, projectiles and health bars still use the flat projection (they sit a little off on a sloped floor).

## The dressing (floors, decor, furniture)

The rooms are filled out so the facility does not read as a hollow shell. Everything here is data in `js/facilitymap.js` (`floors`, `decor`, and more `props`), compiled and validated by `js/facility.js`, and drawn by `js/facilitydecor.js`. It is paint and light plus solid furniture: nothing else about movement changes.

- **`floors`**: each room's floor covering (carpet, plate, tile, labtile, concrete, grate, polished, wood, sand), painted over the game's floor tile by the tile's own draw (`facilityDrawFloorOn`), so a unit standing on a tile is always drawn over it. A tile's static marks are painted once into a small sprite and stamped; steady lights are baked in, and only flickering lights are painted live.
- **`decor`, on the floor**: `rug`, `hazard` stripes, `lane` lines, `arrows`, `cables`, `ring`, `stain` / `puddle` / `scorch`, `papers` / `debris`, `light` pools, `tint`. Floor decor is not snapped to the grid.
- **`decor`, on a wall**: `poster`, `vent`, `pipe`, `wall_light`, `screen`, `board`, `whiteboard`, `exit_sign`, `clock`, `graffiti`, `panel`, `banner`, `mirror`, `shelf`, `sign`, `hazard_sign`, `cable_run`, `window_blind`, and the rock / steel kinds `crack`, `vein`, `beam`, `chain`, `claw`, `ooze_drip`, `lamp`, `ladder`, `scoreboard`, `plate`, `moss`, `bulbs`. The camera sees a wall's +y / +x face, so a piece of wall decor names the room on THAT side (the lobby's south wall faces the hallway). The validator checks the side, that it is on a real solid wall (not glass or a gap), and that it is not across a door (an exit sign, pipe, banner, light or sign may be). Wall decor sorts just after the wall pieces under it (`depth`).
- **Furniture** (solid, in `props`): plant, vending, cooler, couch, table, pingpong, crate, crate_stack, barrel, locker, cabinet, bin, sink, stall, pallet, barrier, generator, console, display_case, telescope, floodlight, teleporter, beanbag, cart, desk, rail. Small pieces draw the floor under them (their tile is solid, so the game draws none).
- **Authoring.** Positions are sketch pixels like everything else; on the 20 px grid a tile cell k is [20k-10, 20k+10]. The dressing is authored on the snapped tile grid the game plays on, so the decor checks run on the snapped copy (`facilityValidateDressing`). A piece that stands in an aisle or a door's clear space fails the existing walkability and doorway tests, and a flood from the crystal must still reach nearly every floor tile of every room.
- **What is placeholder:** the products in the show room's display cases (a coloured glow), the wording on signs (a few bright bars), the poster art, and the lobby sign.

### The arena's walls and the catwalks

The arena's rock edge is diagonal on the tile grid, so decor on it is given on the wall line itself (not snapped); the validator takes the camera-facing side of any wall (towards larger x + y) and checks the room is there. The north rock faces the arena and carries moss, cracks, glowing veins, claw marks, chains, a timber support, caged lamps, string lights and the arena scoreboard. The south and east rock face away from the arena, but the camera sees that outer face in front of the arena, so it is dressed too: set `outside: true` and the validator checks the room is BEHIND the wall instead. The catwalks are scaffolding, not a room (see below): they carry painted lanes and arrows, light, a control booth (consoles, a chair), PA speakers, floodlights, cable drums and a generator on the deck, but no wall decor.

### Floor under the walls, and walls that do not fade

- **No holes.** Floor tiles are drawn only for walkable cells, so where a wall met the floor on a diagonal (the arena's rock) a black gap showed. `_fpFillCells` (js/facilitydecor.js) adds drawing-only FILL cells: any cell a room even partly covers gets that room's floor, cut to the room's outline (`clip`: past a rock edge the camera sees side-on nothing would hide it, and the Cold Storage's pale floor showed as white teeth in the void); and the pocket of void behind the arena's north rock (between the rock teeth and the hallway, up to 3 tiles) gets the arena's floor, only where a room lies behind it (since build `2026-11-09rr`: behind the west rock it floated in the void). The void beyond the south and east rock is NOT filled: it is in front of those walls. Fill cells are never walkable, are drawn as `fill` items sorted just before the walls (`depth`), and carry the room's floor marks. ## The website's background (js/attract.js)

## Steps with no picture (frame rate on a slow device)
`gameLoop` (js/game.js) keeps the game at 60 steps a second; a device that draws 15 frames a second owes four steps a refresh, and all but the last are run with **no picture** (`_simStep(false)`, `_simGhost`). They used to build and draw the whole facility (every floor tile, wall, door and prop) into a null context: ~7 ms each, three of them a frame, which lengthened the frame and so owed more of them. **A step with no picture now builds no tile list and no item list** (`const _fpGhost` in `render()`, an early return in `facilityDrawItems`, no cable or ground-pool layout). What stays, because logic lives there: the camera height (`_fpCam`, read by input and targeting), the doors' easing (`facilityDoorsTick`, moved out of `_fpStyleDoor` so it runs once a *step*, for every door), the room light (`_fpLightUpdate`) and the facility's tick (`facilityTutorialTick`). `tests/ghost.js` proves it: two copies of the facility run in lock step from one seed, one with a normal `render()` every step and one with `_simStep(false)` (and a 60 Hz against a 15 Hz `gameLoop`), in the dark, lit with a fight, and as the website's background, and every module-level variable is compared (only the draw-only caches are allow-listed, each checked for its readers).
The Settings PERFORMANCE readout splits a frame: logic, draw, catch-up and the browser's own time, the steps a frame, the canvas size, and whether the facility is LIT or DARK. It also shows how the page started (when the clock began, the time of each stage, the sheets) and the spread of the last 120 gaps between pictures: docs/STARTUP.md. The clock itself is started once, by `gameLoopStart` (js/game.js), after the start screen's work is done (js/init.js).
