What this is. A PlayStation emulator written from scratch: a full MIPS I interpreter with the R3000A's visible load delay slot, COP0 and exceptions, the memory map, DMA, and a GPU that rasterises into a real 1 MB VRAM and draws it to the canvas above. The PS1 is a far more tractable target than the N64 — no per-game graphics microcode, no 64-bit CPU — which is why this one can render rather than only single-step.
What works. PS-EXE homebrew loads and runs directly: flat and Gouraud polygons, rectangles, fills and CPU→VRAM uploads all draw. A BIOS image boots from the reset vector at 0xBFC00000 so you can step through it.
What is missing. The GTE (so 3D geometry does not transform), SPU, CD-ROM, timers and controllers. Those are counted rather than faked — the panel on the right lists exactly what a program tried to use, so you can see why something stalls. Commercial discs will not run.
Nothing is included. Load a BIOS dumped from your own console, or homebrew. For playing games, the Retro Player runs a complete PS1 core.
